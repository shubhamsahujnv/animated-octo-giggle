## LinkedIn

AI data centres are quietly turning into plumbing projects.

→ The most common server rack draws about 11 kW (Uptime Institute, 2026).
→ NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 packs 72 chips into one rack so they work as one giant chip, at about 120 kW.
→ Fans and chilled air top out at roughly 20 kW per rack.
→ Per litre, water holds ~3,500× more heat than air. That's why cold plates now sit directly on chips, and some servers are fully immersed in non-conductive fluid.
→ Liquid doesn't have to mean more water. In a closed loop nothing evaporates: Microsoft estimates its zero-evaporation design saves 125 million litres a year per site.
→ In India, operators such as Yotta already run their AI chips on liquid.

The caveat: if heat leaves through evaporative cooling towers, water use comes back. Cooling design, not just the coolant, decides the footprint.

When you assess an AI data centre, do you ask how it's cooled and where the heat goes?

Sources: Uptime Institute (2026); NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 (2024); Yotta Data Services (2026); Microsoft (Aug 2024, company estimate).
