# AI's Climate Bill: video script and storyboard

**Format:** Vertical video, 1080×1920, 30fps, 51 seconds (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, LinkedIn)
**Design:** *editorial-carousel-design* skill, The Green Solve brand: serif headlines with one italic emphasis, deep ink (#02403D) with a lime accent (#E2FF4D), one idea per scene, a source line on every number
**Motion and sound:** */brag* (brag-slim) rules: hook in the first 2 seconds, cuts on the beat, staggered transitions with no muddy crossfades, original soundtrack, settled hook frame as the thumbnail
**Tone:** Polished, evidence-led, hopeful (problem → evidence → solution)
**Music:** Original track, 96 BPM, A minor (Am–F–C–G). Each scene is 10 beats (6.25s), so every cut lands on a beat and gets a soft swell and chime.

The voiceover is optional. On-screen text carries the whole story, so the video works muted, which is how most feeds play it. To add narration, record the **VO** lines below; each fits its scene at a natural pace.

## 🎣 Scene 1: HOOK (0:00–0:06)

**Background:** Ink (dark)
**On screen:**
> AI & THE CLIMATE
> **America's AI boom could eat up *44% of its power-sector climate budget.***

**Motion:** The headline is on screen from frame 0. A ring sweeps to 44% while the number counts up. Then the caption appears: "of the US power sector's allowable emissions under its 2035 climate target, if data centres run on fossil fuels."
**VO:** "America's AI boom could eat up forty-four percent of its power-sector climate budget."
**Source on slide:** Center for Biological Diversity, *Data Crunch* (2025). Projection.

## Scene 2: THE SCALE (0:06–0:12)

**Background:** Paper (light)
**On screen:** **AI could nearly *triple* data centres' share of US power.**
**Motion:** Two columns grow: 2024 at ~4% (183 TWh), then 2030 at 9–12%, with a hatched band showing the range.
**VO:** "Data centres used about four percent of US power in 2024. By 2030: up to twelve."
**Source on slide:** Pew Research / IEA (2024 use); EPRI (2024) and LBNL (2024) 2030 projections.

## Scene 3: OLD COAL, NEW LIFE (0:12–0:19)

**Background:** Stone (surface)
**On screen:** **At least 15 US coal plants *didn't retire* in 2025.**
**Motion:** A grid of 15 plant icons, outlined as "due to close", fills in one by one to solid "kept running". Note: "**Why:** soaring power demand, including from AI data centres, plus federal emergency orders."
**VO:** "Last year, at least fifteen coal plants that were due to close kept running instead."
**Source on slide:** DeSmog analysis of US EIA retirement data (Dec 2025).

## Scene 4: LOCKED IN (0:19–0:25)

**Background:** Ink (dark)
**On screen:** **Gas already powers *over 40%* of US data centres.**
**Motion:** "40%+" counts up and a lime bar fills 40% of the track. Note: "**The risk:** new gas turbines built for AI can run for decades, locking in CO₂ and methane."
**VO:** "Gas already powers over forty percent of them, and new turbines lock that in for decades."
**Source on slide:** IEA, *Energy and AI* (2025).

## Scene 5: PLEDGES UNDER PRESSURE (0:25–0:31)

**Background:** Paper (light)
**On screen:** **Big Tech's emissions went *up, not down.*** Rise in operational emissions, 2020 → 2023.
**Motion:** Ranked bars grow in turn: Amazon +182%, Microsoft +155%, Meta +145%, Alphabet +138%. Note: "**The pattern:** the biggest buyers of clean energy are now leaning on fossil power to keep up with AI."
**VO:** "Big Tech's own operational emissions rose by up to 182 percent from 2020 to 2023."
**Source on slide:** ITU & World Benchmarking Alliance, *Greening Digital Companies* (2025).

## Scene 6: THIRSTY SERVERS (0:31–0:38)

**Background:** Stone (surface)
**On screen:** **Cooling AI could need *1.1 billion m³* of water a year.** "That's the household water use of up to 10 million Americans, every year, by 2030."
**Motion:** A water drop, then 10 person icons fill in one by one (each = 1 million Americans).
**VO:** "And cooling those servers could take 1.1 billion cubic metres of water a year."
**Source on slide:** Cornell University, *Nature Sustainability* (2025). Upper-end US projection.

## Scene 7: THE HOPEFUL PART (0:38–0:44)

**Background:** Ink (dark)
**On screen:** **On clean power, the same boom uses *just 4%.*** Share of the US 2035 power-sector emissions budget.
**Motion:** Two bars: fossil-powered AI at 44% (stone), then renewable-powered AI at 4% (lime). Note: "**Honest note:** both are projections, and demand forecasts vary widely. The choice of power is what moves the number."
**VO:** "The good news? Run on clean power, the same boom uses just four percent."
**Source on slide:** Center for Biological Diversity, *Data Crunch* (2025).

## Fact check: what changed from the Google AI Mode answer

Every figure was checked against its original source before it went on a slide.

| AI Mode claim | What the video uses | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "One AI query = up to 10× a web search" (opening hook of the first script) | **Dropped as the hook**; replaced with the 44% figure | The 10× figure comes from a 2024 EPRI estimate (2.9 Wh vs 0.3 Wh). Newer estimates (Epoch AI, OpenAI, Google) put a typical query near 0.24–0.34 Wh, so the ratio is disputed. |
| 44% of allowable power-sector emissions | Kept | Matches the Center for Biological Diversity *Data Crunch* report (Oct 2025). The same report gives 4% if renewables power the boom, which became the hopeful turn. |
| "15 coal plants kept open… (Lamont-Doherty)" | Kept, **re-attributed to DeSmog** | The count comes from a DeSmog analysis (Dec 2025). The delays were driven partly by data-centre demand and partly by federal emergency orders, so the slide says both. |
| "~40% from natural gas" | Kept, attributed to the **IEA** | IEA *Energy and AI* (2025) |
| "Emissions at 138%–182% **of 2020 levels**" | Corrected to a **rise of** 138%–182% (2020→2023) | ITU / World Benchmarking Alliance (2025). These are percentage increases, which makes them larger than the AI answer said. |
| "150% increase in structural carbon emissions" | Left out | It's the ITU average rise in operational emissions, not a construction figure, so it was mislabelled. |
| 1.1 billion m³ of water by 2030 | Kept, labelled "upper-end" | Cornell, *Nature Sustainability* (2025): projected range 731–1,125 million m³ a year |
| Diesel generators and air pollution | Left out of this cut | It's a valid point, but there was no single sourced number for it. It fits better in a follow-up video. |
