## LinkedIn

Data centres in orbit have moved from slide decks to launch pads. The economics haven't.

→ On 1 October 2026, Google and Planet launched a Project Suncatcher prototype carrying 4 AI chips (TPUs) to test radiation and extreme temperatures.
→ The appeal: in a dawn-to-dusk orbit, Google says solar panels can produce up to 8× more power than on Earth, with no land or local water needed.
→ Starcloud has already trained a small AI model on an Nvidia chip in orbit.
→ SpaceX has filed to launch up to 1,000,000 computing satellites. About 16,800 working satellites exist today.
→ The catch is cooling. In a vacuum, heat can only be radiated away; one World Economic Forum estimate puts radiators at ~100 tonnes per MW. Radiation and no repairs add to the challenge.
→ Google's own outlook: it might add up by the mid-2030s, if launch costs keep falling.

For this decade, the clean power AI needs still has to be built on the ground.

Is orbital computing worth the research spend, or a distraction from fixing grids on Earth?

Sources: Google Research, Project Suncatcher (2025–26); Starcloud (Nov 2025); SpaceX FCC filing (Jan 2026) via DCD; KeepTrack (Oct 2026); World Economic Forum (June 2026).
