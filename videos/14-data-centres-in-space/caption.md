## LinkedIn

Data centres in orbit have moved from slide decks to launch pads. The economics are still unproven.

→ On 1 October 2026, Google and Planet launched a Project Suncatcher prototype carrying AI chips (TPUs) to test radiation, launch stress and extreme temperatures in orbit.
→ Starcloud put an Nvidia GPU in orbit in November 2025 and ran a small AI model on it.
→ SpaceX has filed with the US FCC for up to 1 million computing satellites. It is a filing, not an approval.
→ The appeal: near-constant sunlight in the right orbit, no land acquisition, no local water.
→ The catch is cooling. In a vacuum, heat can only be radiated away. The World Economic Forum estimates ~100 tonnes of ISS-style radiators for 1 MW of computing; other analyses expect much lighter designs. One cost model finds orbit only competes if launch costs fall much further than today.

Rocket emissions, crowded orbits and brighter night skies are costs too. For this decade, the clean power AI needs still has to be built on the ground.

Is orbital computing worth the research spend, or a distraction from fixing grids on Earth?

Sources: Google (Oct 2026); CNN (1 Oct 2026); Starcloud (Nov 2025); DCD (SpaceX FCC filing, Jan 2026); World Economic Forum (June 2026); Forethought (2026, launch-cost model).
