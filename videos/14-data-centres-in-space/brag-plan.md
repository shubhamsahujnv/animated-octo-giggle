# brag plan: Data centres in space, genius or fantasy? (Script 14)

Built with **The Green Solve guardrails** skill (`thegreensolve-guardrails`): brag video workflow, held to the TGS scale, palette, voice and claims rules. Facts as of 31 October 2026.

## Brief

- **Series:** Data centres, AI and tech, Episode 14
- **One idea:** Tech companies are testing AI chips in orbit for near-constant sunlight. The tests are real, but cooling in a vacuum is the catch, so it's an experiment, not an escape from clean power on Earth.
- **Arc:** hook (it's happening) → evidence (who's up there) → why they're trying → the catch (cooling) → close.
- **Audience:** sustainability managers, auditors, consultants and informed professionals in India.

## Settings

- Preset: polished
- Format: vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps (Reels, Shorts, Stories). Landscape 1920×1080 re-layout allowed for YouTube/LinkedIn.
- Length: 23 s (TGS window 15–25 s)
- Transitions: soft crossfade `--t-xfade` 700 ms, dipping through ink/paper between busy layouts
- Audio: original synthesised music bed at 0.28 (calm, spacious); 3 subtle SFX at 0.6; music ducks to 0.13 under the voiceover
- Voiceover: optional (use only with `--voice`). Indian-English female voice. It fits inside the 23 s and never extends it.

## Visual identity

- Background: `#02403D` (ink) / `#FFFDF8` (paper) / `#D8CAC3` (surface)
- Text: `#FFFDF8` on ink, `#02403D` on paper and surface
- Accent: `#E2FF4D`, one thing per scene, never as text on paper or surface
- Display font: Libre Baskerville. Body font: Helvetica Neue.
- Logo: large `logo-light.png` (150 px) on the close; small logo (54 px) in the footer of scenes 2 and 4.
- Rhythm: ink → paper → surface → paper → ink.
- Earth, orbit, satellite and radiator panels are original flat line illustrations. No space photos, rocket footage, star fields, glows or company logos.

## Hook options

| Option | Line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **A (chosen)** | "In October 2026, Google sent *AI chips* into orbit." | A specific, verifiable event that makes people stop and ask "why?" |
| B | "The next data centre might not be on Earth." | Curious, but closer to a topic label; no fact in it |

### Scene 1 – Hook – 4.5 s

- **Background:** ink
- **Archetype:** Hook cover
- **On screen:**
  - Kicker: DATA CENTRES IN SPACE?
  - Headline (h1, 96 px): In October 2026, Google sent *AI chips* into orbit.
  - Small line illustration: one satellite on a thin orbit arc
  - Source line: Source: Google, Project Suncatcher prototype launch with Planet (1 Oct 2026).
- **Accent:** the italic phrase "AI chips"
- **Motion:** headline rises 44 px over 450 ms; italic phrase lands last; satellite drifts ≤ 44 px along the arc and stops; settled ≥ 3.3 s (hook floor 1.8 s; 9 words × 0.3 s = 2.7 s)
- **SFX:** soft airy tone as the satellite settles (1 of 3)
- **Voiceover:** "In October 2026, Google sent AI chips into orbit."

### Scene 2 – The race – 6 s

- **Background:** paper
- **Archetype:** Timeline (2 events, revealed in order)
- **On screen:**
  - Label (kicker): IT'S NOT ALONE
  - Event 1: **Nov 2025** · Starcloud runs an Nvidia GPU in orbit
  - Event 2: **Jan 2026** · SpaceX asks to launch up to 1 million computing satellites
  - Source line: Source: Starcloud (Nov 2025); SpaceX filing to the US FCC (Jan 2026), via DCD.
- **Accent:** none on paper (ink dots on the timeline)
- **Motion:** label (0.8 s floor), then event 1 rises (7 words, 2.1 s floor), then event 2 rises (10 words, 3.0 s floor) with a 150 ms stagger; set holds to the end
- **Footer:** small logo
- **SFX:** none
- **Voiceover:** "A startup ran an Nvidia chip up there in 2025. SpaceX wants up to a million satellites."

### Scene 3 – Why orbit – 4.5 s

- **Background:** surface
- **Archetype:** Checklist
- **On screen:**
  - Headline (h2, 72 px): Why put AI in *orbit*?
  - Ticks (one per beat):
    - Near-constant sunlight
    - No land to fight over
    - No local water used
  - Source line: Source: Google Research, Project Suncatcher (2025).
- **Accent:** none (surface scene; ink text and ink ticks)
- **Motion:** headline in (1.5 s floor), then ticks appear every 0.6 s; set holds ≥ 1.5 s
- **SFX:** none (ticks are silent so the scene stays calm)
- **Voiceover:** "Why? Near-constant sunshine. No land fights. No water."

### Scene 4 – The catch – 5 s

- **Background:** paper
- **Archetype:** Big stat with a radiator illustration
- **On screen:**
  - Headline (h2): The catch: no air to *cool* the chips.
  - Stat: ~100 tonnes
  - Label: of space-station-style radiators for just 1 MW of computing (estimate). Lighter designs are being tested.
  - Illustration: a small chip box beside two very large flat radiator wings (drawn to rough scale)
  - Source line: Source: World Economic Forum (June 2026), estimate for ISS-style radiators.
- **Accent:** none on paper; the stat is ink
- **Motion:** radiator wings unfold once (no pulsing); stat counts 0 → 100 over 1.0 s; holds 2.5 s with its source line
- **Footer:** small logo
- **SFX:** low soft thud as the wings lock open (2 of 3)
- **Voiceover:** "The catch: there's no air. Heat has to radiate away, through very heavy panels."

### Scene 5 – Close – 3 s

- **Background:** ink
- **Archetype:** CTA close
- **On screen:**
  - Line (h2): An experiment, not an *escape hatch*.
  - Large logo (150 px), held ≥ 1.5 s
  - Pill (accent on ink): Follow The Green Solve
  - Next-in-series: Next: AI's factories and shop counters
- **Accent:** the pill (the italic phrase stays paper so accent marks one thing)
- **Motion:** line rises; logo fades in at 0.6 s and holds to the end
- **SFX:** soft resolve chord under the logo (3 of 3)
- **Voiceover:** "An experiment, not an escape hatch."

Left out on purpose:
- The ~$100/kg launch-cost threshold (one model's result; a second idea that would crowd scene 4). The LinkedIn caption mentions launch cost without the number, because TGS captions may not carry numbers that aren't on screen.
- Starcloud's valuation and funding, and the SpaceX–xAI merger (not needed for the one idea).
- Rocket emissions, debris and night-sky impacts: real concerns, but no single number to show; mentioned in the LinkedIn caption.

Nothing in this piece covers offsets or credit markets, so the two TGS disclosures are not needed.
