Top 10 Decarbonisation Strategies Manufacturers Can Use to Cut Emissions in 2026

Motors use 72% of industrial electricity and leaks waste up to 30% of compressed air. The top 10 decarbonisation strategies for manufacturers in 2026.

Quick answer: The top decarbonisation strategies for manufacturers in 2026 start with efficiency: electric motor systems use 72% of industrial electricity [1], and compressed-air leaks can waste 20 to 30% of compressor output [2]. After that come electrifying low-temperature heat, switching to clean power, controlling refrigerants, using less material, engaging suppliers and screening projects for carbon credits.

Decarbonisation strategies are the technical, operational and commercial changes a manufacturer makes to cut greenhouse gas emissions across Scope 1, 2 and 3. Many of them also cut energy bills. In 2026 the business case strengthened further: India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme set binding intensity targets for several heavy industries [3], and the EU's CBAM began charging for embedded emissions in imports [4].

Here are the top 10 strategies, a table of decarbonisation levers, and a simple roadmap.

What is decarbonisation in manufacturing?

Decarbonisation in manufacturing means reducing the greenhouse gases a factory and its value chain emit per unit of output, and eventually in absolute terms. It covers fuel burned on site (Scope 1), purchased power (Scope 2) and materials and logistics (Scope 3), where CDP found supply-chain emissions average 26 times operational ones [5].

Most programmes move through 3 stages:

  • Efficiency: use less energy for the same output
  • Substitution: replace fossil fuels and high-GWP gases with lower-carbon options
  • Value chain: redesign products and work with suppliers on materials

The order matters. Efficiency first shrinks the load, so every later investment in clean heat or power can be smaller.

What are the top 10 decarbonisation strategies for manufacturers?

The 10 strategies run roughly from quick, low-cost wins (1 to 4) to larger investments (5 to 7) and value-chain work (8 to 10). Most plants can start 3 or 4 of them in the same year.

1. Measure a baseline before you spend

You can't prioritise what you haven't measured. A Scope 1 to 3 baseline shows which sites, processes and suppliers drive emissions, so capital goes where tonnes are largest. It also becomes the yardstick for targets and any carbon credit claims.

2. Fix compressed-air leaks

Leaks can waste 20 to 30% of a compressor's output in poorly maintained systems, while well-maintained systems keep losses under 10% [2]. Ultrasonic leak surveys, tagging and a monthly repair routine are among the cheapest projects a plant can run.

3. Fit variable speed drives to motors

Electric motor systems account for 72% of industrial electricity use, according to an IEA 4E policy brief [1]. Pumps, fans and compressors running at full speed against throttles waste much of that. Variable speed drives match motor speed to load, with typical savings of about 25% on suitable variable-load applications (industry estimate).

4. Recover waste heat

Furnaces, compressors, ovens and exhaust stacks release heat that can be captured with heat exchangers. A waste heat recovery system can preheat boiler feedwater, combustion air or process water, cutting the fuel burned in Scope 1. Start with the hottest, steadiest streams.

5. Electrify low-temperature process heat with industrial heat pumps

About 75% of heat demand in less energy-intensive industries is below 200 °C, the range today's industrial heat pumps can serve [6]. The IEA expects electricity's share of industrial process heat to rise from 4% in 2024 to 12% in 2030 [7]. Food, paper, textiles and chemicals are the best early candidates.

6. Switch to renewable electricity

Rooftop solar, power purchase agreements and green tariffs cut Scope 2 emissions. Remember that renewable contracts reduce the market-based Scope 2 figure; the location-based figure still follows the grid, and the GHG Protocol requires companies with such instruments to report both [8].

7. Control refrigerant leaks and choose lower-GWP options

Refrigerants punch above their weight. HFC-134a has a 100-year GWP of 1,530 and HFC-32 has one of 771 under IPCC AR6 [9]. Regular leak checks cut Scope 1 now; choosing lower-GWP refrigerants at equipment replacement cuts the risk for the machine's whole service life.

8. Use less material, and lower-carbon material

For most manufacturers, purchased goods are the largest Scope 3 category. Design products with less material, raise recycled content, cut scrap and rework, and specify lower-carbon grades of steel, aluminium, cement or plastics where they meet performance needs.

9. Engage your largest suppliers

Only 15% of companies disclosing to CDP have set upstream Scope 3 targets [5]. Rank suppliers by the emissions they bring you, ask the top ones for product-level data, and add carbon criteria to procurement, using a Scope 3 sequence for manufacturers without a data team. A small number of suppliers usually drives most of the total.

10. Screen projects for carbon credit pathways

Some efficiency, fuel-switching and renewable projects can earn credits, and credit-eligible projects are not just for big companies. Chemical sites can also look at the new UN Article 6.4 methodology for N₂O abatement. Under India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, obligated entities that beat their emission-intensity targets earn tradable certificates [3]. Screen projects early for additionality, baseline and monitoring requirements, and remember credits never replace reductions in your own inventory.

What are examples of decarbonisation levers?

Decarbonisation levers are the specific actions that reduce emissions, and each one acts on a particular scope. The table maps 8 common levers to the scope they affect and a practical first step.

Lever Scope affected Practical first step
Compressed-air leak repair Scope 2 Ultrasonic leak survey
Variable speed drives Scope 2 List motors over a set size running at constant speed
Waste heat recovery Scope 1 Map hot exhaust and cooling-water streams
Industrial heat pumps Scope 1 to Scope 2 Find process heat demand below 200 °C
Renewable electricity Scope 2 (market-based) Rooftop solar assessment or PPA tender
Refrigerant management Scope 1 Log top-ups per unit every quarter
Material efficiency Scope 3, category 1 Scrap and yield analysis by product line
Supplier engagement Scope 3, category 1 Rank suppliers by emissions contribution

Note the heat-pump row: electrifying heat moves emissions from Scope 1 to Scope 2. It cuts the total only if the electricity is cleaner than the fuel it replaces, which is why the clean-power lever matters.

How can India's carbon market reward factory decarbonisation?

India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme rewards factories that beat government-set emission-intensity targets with tradable carbon credit certificates. Targets cover compliance years 2025-26 and 2026-27 against an FY 2023-24 baseline, and the first round alone covered 282 plants in aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali and pulp and paper [3].

A second round, notified in January 2026, added refinery, petrochemicals and textiles plants [3]. Entities that miss their targets must buy and surrender certificates, so every tonne saved has a market value on both sides of the ledger.

For exporters, the EU CBAM adds a second price signal. Its definitive regime began on 1 January 2026, and EU importers start buying certificates on 1 February 2027 [4]. Lower embedded emissions in steel, aluminium or cement shipments mean lower costs for your EU buyers. Plants in both markets should also track how Indian and US reporting rules differ.

How do you build an industrial decarbonisation roadmap?

Build an industrial decarbonisation roadmap in 4 steps: measure, prioritise, act and track. Prioritising by cost per tonne and payback lets quick wins fund the larger projects that come later.

  1. Measure. Build a Scope 1 to 3 baseline and a site-by-site energy map.
  2. Prioritise. Estimate tonnes saved, cost and payback for each lever, and rank them.
  3. Act. Start with low-cost efficiency, then schedule heat electrification and clean power around equipment replacement cycles.
  4. Track. Compare monthly emissions with the plan and report progress openly.

Tie each step to a named owner and a budget line. A roadmap without owners is a wish list.

What happens after you start decarbonising?

After the first projects land, 3 things matter: proving the savings, reporting them correctly and reinvesting. Measured savings against your baseline become evidence for customers, lenders and, where eligible, carbon credit programmes.

Practical follow-through:

  • Verify savings with metered data, not engineering estimates alone.
  • Report gross emissions and any credits separately.
  • Update the roadmap every year as costs and technology change.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Can industrial heat pumps supply heat above 200 °C?+

Not at commercial scale yet. Only a few early-stage prototypes exist for temperatures beyond 200 °C, and none are ready for the mass market [12]. For higher-temperature processes, manufacturers usually look at efficiency, heat recovery, electric boilers or furnaces, and lower-carbon fuels, while keeping heat pumps for the sub-200 °C share.

Who pays the EU CBAM, the exporter or the importer?+

The EU importer pays, by buying CBAM certificates, but the cost flows back to exporters through pricing and supplier choice. From 1 February 2027, importers can buy certificates covering 2026 imports [4]. Exporters that can document low embedded emissions per tonne give their buyers a direct cost advantage.

Does buying renewable energy certificates count as decarbonisation?+

Certificates lower your market-based Scope 2 figure, but they don't change the grid's emissions or your location-based figure [8]. Direct investments such as rooftop solar or long-term power purchase agreements add new clean supply. Most credible programmes use certificates as a bridge, not the whole strategy.

Is electrification always lower carbon?+

No, it depends on the grid. Electrifying heat moves emissions from Scope 1 fuel to Scope 2 power, so the result depends on the electricity's emission factor. The US grid averaged 823.1 lb of CO₂ per MWh in 2022 [10], and pairing electrification with clean power secures the reduction.

Do carbon credits reduce a factory's reported emissions?+

No. Credits sit outside all 3 scopes: your inventory reports gross emissions from your own operations and value chain, and credits are reported separately. Under India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, certificates reflect performance against an intensity target [3]. They are a financial reward, not a deduction from the tonnes you actually emitted.

What do EU greenwashing rules mean for manufacturers' climate claims?+

Since 27 September 2026, EU Directive 2024/825 bans product claims of neutral, reduced or positive climate impact that rely on offsetting [11]. Claims based on actual life-cycle reductions within the product's value chain are still allowed. Manufacturers selling into the EU should base product claims on measured reductions.
