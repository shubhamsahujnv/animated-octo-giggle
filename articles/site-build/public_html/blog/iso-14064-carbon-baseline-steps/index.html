Top 7 Steps to Build an ISO 14064-1 Carbon Baseline for Manufacturers in 2026

A carbon baseline is your base-year emissions inventory across ISO 14064-1's 6 categories. Here are the top 7 steps manufacturers use to build one in 2026.

Quick answer: A carbon baseline is a documented greenhouse gas inventory for one base year that all future reductions are measured against. Manufacturers build an ISO 14064-1 baseline in 7 steps: set boundaries, list sources across the standard's 6 categories, choose a base year, collect activity data, apply emission factors and AR6 GWPs, assess uncertainty, and document everything so a verifier can check it.

A carbon baseline is the reference inventory a company uses to prove that its emissions went down. ISO 14064-1:2018 is the international standard for organisation-level greenhouse gas inventories, and it sits alongside the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard. For manufacturers in 2026, a solid baseline is also the entry ticket to India's carbon market, to customer reporting and to science-based targets.

This guide covers what a baseline is, how ISO 14064-1 compares with the GHG Protocol, and the top 7 steps to build one.

What does carbon baseline mean?

A carbon baseline means your organisation's total greenhouse gas emissions in a chosen base year, calculated with documented boundaries, methods and emission factors. India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, for example, measures obligated plants against an FY 2023-24 baseline [1].

A baseline has three parts:

  • A base year: the 12-month period you compare against
  • An inventory: emissions by source, category and gas, in tonnes of CO₂e
  • A method record: boundaries, factors, assumptions and exclusions

Without the method record, a baseline is just a number. With it, the baseline becomes evidence you can defend to customers, verifiers and regulators.

ISO 14064-1 vs GHG Protocol: what's the difference?

ISO 14064-1:2018 and the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard measure the same emissions but group them differently: the GHG Protocol uses 3 scopes, while ISO 14064-1 uses 6 categories. An inventory built to the GHG Protocol can conform to ISO 14064-1, provided significant indirect emissions have been considered.

GHG Protocol Corporate Standard ISO 14064-1:2018
Structure Scope 1, 2 and 3 (15 Scope 3 categories) 6 categories
Indirect emissions Scope 3 encouraged; company-defined boundary Significant indirect emissions must be assessed and reported
Form Accounting framework and guidance International standard with formal requirements
Verification Assurance by choice or by programme rules Designed to pair with ISO 14064-3 verification

The ISO 14064 series has 3 parts: part 1 covers organisation-level inventories, part 2 covers project-level reductions, and part 3 covers validation and verification of GHG statements [2]. Most manufacturers design one inventory that satisfies both frameworks, which also simplifies reporting in India and the US.

What are the 7 steps to build an ISO 14064-1 carbon baseline?

The 7 steps are boundaries, sources, base year, activity data, factors, uncertainty and documentation. Done in order, they turn scattered plant records into a verifiable base-year inventory in weeks rather than months.

Step 1: Define your organisational boundary

Choose a consolidation approach and apply it everywhere. Under the control approach you include 100% of emissions from operations you control; under the equity share approach you include emissions in proportion to ownership. Most manufacturers choose operational control because plant managers hold the data.

Step 2: List emission sources by ISO 14064-1 category

Walk every site and list each source in 1 of the 6 categories: direct emissions, imported energy, transportation, products used by the organisation, use of the organisation's products, and other. Plant walk-throughs regularly find sources that never reached the finance system, such as standby generators or process gases.

Step 3: Choose a representative base year

Pick a recent 12-month period with reliable data and normal production. Avoid years distorted by long shutdowns or one-off spikes. Then write a recalculation policy that says when you'll restate the base year, for example after an acquisition or divestment.

Step 4: Collect activity data for every source

Gather fuel invoices, meter readings, utility bills, refrigerant top-up logs, production volumes, procurement quantities, freight records and waste manifests. Assign 1 owner per data source and a monthly upload date, so next year's update is routine instead of a scramble.

Step 5: Apply emission factors and AR6 GWPs

Convert activity data into CO₂e using factors that match each country, year and fuel. The GHG Protocol recommends the latest IPCC values, AR6, for global warming potentials [3]. Record the source and version of every factor in the inventory itself, not in a separate note.

Step 6: Assess data quality and uncertainty

Rate every major source as measured, calculated from records or estimated. Metered fuel is strong; spend-based Scope 3 estimates are weak. The weakest large sources become next year's data-improvement plan; a five-tier rating for every Scope 3 line works well.

Step 7: Document everything and prepare for verification

Write a GHG inventory report covering boundaries, base year, methods, factors, assumptions, exclusions and the recalculation policy. If you plan public claims, targets or credits, have it verified against ISO 14064-3 [2]. A clean evidence trail makes verification faster and cheaper.

What are the ISO 14064-1 categories?

ISO 14064-1:2018 sorts emissions into 6 categories: category 1 is direct emissions, category 2 is indirect emissions from imported energy, and categories 3 to 6 are other indirect emissions. Companies must define a process to decide which indirect categories are significant enough to report.

ISO 14064-1 category What it covers Closest GHG Protocol equivalent
1. Direct emissions and removals Fuel combustion, process emissions, fugitive leaks Scope 1
2. Indirect emissions from imported energy Purchased electricity, steam, heat, cooling Scope 2
3. Indirect emissions from transportation Freight, business travel, commuting Scope 3 transport categories (freight, travel, commuting)
4. Indirect emissions from products used Purchased goods, capital goods, waste disposal Mainly Scope 3 upstream categories
5. Indirect emissions from use of products Use and end-of-life of sold products Mainly Scope 3 downstream categories
6. Other indirect emissions Anything not covered above Any remaining sources

The mapping is approximate, not one-to-one. Keep a crosswalk table in your inventory report so readers of either framework can follow your numbers.

How do you choose and recalculate base year emissions?

Choose base year emissions from a recent, representative year, then recalculate them when significant structural or method changes occur. The SBTi's Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0 says companies shall recalculate their target base-year inventory in response to significant changes [4].

Common triggers for recalculation:

  • Acquisitions and divestments that move emissions in or out of your boundary
  • Methodology changes, such as switching from spend-based to supplier-specific data
  • Discovered errors that materially change the total

How big is "significant"? The GHG Protocol is revising its Corporate Standard, and a working group's indicative poll showed majority support for a 5% significance threshold if one is set; that threshold is not yet adopted [5]. Until it is, define your own threshold, write it into your policy and apply it consistently.

How do you calculate a carbon footprint for manufacturing?

You calculate a manufacturing carbon footprint by multiplying each activity by its emission factor and adding the results by category. A plant using 2,000 MWh of US grid power emits about 747 tonnes of CO₂ location-based, at eGRID's 2022 average of 823.1 lb per MWh [6].

Here is a simplified example for one US plant:

Source Activity Emission factor Emissions (t CO₂e)
Natural gas boilers 20,000 MMBtu 53.06 kg CO₂/MMBtu [7] 1,061
Grid electricity 2,000 MWh 823.1 lb CO₂/MWh [6] 747
Refrigerant leak (HFC-32) 20 kg GWP 771 [3] 15
Total (partial) 1,823

This covers only Scope 1 and 2 and CO₂ from combustion. A full baseline adds methane and nitrous oxide from combustion, process emissions, and the significant indirect categories, which for most manufacturers include purchased materials.

What happens after your carbon baseline is built?

After the baseline is built, it becomes the yardstick for 3 things: reduction targets, regulatory reporting and any carbon credit claims. In India, plants covered by the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme that beat their intensity targets earn tradable certificates, and those that miss must buy and surrender them [1].

Typical next steps:

  1. Set targets grounded in the baseline, not in guesses.
  2. Rank reduction projects by tonnes saved and cost.
  3. Screen projects for credit pathways where energy efficiency or fuel switching may qualify; carbon credits are not just for big companies.
  4. Track monthly so drift from the plan shows early.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

What is the latest version of ISO 14064-1?+

The current edition is ISO 14064-1:2018. It replaced the 2006 edition and introduced the 6-category structure for direct and indirect emissions. Inventories built under the older edition can usually be carried forward, but the categories need remapping and any newly significant indirect sources must be assessed.

What is the difference between ISO 14064-1, 14064-2 and 14064-3?+

They cover 3 different jobs. ISO 14064-1 quantifies an organisation's inventory, ISO 14064-2 quantifies emission reductions or removals from a specific project, and ISO 14064-3 sets out how those statements are verified or validated [2]. A manufacturer seeking carbon credits for a project may need all 3.

Does ISO 14064-1 require Scope 3 emissions?+

Not all of them. ISO 14064-1 requires you to define a process for deciding which indirect emissions are significant, then quantify and report those. Categories 3 to 6 cover indirect sources, so purchased materials or product use often qualify. Excluded categories need a documented justification.

Is India's CCTS baseline the same as an ISO 14064-1 baseline?+

No. India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme sets targets as GHG emission intensity, meaning tonnes of CO₂e per unit of output, measured against FY 2023-24 [1]. An ISO 14064-1 baseline is usually an absolute total for the whole organisation. Many plants need both views, so keep production data alongside emissions.

Which GWP values should a 2026 carbon baseline use?+

Use IPCC AR6 values unless a programme requires otherwise; the GHG Protocol recommends the latest set [3]. The change matters: HFC-134a is 1,530 under AR6 but 1,300 under AR5. Some programmes still use AR5, so check before you submit, and record which set you used.

Can I reuse a GHG Protocol inventory for ISO 14064-1?+

Usually yes. A GHG Protocol inventory can conform to ISO 14064-1 if significant indirect emissions have been considered. In practice you remap the 3 scopes into 6 categories, document how you judged significance, and add any indirect sources your Scope 3 screening had skipped.
