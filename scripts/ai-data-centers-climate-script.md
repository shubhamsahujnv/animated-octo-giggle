# The Hidden Climate Cost of AI: How Data Centers Threaten US Climate Progress

## 0:00 – 0:25 | HOOK

**VISUAL:** Close-up of a phone typing a question into an AI chatbot. Cut to a vast, humming data center at night, then to smokestacks from a coal plant.

**NARRATOR:**
Every time you ask an AI a question, somewhere a server wakes up, and it's hungry.
A single generative AI query can use up to **10 times** the electricity of a normal web search.
Multiply that by billions of queries a day, and you get one of the biggest new threats to America's climate goals.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** "1 AI query ≈ up to 10× a web search"

## 0:25 – 0:50 | THE BIG PICTURE

**VISUAL:** Split screen. Left: wind turbines and solar panels. Right: rows of glowing server racks multiplying faster.

**NARRATOR:**
For years, the tech industry promised a clean-energy future. But training and running AI models takes so much computing power that demand is now **outpacing the rollout of clean energy**.
The result? AI is driving a surge in electricity use that keeps old fossil-fuel plants running, strains the power grid, and is undoing the climate pledges of the world's biggest tech companies.
Let's break it down into four parts.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** 1. Grid Strain · 2. Coal & Gas · 3. Broken Pledges · 4. Water & Air

## 0:50 – 1:50 | PART 1: PROJECTIONS AND GRID STRAIN

**VISUAL:** Animated bar chart of US electricity use. A "data centers" slice grows from 4% to 9–12%.

**NARRATOR:**
First, the sheer scale.
In 2024, US data centers used about **183 terawatt-hours** of electricity. That's roughly **4%** of all the power the country consumes.
Because of AI, that share is projected to rise to **9 to 12% by 2030**. In about six years, it could triple.

**VISUAL:** Map of the US with the 2035 climate target shown as a "carbon budget" bar. A large chunk labeled "Data centers" fills it.

**NARRATOR:**
And that has consequences for climate targets. According to the **Center for Biological Diversity**, if these facilities keep relying on fossil fuels, they could account for **44% of all the power-sector emissions** the US can afford under its 2035 climate commitments.
Nearly half the budget, for a single industry.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** "Up to 44% of allowable power-sector emissions (2035 targets)"

---

## 1:50 – 2:45 | PART 2: KEEPING COAL AND GAS PLANTS ALIVE

**VISUAL:** Time-lapse of a solar farm going dark at sunset while a data center stays lit.

**NARRATOR:**
Here's the core problem. AI never sleeps. It needs **always-on, 24/7 power**, known as "base load."
Wind and solar are clean, but they're intermittent. The sun sets and the wind drops. So utilities are turning back to fossil fuels.

**VISUAL:** A coal plant with a "CLOSING" sign. The sign gets crossed out and replaced with "STILL OPEN."

**NARRATOR:**
According to the **Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory**, in 2025 alone, **15 US coal plants** that were scheduled to retire were kept running, specifically to power nearby data centers.

**VISUAL:** Pie chart showing natural gas at ~40% of data center electricity. New gas turbines being installed.

**NARRATOR:**
Natural gas already supplies roughly **40%** of the electricity used by US data centers. Now there's a rush to build new gas turbines for the AI boom.
Those turbines are built to run for decades, so the carbon and methane emissions get locked in for decades too.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** "15 coal plants kept open in 2025 · ~40% of data center power from gas"

---

## 2:45 – 3:40 | PART 3: THE REVERSAL OF CORPORATE CLEAN-ENERGY GOALS

**VISUAL:** Logos-free montage of corporate sustainability reports and "100% Renewable" headlines, then a line graph bending sharply upward.

**NARRATOR:**
Google, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon have historically been the **largest corporate buyers of renewable energy** in the world.
But the AI race has knocked those plans off course.
Leaning on fossil fuels to back up AI has led some tech giants to break their **100% renewable** pledges. Their operational emissions have climbed to **138 to 182% of 2020 levels**.

**VISUAL:** Construction cranes, concrete pours, a mine extracting rare earth metals.

**NARRATOR:**
And that's just from running the facilities. **Building** them has its own footprint. The concrete, the infrastructure and the heavy hardware have caused a reported **150% increase in structural, or "embodied," carbon emissions**.
On top of that, there's environmental damage from concrete production and from mining rare earth metals.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** "Operational emissions: 138–182% of 2020 · Embodied emissions: +150%"

---

## 3:40 – 4:35 | PART 4: CO-POLLUTANTS AND WATER SCARCITY

**VISUAL:** Steam rising from cooling towers. Then a drought-cracked riverbed.

**NARRATOR:**
The damage isn't limited to carbon dioxide. Data centers also create very **local** environmental crises.
AI servers run extremely hot, and cooling them takes enormous amounts of fresh water. US data centers are projected to use up to **1.1 billion cubic meters of water a year by 2030**.
They need clean freshwater to avoid corroding equipment, so they're competing with local communities for it, often in dry, water-scarce regions.

**VISUAL:** Rows of diesel generators. A haze over a nearby neighborhood. A child using an inhaler.

**NARRATOR:**
Then there's the air. To stay online during power outages, data centers rely on huge **diesel backup generators**. These release fine particulate matter and nitrogen oxides, which worsen air quality and raise health risks like **asthma** for the people living nearby.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** "1.1 billion m³ of water per year by 2030"

## 4:35 – 5:10 | CONCLUSION AND CALL TO ACTION

**VISUAL:** Slow pull-back from a data center to a wide view of the planet.

**NARRATOR:**
AI is changing the world, but it's also changing the climate math.
More demand. Old coal plants kept alive. New gas plants locked in. Broken pledges. Thirsty servers and polluted air.
The question isn't whether we use AI. It's **how we power it**. Will the AI boom be built on clean energy, or will it set US climate progress back by a decade?
