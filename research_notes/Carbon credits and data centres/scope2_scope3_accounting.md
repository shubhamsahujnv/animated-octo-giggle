## Q1. How big is the gap between market-based and location-based emissions for Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta? (including the Guardian "662%" analysis)

### Takeaway
Market-based Scope 2 is consistently a small fraction of location-based Scope 2 for the hyperscalers, often by an order of magnitude or more. In the most recent reports (published mid-2026, covering 2025 or FY25), absolute emissions are rising sharply under both methods. Microsoft's FY25 market-based Scope 2 jumped about 10x after it stopped counting unbundled RECs. That is the clearest single proof of how much certificate choices drive reported numbers.

### Cited Findings
**Guardian analysis (published 15 Sept 2024; data 2020–2022, so now dated)**
- The Guardian estimated that the in-house data centre emissions of Google, Microsoft, Meta and Apple for 2020–2022 were about 7.62x what was officially reported, i.e. 662% higher. It reached this by substituting location-based figures for the reported market-based ones. Amazon was excluded because its data-centre emissions could not be isolated. — [eceee summary of Guardian](https://www.eceee.org/all-news/news/data-center-emissions-probably-662-higher-than-big-tech-claims-can-it-keep-up-the-ruse/); [Asia Financial](https://www.asiafinancial.com/big-techs-real-data-centre-emissions-660-higher-the-guardian); original article: theguardian.com/technology/2024/sep/15/data-center-gas-emissions-tech
- Starkest example: Meta's official 2022 Scope 2 was 273 tCO2 (market-based). On a location-based basis it was about 3.8 million tCO2. — [eceee](https://www.eceee.org/all-news/news/data-center-emissions-probably-662-higher-than-big-tech-claims-can-it-keep-up-the-ruse/)
- Some per-company values were inferred from broader Scope 2 comparisons, not disclosed directly, so the 662% figure is an estimate. — [eceee](https://www.eceee.org/all-news/news/data-center-emissions-probably-662-higher-than-big-tech-claims-can-it-keep-up-the-ruse/)

### Inferences
- Microsoft's FY25 change gives a natural experiment. Dropping unbundled RECs alone raised market-based Scope 2 about 10x (0.26 to 2.7 Mt). If the FY24 location-based figure (about 10 Mt) grew roughly in line with the 24% rise in electricity use, FY25 location-based would be around 11–12 Mt, still about 4x the new market-based figure. This is an estimate, not a disclosed number.
- The rhetorical gap is now driven less by Scope 2 and more by Scope 3, which is 80–86% of footprints at Google and Microsoft. Even "honest" Scope 2 reporting leaves most of the AI build-out footprint in the supply chain.
- Amazon's simultaneous claims of "100% renewable matched" and "+34% Scope 2 emissions" are only reconcilable through method ambiguity. That is a strong hook for content piece A.

## Q2. Where does the GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision stand, what are company positions, and what changed with SBTi CNZS v2?

### Takeaway
The GHG Protocol's 2025–26 Scope 2 consultation (closed 31 Jan 2026; results 29 Jul 2026) drew strong corporate opposition to mandatory hourly matching and deliverability. In July 2026 the Independent Standards Board leaned towards two parallel market-based methods with different requirements and claims. No final standard or effective date is confirmed. SBTi's final Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2 (11 Jun 2026) makes hourly matching a voluntary "recognition" pathway, not a mandate.

### Cited Findings
**GHG Protocol process**
- The consultation ran until 31 January 2026, after an extension. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494)
- The GHG Protocol published a Scope 2 public consultation summary dated 29 July 2026, revised 25 August 2026. — [GHGP summary 2026-07-29](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/S2-PublicConsultationSummaryofFeedback-2026.07.29.pdf); [GHGP summary 2026-08-25](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/S2-PublicConsultationSummaryofFeedback-2026.08.25.pdf)
- There were nearly 1,100 responses from 56 countries, 54% of them anonymous. — [EnergyTag "Under the hood"](https://energytag.org/under-the-hood-ghgp/)
- On hourly matching, of 909 respondents, 70% gave little or no support, 22% were in favour and 7% neutral. 59% opposed deliverability requirements. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494); [OnestopESG](https://onestopesg.com/esg-resources/article/ghg-protocol-scope-2-revision)
- Companies and trade groups broadly opposed the updates. Academia and NGOs supported them at much higher rates. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494)
- The proposal included hourly matching, deliverability (generation in the same grid region as load), a revised emission-factor hierarchy, exemptions for smaller organisations, and a legacy clause letting existing long-term contracts use annual matching for their original term. — [Compare Your Footprint](https://www.compareyourfootprint.com/en-us/blog/ghg-protocol-scope-2-changes-2026); [Renewabl](https://www.renewabl.com/post/ghg-protocol-scope-2-revision-what-it-means-for-your-next-ppa); [Go2 Markets](https://www.go2-markets.com/insights/proposed-changes-to-the-ghgp-scope-2-guidance)
- At its July 2026 meeting, the Independent Standards Board (ISB) showed majority support for developing two market-based methods with differentiated requirements and claims. The Scope 2 Technical Working Group is to develop the approach. — [GHGP blog](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-market-based-method-update-2025-2026-public-consultation-current-status)
- Also in July 2026, the GHG Protocol announced it would consolidate its corporate standards with ISO's. One secondary source says the next milestone is an integrated public consultation on the consolidated standard in Q2 2027. — [GHGP announcement](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/ghg-protocol-announces-key-standard-development-updates); [Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/ghg-protocol-and-iso-standard-consolidation-what-the-july-2026-announcement-means-for-your-carbon-accounting)
- Some commentary describes a "Method 1 vs Method 2" framing for the 2026 revision. — [Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/ghg-protocol-scope-2-revision-2026-method-1-vs-method-2-and-what-executives-should-do-now)

**Company positions**
- Google responded publicly in support of the draft updates. Apple, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft were notably absent from the public responses. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494)
- The Emissions First Partnership (members include Amazon, Intel, Meta and Salesforce) declined to respond publicly, but InfluenceMap found evidence of its participation. The group backs "carbon matching" / emissions-impact accounting over hourly matching. Membership lists vary by source. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/corporate-clean-energy-procurement-carbon-matching-meta-google-amazon-tabors-report/652011/)
- Apple, Amazon and Schneider Electric warned the GHG Protocol that tougher Scope 2 rules would slow the corporate energy transition. — [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/apple-amazon-schneider-electric-warn-ghg-protocol-that-proposed-scope-2-accounting-changes-will-slow-corporate-energy-transition/)
- Microsoft has funded emissions-accounting research through REsurety, per InfluenceMap. Its formal consultation position was not found. — [InfluenceMap](https://influencemap.org/insight/Corporate-Engagement-on-the-GHG-Protocol-Scope-2-Consultation-39494)
- EnergyTag (pro-hourly) published a guidebook urging consultation responses. — [EnergyTag guidebook (Nov 2025)](https://energytag.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GHGP-Scope-2-Public-Consultation-Guidebook.pdf); [EnergyTag FAQ](https://energytag.org/ghgp-webinar-questions/)

**SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2.0**
- The final version was published on 11 June 2026 after two public consultations in 2025. — [SBTi CNZS v2 PDF](https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Corporate-Net-Zero-Standard-version-2.pdf); [SBTi criteria](https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Corporate-Net-Zero-Standard-V2-Criteria.pdf); [SBTi blog on 2nd draft](https://sciencebasedtargets.org/blog/whats-next-for-net-zero-an-updated-draft-of-the-corporate-net-zero-standard-v2)
- Hourly matching sits in an optional recognition programme with tiered thresholds: at least 50% hourly-matched until 2030, at least 75% until 2035, and at least 90% from 2035, with third-party assurance for participants. — [SBTi criteria](https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Corporate-Net-Zero-Standard-V2-Criteria.pdf)
- Secondary readings conflict. One says large consumers (≥10 GWh/yr) must report their hourly-matched share from 2030 while matching itself stays voluntary. ClimatePartner describes it as a requirement for large consumers from 2030. Soldera calls it semi-optional, with more weight on deliverability and generator age. — [ClimatePartner](https://www.climatepartner.com/en/knowledge/blog/sbti-net-zero-standard-v2); [Soldera](https://www.soldera.org/articles/guide-to-market-based-scope-2-sbti-cnzs-june-2026-net-zero-standard-v2-explained); [EcoAct](https://eco-act.com/blog/sbti-corporate-net-zero-standard-version-2-0/)

### Inferences
- The likely outcome is a two-tier market-based system: a stricter hourly and deliverable method alongside a looser legacy-style method. Both "camps" would get a sanctioned claim. That could institutionalise, rather than resolve, the Google/Microsoft versus Amazon/Meta split.
- With a consolidated GHGP–ISO consultation slated for around Q2 2027, final rules plausibly land in 2027–28, with effective dates and legacy grandfathering pushing real-world impact towards the end of the decade. This is inference; no confirmed date exists.

### Gaps
- No confirmed final-publication or effective date for the revised Scope 2 guidance. The "~2027 finalisation" in the brief is not confirmed by GHGP sources found.
- No primary text found for the Emissions First Partnership submission or Microsoft's formal consultation position. Microsoft's support for hourly matching is inferred from its 100/100/0 by 2030 commitment, not from a consultation filing.

## Q3. What is 24/7 CFE: Google's goal and CFE%, the UN Compact, EnergyTag Granular Certificates, and research on hourly matching?

### Takeaway
Google's hourly CFE share has stalled around 64–66% while its electricity demand has surged. Its 2025 CFE was reported at about 65%, down from 66% in 2024. Granular Certificate infrastructure (EnergyTag-accredited issuers since June 2025) now exists. Peer-reviewed modelling (Princeton/TU Berlin, Joule 2025) finds that hourly matching is the only procurement strategy that reliably cuts system emissions, though this is contested.

### Cited Findings
- Google defines CFE% as the average percentage of carbon-free energy consumed in a location on an hourly basis, including its CFE investments in that grid. — [Google Cloud region carbon](https://cloud.google.com/sustainability/region-carbon)
- 2023: 64% global hourly CFE; ten regions at ≥90% CFE and thirteen at ≥85%. — [ESIG/Google presentation](https://www.esig.energy/attachment/serve/session-3b-the-path-to-24-7-carbon-free-energy-allegra-reister-2)
- 2024: 66% CFE; 2025: about 65% (secondary readings of the 2026 report). ## Q4. What are the criticisms of unbundled RECs, of Amazon's use of them, and of EU Guarantees of Origin?

### Takeaway
Unbundled RECs are cheap certificates bought separately from the power and rarely drive new build. They have been central to "100% renewable" claims, especially Amazon's. Microsoft's FY25 move away from them shows their scale. US state attorneys general (2025) and Amazon's own employees have challenged them.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon Employees for Climate Justice (July 2024, on 2023 data) said 68% of Amazon's RECs were unbundled and did not fund new infrastructure. They put Amazon's "real" clean power share at about 22%. Market-based Scope 2 rose from about 0.26 Mt to about 2.7 Mt. — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/microsoft-adjusts-climate-agenda-as-emissions-leap/); [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/microsofts-data-center-expansion-drove-25-emissions-spike-2025-environmental-sustainability-report/825082/)
- Tech Times (July 2026): "renewable certificates cannot cover the supply chain carbon". — [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319712/20260704/google-ai-electricity-37-renewable-certificates-cannot-cover-supply-chain-carbon.htm)
- General criticism of GOs and EACs: they are annual and allow temporal and geographic mismatch, so one hour or region's clean output can "cover" another's fossil consumption. — [Eurelectric](https://www.eurelectric.org/in-detail/247-2/); [NESO paper on trading 24/7 CFE](https://www.neso.energy/document/365496/download)

### Inferences
- The Microsoft FY25 restatement is the best quantitative proof point for piece A. A single accounting choice, dropping unbundled RECs, moved reported Scope 2 by about 2.4 Mt, more than the whole of many large companies' footprints.

### Gaps
- Amazon's 2025 (July 2026) unbundled-REC share was not found.
- No source was found on the Norway/Iceland GO problem (hydro GOs sold abroad, leaving domestic "residual mix" consumers with fossil-heavy attributes). This is commonly cited but unverified here.
- Specific EU GO reforms (RED III, CEN EN 16325 revision, AIB hourly GO pilots) were not researched for lack of tool budget.

## Q5. Scope 3 and embodied carbon: chips, servers, concrete, steel, mass timber, GPU LCAs and e-waste

### Takeaway
Scope 3 makes up roughly 80% (Google, 2025) to 86% (Microsoft, FY25) of hyperscaler footprints, and data centre construction alone was about 2.3 Mt (around 20% of Scope 3) for Google in 2025. Per-chip embodied carbon is in the hundreds of kgCO2e per accelerator, and over 2 tCO2e for a B200 baseboard. Chipmaker direct emissions, including fluorinated gases, are still rising in absolute terms.

### Cited Findings
**Hyperscaler Scope 3 share**
- Google 2025: Scope 3 about 11.6 Mt, around 80% of 14.5 Mt total (73% in 2024), up 25% year on year. Data centre construction about 2.3 Mt, roughly 20% of Scope 3. — [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/googles-emissions-continue-to-climb-due-to-ai-buildout/824373/); [The Planet Brief](https://theplanetbrief.com/esg/google-2025-environmental-report/)
- Microsoft FY25: Scope 3 about 85.8% of about 20.3 Mt total. — [BiocharToday](https://biochartoday.com/news/microsoft-2026-environmental-sustainability-report-details-25-percent-emissions-spike-driven-by-global-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure-expansion/)
- Microsoft (2024 framing): direct emissions down 6.3% over three years, indirect emissions up 30.9%, partly from data centre construction. — [Microsoft Source](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/sustainability/microsoft-builds-first-datacenters-with-wood-to-slash-carbon-emissions/); [E&T](https://eandt.theiet.org/2024/11/04/microsoft-builds-datacentres-out-wood-bid-cut-its-rising-carbon-emissions)
- Hyperscalers may exclude upfront construction carbon, and embodied carbon can be allocated differently across scopes. — [MECLA report (Apr 2026)](https://mecla.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/MECLA-Report-on-Reducing-Embodied-Carbon-in-Data-Centre-Delivery.pdf)
- Large data centres historically reported operational Scope 2 above Scope 3, but newer studies suggest embodied emissions may now dominate. — [ATLAS computing paper (arXiv 2505.08530)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08530)

**Construction materials and mass timber**
- Microsoft's two Northern Virginia data centres (announced Oct/Nov 2024) use a hybrid of cross-laminated timber, steel and concrete. Microsoft projects embodied carbon about 35% lower than conventional steel construction and 65% lower than typical precast concrete. These are design estimates, not measured results. Microsoft will also require contractors to use low-carbon materials through updated contract language. — [Microsoft Source](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/sustainability/microsoft-builds-first-datacenters-with-wood-to-slash-carbon-emissions/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-uses-wood-to-build-two-data-centers-in-northern-virginia/); [Gensler](https://www.gensler.com/projects/microsoft-mass-timber-hyperscale-data-centers)
- The MECLA (Australia) April 2026 report models a hyperscale AI build of 8,000 eight-GPU nodes and sets out embodied-carbon reduction levers in data centre delivery. — [MECLA](https://mecla.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/MECLA-Report-on-Reducing-Embodied-Carbon-in-Data-Centre-Delivery.pdf)

**Semiconductors (TSMC)**
- TSMC gross Scope 1 emissions: 1,596,031 tCO2e (2023), 1,825,872 (2024), 2,196,516 (2025). Perfluorinated compounds: 335,714, 347,206 and 403,167 tCO2e respectively. — [TSMC SASB index](https://esg.tsmc.com/en-US/ESG-data-hub/framework-disclosure/sasb?cb=was_741_ysje); [TSMC 2025 Sustainability Report](https://esg.tsmc.com/file/public/2025-TSMC-Sustainability-Report-e.pdf)
- TSMC says it is installing and replacing local scrubbers and piloting carbon capture. Its F-GHG declaration for 2025 in-scope plants states at least 75% abatement and lists 97% total F-GHG reduction (a third-party summary says 96%). New targets: Scope 1+2 down 31% and Scope 3 down 15% from a 2025 base year. — [TSMC F-GHG declaration](https://esg.tsmc.com/file/public/TSMC_F-GHGs_Emissions_Reduction_Declaration_c.pdf); [TSMC 2025 SR](https://esg.tsmc.com/file/public/2025-TSMC-Sustainability-Report-e.pdf)

**GPU, accelerator and server LCAs**
- NVIDIA's FY2025 sustainability report says it published its first product carbon footprint (PCF) for a GPU-accelerated server, with manufacturing emissions lower than earlier estimates. NVIDIA Scope 3 was 6,912,577 tCO2e in FY25, versus 3,638,432 in FY24. Its Scope 3 target is a 75% cut in emissions intensity from use of sold products. — [NVIDIA FY2025 Sustainability Report](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/NVIDIA-Sustainability-Report-Fiscal-Year-2025.pdf)
- NVIDIA PCF for an HGX H100 eight-GPU baseboard: about 1,312 kgCO2e embodied, roughly 164 kg per GPU, with memory the largest contributor. The PCF's scope is partial. — [Introl blog](https://introl.com/blog/carbon-accounting-ai-workloads-gpu-emissions-tracking-2025); [Lucie 7B LCA, arXiv 2607.05408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05408)
- HGX B200 baseboard: about 2,274 kgCO2e embodied. — [MECLA](https://mecla.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/MECLA-Report-on-Reducing-Embodied-Carbon-in-Data-Centre-Delivery.pdf)
- Google TPU cradle-to-grave LCA (arXiv 2502.01671, Feb 2025; TPU v4i, v4, v5e, v5p, v6e): 386 to 1,101 kgCO2e embodied per TPU over a 6-year life including host and transport. TPU manufacturing and transport alone is 208 to 585 kg. Construction is excluded. — [arXiv 2502.01671](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01671); [arXiv 2608.16657](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16657)
- A vendor blog (low reliability) estimates manufacturing at 50–80% of a server's lifetime footprint on a 3-year refresh cycle. — [Cloudatler](https://cloudatler.com/blog/scope-3-emissions-tracking-gpu-supply-chain)

**E-waste**
- Nature Computational Science (Wang et al., 28 Oct 2024): generative AI could produce 1.2–5.0 Mt of cumulative e-waste in 2020–2030, or up to about 2.5 Mt per year by 2030 with no mitigation. The 2023 baseline was about 2,400 tonnes. Circular-economy strategies could avoid more than 3 Mt. For scale, global e-waste was 62 Mt in 2022 (UN Global E-waste Monitor). Media headlines of "up to 5 Mt by 2030" conflate the cumulative and annual figures. — [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2024-10-29/generative-ai-generating-millions-tonnes-electronic-waste-data/104514376); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/generative-ai-could-create-1-000-times-more-e-waste-by-2030); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/10/28/1106316/ai-e-waste/); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/e-waste)

### Inferences
- The scale is easy to illustrate. At about 164–284 kgCO2e per GPU (H100 to B200 baseboard per GPU), a 100,000-GPU cluster embeds roughly 16–28 ktCO2e in accelerator baseboards alone, before servers, networking, storage and the building. This is a back-of-envelope estimate from the cited PCFs.
- TSMC's PFC emissions rose about 20% in 2025 despite more than 95% abatement claims at in-scope fabs, consistent with AI-driven output growth outpacing abatement.

### Gaps
- No hyperscaler breakdown was found splitting Scope 3 into purchased goods (servers and chips) versus capital goods (construction) versus other categories, beyond Google's 2.3 Mt construction figure.
- Low-carbon concrete and green steel procurement specifics (e.g., Microsoft/Amazon purchase agreements with H2 Green Steel/Stegra, CarbonCure, Amazon's low-carbon concrete) were not researched for lack of tool budget.
- NVIDIA's own B200/GB200 PCF document was not retrieved directly; the 2,274 kg figure comes via MECLA.

## Q6. Carbon intensity of AI per query and per training run, and methodological criticisms

### Takeaway
Company disclosures are converging on "a prompt is about 0.2–0.4 Wh". But boundaries differ: Google's 0.24 Wh is a median text prompt with no token count, and its narrower method gives 0.10 Wh. OpenAI's 0.34 Wh comes with no method. Mistral's ISO-aligned LCA (1.14 gCO2e per 400-token response) includes hardware manufacturing. So the figures are not comparable, and none is independently verified.

### Cited Findings
- Google (paper August 2025, data May 2025): median Gemini Apps text prompt uses 0.24 Wh, emits 0.03 gCO2e and consumes 0.26 mL of water. The narrower "existing approach" boundary gives 0.10 Wh. Energy per median prompt fell 33x in 12 months and carbon per prompt fell 44x, attributed to software and model improvements (about 23x) and better utilisation (about 1.4x). — [Google arXiv 2508.15734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15734); [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/google-says-a-prompt-uses-0-24-wh-researchers-say-the-math-is-incomplete/)
- Criticisms of Google's figure: no independent verification; prompt length and token count not specified; unclear derivation of the Scope 1+3 component (0.010 gCO2e per prompt); use of market-based emission factors; "median" hides heavy reasoning and multimodal use; and the 0.24 versus 0.10 boundary choice. — [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/is-googles-reveal-of-geminis-impact-progress-or-greenwashing/); [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/google-boasts-that-a-single-gemini-prompt-uses-roughly-the-same-energy-as-a-basic-search-but-thats-not-painting-the-full-picture); [Greenly](https://greenly.earth/en-us/leaf-media/data-stories/ai-efficiency-a-gemini-reality-check-with-greenly)
- OpenAI (Sam Altman blog, June 2025): an average ChatGPT query uses about 0.34 Wh, with no methodology. One independent benchmark estimated about 0.42 Wh for a short GPT-4o prompt. — [Hannah Ritchie (Aug 2025)](https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/ai-footprint-august-2025); [arXiv 2505.09598 "How Hungry is AI?"](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09598v5)
- Mistral (July 2025, with Carbone 4 and ADEME, ISO 14040/44 and GHG Protocol Product Standard): Mistral Large 2 lifecycle emissions of 20.4 ktCO2e (training plus 18 months of use; one paywalled report says Mistral attributes about 95% of lifecycle emissions to training, which is unverified), and 1.14 gCO2e plus 45 mL of water per 400-token Le Chat response, including hardware manufacturing. — [MultiLingual](https://multilingual.com/mistral-ai-lifecycle-analysis/); [Hugging Face (Sasha Luccioni)](https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/environmental-impact-disclosures)
- Hugging Face's comparison of AI company disclosures concludes they are not comparable across providers, given differing boundaries and metrics. — [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/environmental-impact-disclosures)
- An August 2026 preprint, "The ultimate carbon cost of a ChatGPT query", attempts a fuller cradle-to-grave per-query estimate, including embodied hardware. — [arXiv 2608.16657](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16657)
- A training LCA of Lucie 7B on France's Jean Zay supercomputer (July 2026) applies NVIDIA's per-GPU embodied figure and notes its partial scope. — [arXiv 2607.05408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05408)
- Historical baseline (2021): Patterson et al. at Google estimated training emissions for GPT-3 and other large models. Now old. — [arXiv 2104.10350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10350)

### Inferences
- Per-prompt numbers are tiny by construction, while aggregate emissions are rising 16–25% a year across Amazon, Google and Microsoft. Per-unit efficiency disclosures are therefore being used rhetorically to deflect from absolute growth (a Jevons dynamic). This links pieces A and B: Google's per-prompt carbon depends on market-based factors, the very accounting under dispute.

### Gaps
- The exact share of Mistral's 20.4 kt attributable to training alone was not verified. The only figure found (95%) comes from a paywalled article.
- No 2026 updates to Google's or OpenAI's per-prompt figures were found, nor any Anthropic or Meta per-query disclosures.
- Frontier training-run carbon figures (e.g., GPT-4/5, Gemini 2.x) are not disclosed by companies, and no reliable estimates were gathered in this session.
