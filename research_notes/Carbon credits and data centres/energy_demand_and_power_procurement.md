## 1. How much electricity do data centres use today, and what are the 2030/2035 projections (global, US, national shares)?

### Takeaway
Data centres used roughly **485 TWh in 2025, about 1.5% of global electricity** (IEA, April 2026). The IEA expects that to **roughly double to about 950 TWh by 2030 (about 3%)**, with AI-focused use tripling. US numbers have been revised sharply upward. LBNL's 2025 update and EPRI's February 2026 scenarios now put data centres at **about 9–17% of US electricity by 2030**, up from about 4–5% today. Ireland is the extreme case at **23% of national electricity in 2025**.

### Cited Findings
**Global (IEA)**
- **IEA "Key Questions on Energy and AI" (WEO special report, April 2026):** data centres use "almost 500 TWh", slightly more than 1.5% of global electricity. Total data-centre consumption is projected to double from **485 TWh (2025) to 950 TWh (2030)**, about 3% of global demand. **AI-focused** use is expected to **triple to about 465 TWh** by 2030. The near-term outlook stays close to the 2025 central case, with upside risk in the longer term. — [IEA Key Questions on Energy and AI (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3179f7f8-01f6-4dd6-bffa-c9f7b73f1dc9/KeyQuestionsonEnergyandAI.pdf); summarised by [Enlit](https://www.enlit.world/library/ai-and-data-centre-electricity-use-continue-to-surge-iea-finds) and [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/ai-five-charts-that-put-data-centre-energy-use-and-emissions-into-context)
- IEA-based sources give **about 17% growth in data-centre electricity use in 2025**. One third-party site says 15%. — [search synthesis of IEA-derived sources](https://www.enlit.world/library/ai-and-data-centre-electricity-use-continue-to-surge-iea-finds) (flag: verify against the IEA primary)
- **IEA Electricity 2026 (early 2026):** data centres are expected to account for **about 50% of global electricity demand growth out to 2030** (this likely refers to advanced economies; check the wording in the primary). — [IEA Electricity 2026 – Demand](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/demand)
- **IEA Electricity Mid-Year Update 2026 (July 2026):** global electricity demand is forecast to rise **3.6% in 2026**, with data centres "the main driver" of demand growth. — [IEA Electricity Mid-Year Update 2026 (PDF)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3c66a000-244c-4efb-aaeb-cda33eb833da/ElectricityMid-YearUpdate2026.pdf)
- **[background, not re-verified this session] IEA "Energy and AI" (April 2025):** about **415 TWh in 2024 (about 1.5%)**, with a base case of **about 945 TWh by 2030**. The US and China account for nearly 80% of growth. The 2026 numbers above are a slight upward revision of the 2025 baseline (415 → 485 TWh reflects one more year of growth). — [IEA Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai)
- Beware outlier claims: one widely circulated site says "1,000 TWh by 2026", which does **not** match IEA figures. — [tech-insider.org (unreliable)](https://tech-insider.org/ai-data-center-power-crisis-2026/)

**United States**
- **LBNL, Dec 2024 report (older data):** data centres used about **176 TWh in 2023 (4.4% of US electricity)**. It projected **325–580 TWh by 2028 (6.7–12%)**. — [Berkeley Lab news release, 15 Jan 2025](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2025/01/15/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-in-electricity-demand-from-data-centers/)
- **LBNL "United States Data Center Energy Usage Report – 2025 Update"** (circulated around June 2026). Its reference case is **464 TWh in 2028 and 649 TWh in 2030**, which is **about 11.8% of forecast US electricity in 2030**. The 2030 scenario range is **521–843 TWh (9.5–15.3%)**. — [LBNL 2025 Update (PDF via RTO Insider)](https://www.rtoinsider.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/data-center-energy-usage-2025-update.pdf)
- **EPRI "Powering Intelligence: Updated U.S. Data Center Scenarios" (Feb 2026):** data centres reach **9–17% of US electricity by 2030**, up from 4–5% today. That is about **380–790 TWh**, roughly 60% higher than EPRI's 2024 projections. **Virginia's** data-centre share could reach **39–57% of state electricity by 2030**. — [EPRI Feb 2026 PDF](https://esca.epri.com/_astro/2026-02-powering-intelligence-updated-u-s-data-center-scenarios.CqZpC1-r.pdf); [EPRI executive summary](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/executive-summary.html); [mgrid summary](https://mgrid.org/2026/02/27/epri-data-centers-17-percent-us-electricity-2030/)
- **BloombergNEF:** data centres reach about **12% of US electricity in 2030 and 20% in 2035**, versus **5.9% currently**. BNEF's US data-centre forecast is cited as **194 GW** of capacity (this year's horizon is not confirmed in the extract). This comes from a secondary summary only. — [mlq.ai summary of BNEF](https://mlq.ai/news/bnefs-194-gw-data-center-forecast-shows-ais-power-pipeline-outrunning-the-grid/)
- A secondary fact-book puts data centres at **4.7% of US power in 2024**. — [energyfactbook.com](https://energyfactbook.com/data-centers/) (secondary)
- **[background, not re-verified this session] Goldman Sachs (April 2024):** forecast **160% growth in data-centre power demand by 2030**, reaching about 8% of US electricity. — [Goldman Sachs Insights](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/AI-poised-to-drive-160-increase-in-power-demand)

**National and regional shares**
- **Ireland (CSO, published 7 July 2026):** data centres used **7,663 GWh in 2025**, up 10% from 6,973 GWh in 2024. That is **23% of metered electricity**, versus 22% in 2024 (Computing.co.uk says 21%) and **5% in 2015**. All urban and rural households together used 28%. — [Irish Times, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/07/07/data-centres-account-for-almost-one-quarter-of-irish-electricity-usage-in-2025/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/data-centers-in-ireland-consumed-23-of-national-electricity-in-2025-report/); [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/datacentre/ireland-datacentres-quarter-electricity-consumption); [FactCheckNI](https://factcheckni.org/articles/energy-on-this-island-do-irelands-data-centres-now-consume-nearly-a-quarter-of-all-electricity-in-the-south/)
- **Europe comparison:** a secondary scorecard puts Ireland's 23.2% highest of 15 European countries, about 7.7 times the European average of about 3%. An ICIS-based analysis ranks the **Netherlands second**, with a share about 2.5 times lower than Ireland's (likely 2023 data, so roughly 8–9%). — [StackPath.ie (secondary, method unverified)](https://stackpath.ie/how-much-electricity-do-data-centres-use-ireland)
- **Virginia:** EPRI projects **39–57% by 2030** (see above). — [EPRI](https://esca.epri.com/_astro/2026-02-powering-intelligence-updated-u-s-data-center-scenarios.CqZpC1-r.pdf)
- **Japan:** METI expects data-centre capacity to rise **68% between 2022 and 2026**. — [Brunel](https://www.brunel.net/en-sg/blog/data-centre/asia-data-center-energy-demand)
- **Southeast Asia:** power supply is lagging data-centre growth, and renewables cover only about 30% of demand in the fastest-growing hubs. — [Brunel](https://www.brunel.net/en-sg/blog/data-centre/asia-data-center-energy-demand)

### Inferences
- Every major forecaster revised upward between 2024 and 2026. EPRI raised its 2030 figure by about 60%, and LBNL's 2028 reference case (464 TWh) sits in the upper half of its own 2024 range. The US is plausibly heading from about 4–5% (2023–24) to about 10–12% central (2030). Ranges are wide because chip supply, financing and grid access are uncertain.
- Globally, data centres remain small: about 1.5% today and about 3% in 2030. They matter because they are **geographically concentrated** (Ireland, Virginia, and parts of Texas, Ohio and Georgia) and dominate **incremental** demand growth.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 figures were found for **Singapore** (historically about 7% of national electricity per older estimates; not verified this session), **Netherlands** (current), **China** or **India**. The search returned no reliable India/China share data, so CEA/NEA or IEA country breakdowns should be consulted.
- Current **Virginia/Dominion** share of today's load was not found; only EPRI's 2030 projection was.
- The BNEF primary report was not accessed. Its numbers come from a secondary summary only.
- **DeepSeek** (Jan 2025) efficiency debate: no 2026 sources were found in this session (see section 6).

## 2. What grid issues have arisen (capacity prices, bills, rate cases, gas buildouts, coal delays, behind-the-meter, xAI)?

### Takeaway
PJM is the epicentre. Capacity prices have hit the FERC-approved cap in **three or four consecutive auctions**, and the July 2026 auction (2028/29) **fell 6.8 GW short** of the reliability target. The market monitor attributes **about 38–46% of capacity costs to data centres**. Utilities are delaying coal and peaker retirements and building gas. Turbine backlogs stretch to about 2031. Developers are turning to behind-the-meter gas, and xAI's unpermitted turbines in Memphis/Southaven are the flashpoint.

### Cited Findings
**PJM capacity market**
- **2025/26 BRA (held July 2024; older data):** **$269.92/MW-day**, a record at the time, up from about $28.92 the prior year (roughly 9–10 times higher). — [IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/projected-data-center-growth-spurs-pjm-capacity-prices-factor-10); [ElectricityRates](https://electricityrates.com/resources/pjm-prices-skyrocket-for-2025-2026/). Note: some sources mislabel the $329.17 figure as 2025/26. $329.17 is the **2026/27** auction (July 2025), which cleared at the cap.
- **2027/28 BRA (Dec 2025):** cleared at the cap, **$333.44/MW-day**, the third consecutive auction at the ceiling. — [search synthesis; ChargedUp](https://www.chargeduppro.com/p/pjm-capacity-auction-333-mw-day-data-center-cost-cre-noi-2026)
- **2028/29 BRA (results 14 July 2026):** cleared at the cap of **$325/MW-day**. It procured **138,318 MW** but landed **6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement**, at a total cost of **$16.4 billion**. Only about **525 MW of new resources** cleared. The uncapped price would reportedly have been about **$555/MW-day RTO-wide and $777/MW-day in ComEd**. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-interconnection-capacity-auction-data-center/808264/); [whopaysfordatacenters.com](https://whopaysfordatacenters.com/capacity-auctions/); [DistroForge](https://distroforge.com/blog/pjm-capacity-auction-results-backstop-procurement/)
- **Independent Market Monitor:** data centres drove **$6.3 billion (38%) of the 2028/29 auction's $16.4 billion** cost, and **$29.4 billion of $63.6 billion (46%)** across four auctions. — [mgrid.org, 23 Jul 2026](https://mgrid.org/2026/07/23/data-centers-drove-6-3-billion-of-pjms-latest-capacity-auction-cost-market-monitor-says/); [ChargedUp](https://www.chargeduppro.com/p/pjm-capacity-auction-333-mw-day-data-center-cost-cre-noi-2026)
- Illinois Citizens Utility Board (15 Jul 2026): sustained high PJM prices "ramp up urgency for data center reform". — [CUB Illinois](https://www.citizensutilityboard.org/blog/2026/07/15/cub-sustained-high-pjm-capacity-prices-ramp-up-urgency-for-data-center-reform/)

**Rate cases and residential bills (Virginia/Dominion)**
- Virginia SCC biennial review (case PUR-2025-00058, decided late 2025). It approved a base-rate increase of **$565.7 million (2026) and $209.9 million (2027)**, against Dominion's request of **$822 million for 2026**. The residential impact is about **+$11.24/month (7.5%) in 2026 plus $2.36/month in 2027** for 1,000 kWh per one tracker, or about **+$16/month** per Inside Climate News (sources differ, possibly because ICN includes riders). — [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/07012026/virginia-regulators-approve-new-dominion-rates/); [Virginia Scope](https://www.virginiascope.com/scc-approves-increase-in-dominion-energy-power-bills/)
- The SCC created a new **GS-5 rate class** for very large loads (data centres), with **upfront collateral** for grid upgrades. Dominion had proposed **14-year minimum contracts** so high-use customers pay even if load does not materialise. — [Virginia Mercury, 3 Sep 2025](https://virginiamercury.com/2025/09/03/dominion-proposes-higher-utility-rates-new-rate-class-for-data-centers/); [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/07012026/virginia-regulators-approve-new-dominion-rates/)
- One analysis says **Dominion bills have risen 34–38% since 2022**. — [gridflexibility.fyi (secondary)](https://gridflexibility.fyi/case-studies/dominion-virginia)
- Large-load tariff and rate cases are also active in **Kentucky, Indiana, Arizona and Pennsylvania**. — [Metro Colo Advisory tracker](https://metrocoloadvisory.com/data-center-electric-rates/)

**Gas buildout and coal and peaker retirements delayed**
- Dominion's IRP cited data-centre demand as a reason to **postpone retiring plants including the Clover coal station** (Halifax County). The SCC approved the **Chesterfield gas plant**. — [Clean Virginia rate case alert](https://www.cleanvirginia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Dominions-2025-Rate-Case-Issue-Alert.pdf); [Virginia Mercury](https://virginiamercury.com/2025/09/03/dominion-proposes-higher-utility-rates-new-rate-class-for-data-centers/)
- Utility Dive: "Coal- and gas-fired power plants have a new best friend: data centers". It reports utilities delaying fossil retirements and proposing new gas across the Southeast and Midwest. — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/fossil-fuel-gas-coal-climate-data-centers/753565/)
- **Gas turbine supply crunch (2026):** Mitsubishi Power says new large-frame orders face delivery **around 2031**, and its large-frame backlog is about **35 GW (up from 23 GW a year earlier)**. GE Vernova's backlog is reported at about 100–116 GW (secondary sources, conflicting). Siemens Energy is at about 69 GW with lead times of three years or more. — [Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/electrical-generation/mitsubishi-power-deliveries-stretch-to-2031-as-gas-turbine-shortage-hits-data-centers-and-utilities/); [briefs.co](https://www.briefs.co/news/gas-turbine-demand-is-outrunning-factory-capacity-mitsubishi/); [Engineering (journal) 2026](https://www.engineering.org.cn/engi/EN/10.1016/j.eng.2026.02.014)

**Behind-the-meter (BTM) generation**
- 451 Research tracks **more than 100 BTM generation projects** linked to data centres. A BTM gas plant can be built in **about 18 months**, versus multi-year interconnection queues. — [S&P Global Market Intelligence (Mar 2026)](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/3/data-center-boom-presents-evolving-opportunities-for-us-gas-utilities-100141898); [Enki.AI (secondary)](https://enkiai.com/data-center/gas-to-power-boom-ai-drives-2026-on-site-energy-shift/)
- **Microsoft:** its 2026 report does not cover later deals, including **gas-powered data centres and a 20-year power agreement with Chevron**. — [Heatmap / MobileSyrup reporting on 2026 reports](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/)

**xAI Memphis / Southaven**
- **10 Mar 2026:** the Mississippi Environmental Quality Permit Board unanimously approved xAI affiliate **MZX Tech** to install **41 permanent gas turbines** at Southaven, Mississippi, to power **Colossus 2** (Memphis). These replace **27 unpermitted turbines** it had been running there. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/10/elon-musk-xai-permit-for-mississippi-plant-despite-pollution-concerns.html); [Mississippi Free Press](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/mississippi-permit-board-grants-xais-request-for-41-southaven-gas-turbines-to-power-memphis-data-center/)
- **NAACP (represented by SELC and Earthjustice):** sent a 60-day notice of intent to sue in February 2026 and filed an **appeal on 9 April 2026** alleging faulty pollution data. — [SELC](https://www.selc.org/news/inside-memphis-fight-against-xai/); [Tennessee Lookout](https://tennesseelookout.com/2026/03/18/a-battle-over-data-centers-heats-up-along-the-mississippi-tennessee-state-line/)
- **EPA (Jan 2026):** revised its NSPS, saying large gas turbines need permits **even for temporary operation**. — [Mississippi Free Press](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/mississippi-permit-board-grants-xais-request-for-41-southaven-gas-turbines-to-power-memphis-data-center/)
- **July 2026 reporting:** xAI installed **59 gas turbines** for Colossus 2 **without federal clean-air permits**, with at least 57 running in Southaven. — [Technology.org, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/07/15/xai-59-unpermitted-gas-turbines-southaven-colossus-2/)
- **April 2026:** Sen. Whitehouse (EPW minority) demanded answers on xAI's "pattern of operating illegal data center gas plants" and criticised EPA non-enforcement. — [Senate EPW](https://www.epw.senate.gov/public/index.cfm/2026/4/whitehouse-calls-for-answers-about-musk-backed-xai-s-pattern-of-operating-illegal-data-center-gas-plants)

**Co-location / FERC (grid-cost dimension)**: see section 4.

### Inferences
- The PJM pattern (capped prices, a reliability shortfall, and almost no new supply clearing) shows that the binding constraint is **physical deliverability** (queues, turbines, transmission), not price signals. Price caps shift risk onto reliability.
- Cost-allocation reform is now the main policy battleground. Examples include large-load tariffs (Virginia GS-5), collateral and minimum-take contracts, and PJM large-load rules.
- Gas, not clean power, is the default "speed to power" solution. Turbine scarcity (to about 2031) may in turn push more onsite reciprocating engines, fuel cells and batteries, plus delayed fossil retirements.

### Gaps
- **ERCOT** (Texas SB6 large-load rules, load forecasts), **Georgia Power** IRP gas additions, and **EirGrid's** Dublin connection moratorium and 2025–26 policy were not covered this session.
- Interconnection-queue totals (for example LBNL "Queued Up" 2025/26) were not retrieved.
- Current status of the xAI appeal and lawsuit after July 2026 is unconfirmed.
- No independent national estimate of residential bill impact attributable to data centres was found. Utility Dive's "$40–50/month in several states" is a projection only.

## 3. How are hyperscalers' reported emissions trending, and how much is due to AI and data centres?

### Takeaway
The 2026 reports (covering CY2025, or FY2025 for Microsoft) show **double-digit increases** at every major hyperscaler that reported. Microsoft's total rose **about 25% year on year** to about 20.3 MtCO2e, Google **+18%**, and Amazon **+16%**. Each attributes the rise largely to data-centre expansion and construction (Scope 3 embodied carbon). Market-based accounting flatters the Scope 2 numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Microsoft, 2026 Environmental Sustainability Report (published 9 Jul 2026, covers FY2025 to 30 Jun 2025):**
  - Total Scope 1+2+3 emissions **up 25% year on year**, the largest jump since the pandemic. — [Microsoft On the Issues, 9 Jul 2026](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/07/09/responsibly-building-the-ai-future/); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/microsofts-carbon-footprint-jumps-25-as-ai-buildout-challenges-climate-goals/)
  - The total is about **20.3 MtCO2e in FY25, versus about 16.2 Mt in FY24**. — [BioChar Today (secondary)](https://biochartoday.com/news/microsoft-2026-environmental-sustainability-report-details-25-percent-emissions-spike-driven-by-global-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure-expansion/)
  - Drivers are datacentre expansion **and Microsoft's decision to stop using unbundled RECs**. Scope 2 rose **nearly 10 times**, from under 2% to **13% of total**. **Scope 3 is 85.8%**. — [Microsoft 2026 Data Fact Sheet](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf); [Microsoft 2026 ESR](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Sustainability-Report-PDF.pdf)
  - Microsoft maintains its **carbon-negative by 2030** goal and its goal to remove historical emissions by 2050. **Bloomberg reported Microsoft might scale back** the 2030 commitment. — [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/microsofts-carbon-footprint-jumps-25-as-ai-buildout-challenges-climate-goals/); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/)
  - A 2026 shareholder proposal (PX14A6G) on Microsoft's climate and AI disclosures was filed for the FY2026 proxy. — [SEC PX14A6G](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789019/000121465926013276/y1027263px14a6g.htm)
  - **[background, not re-verified this session]** Microsoft's 2024 report showed emissions **+29.1% vs its 2020 baseline**. — [Microsoft 2024 ESR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/corporate-responsibility/sustainability/report)
- **Google, 2026 Environmental Report (CY2025):** emissions **+18%**, partly attributed to its expanding data-centre fleet. Google wrote: "While we remain deeply committed to sustainability, reaching our climate moonshot is getting harder." It claims a **12% reduction in "data centre energy emissions"**, which critics say is plausible only under **market-based** accounting. The net-zero target is 2030. — [MobileSyrup, 2 Jul 2026](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/); [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/google-amazon-microsoft-2026-data)
  - **[background, not re-verified this session]** Google's 2024 report showed **+48% vs 2019** and its 2025 report **about +51% vs 2019**. — [Google Environmental Reports](https://sustainability.google/reports/)
- **Amazon, 2025 Sustainability Report (published mid-2026, CY2025):** emissions **+16%**, "driven predominantly by data center construction". The net-zero target is 2040 (Climate Pledge). — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/); [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/daily/google-amazon-microsoft-2026-data)
- **Cross-company:** the majority of Microsoft, Google and Amazon emissions are **Scope 3**, mostly supply chain and the embodied carbon of construction, chips and servers. Market-based figures understate physical (location-based) emissions, which rose more sharply. — [Bisnow special report](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-sustainability-operations/data-center-climate-emissions-special-report-google-amazon-meta-microsoft); [Internet Policy Review, on 2025 reports](https://policyreview.info/articles/news/big-techs-2025-sustainability-reports/2027)
- **Reporting periods differ:** Google and Amazon report on calendar years, Microsoft on its fiscal year ending 30 June. — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/)

### Inferences
- The driver is now visible in both Scope 2 (Microsoft's REC policy change exposes grid-power emissions) and Scope 3 (construction). The latter is directly attributable to the AI buildout, since capex is overwhelmingly AI and data-centre related. None of the companies quantifies an "AI share" separately.
- Reported FY2025/CY2025 figures **predate** the large 2026 gas and BTM deals, so 2027 reports may show further increases.

### Gaps
- **Meta's 2026 sustainability report** (CY2025 data) was not found. Its existence and figures need checking. Meta was reported as targeting net zero across its value chain by 2030.
- Exact absolute tCO2e for Google and Amazon in 2025, and their location-based vs market-based splits, were not retrieved (the Heatmap page could not be fetched).
- The "23.5%" figure (vs 2020 baseline) could not be confirmed for any company.
- No company discloses AI-specific electricity or emissions. Google's per-prompt figure (**0.24 Wh per median Gemini text prompt**, Aug 2025) is the closest. — [cited in the Sopra Steria commentary](https://www.soprasteria.com/insights/details/industrial-ai-why-everything-now-happens-at-the-edge-ai-and-the-jevons-paradox-can-efficiency-backfire)

## 4. Nuclear deals (restarts, uprates, SMRs) and FERC co-location: status as of 2026

### Takeaway
Hyperscalers have signed roughly **13 nuclear deals totalling about 10 GW** (tracker count, May 2026). Near-term output depends on **restarts** (Crane in 2027, Duane Arnold in early 2029) and **existing plants** (Talen–Amazon, Meta–Vistra, Constellation). SMR and advanced-reactor deliveries (Kairos, X-energy, TerraPower, Oklo) are **2030 or later**. FERC's Dec 2025 order forced PJM to write co-location rules, after it rejected the original Talen–Amazon behind-the-meter structure in 2024.

### Cited Findings
**Aggregate**
- Trackers count about **13 hyperscaler nuclear deals, about 9.8 GW, as of May 2026**. — [presenc.ai tracker](https://presenc.ai/research/hyperscaler-nuclear-ppa-tracker-2026); [SMR Intel](https://smrintel.com/nuclear-data-center-deals/) (aggregators; numbers vary)
- **Carnegie Endowment (June 2026), "Beyond the Hype":** hyperscaler nuclear commitments, especially Meta's 6.6 GW, should be read as **maximum supply and development portfolios to 2035**, not purchased power. It assesses them against US build realities. — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/beyond-the-hype-assessing-hyperscaler-nuclear-commitments-against-us-energy-realities) (full text not accessed)

## 5. Clean PPAs: scale of tech procurement, geothermal and fusion, and criticisms

### Takeaway
Tech companies dominate corporate clean-power buying. **Amazon, Meta, Google and Microsoft signed 49% of the world's 55.9 GW of corporate clean PPAs in 2025.** Global volume nonetheless **fell 10%**, the first decline since 2016. The mix is shifting to **firm** products (nuclear, solar plus storage, geothermal) and long-shot fusion offtakes. Critics question additionality, hourly matching and parallel gas lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- **BNEF corporate clean energy buying (Feb 2026):** 2025 global corporate clean PPA volume was **55.9 GW, down about 10%** from the 2024 record, the first decline since 2016 and still the second-highest year. **Meta contracted 10.24 GW and Amazon 10.22 GW.** The four hyperscalers accounted for **49%** of global activity. **US volume hit a record 29.5 GW**, but the number of unique US buyers halved to **33**. EMEA fell 13% to 17 GW. **Firm, "baseload-like" products** (co-located solar plus storage, hybrid, nuclear) totalled **5.2 GW**. — [pv magazine, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/02/20/corporate-ppa-deals-down-10-in-2025-as-ai-demand-plugs-gaps/); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-meta-google-microsoft-account-for-half-of-global-clean-energy-purchase-deals-in-2025-report/); [ESG News](https://esgnews.com/bloombergnef-report-shows-corporate-clean-energy-buying-declined-in-2025/); [Balkan Green Energy News](https://balkangreenenergynews.com/global-clean-ppa-market-shrinks-for-first-time-in-nearly-one-decade/)
- **Microsoft ended its use of unbundled RECs**, which helped drive its Scope 2 increase. This is a tacit acknowledgement of additionality criticism. — [Microsoft 2026 Data Fact Sheet](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf)
- **Geothermal (Fervo / Google):** Google and Fervo have partnered since 2021. Fervo began delivering power from the first commercial enhanced geothermal pilot (Project Red, Nevada) in **Nov 2023** (older). — [Enki.AI (secondary)](https://enkiai.com/geothermal/google-nuclear-data-centers/)
- **Fusion (Google / CFS):** in **June 2025**, Google signed a PPA with **Commonwealth Fusion Systems** for 200 MW from the planned ARC plant in Virginia (early 2030s), and it is also a CFS investor. — [ANS Nuclear Newswire](https://www.ans.org/news/2025-10-28/article-7501/nextera-and-google-ink-a-deal-to-restart-duane-arnold/) (mention); an April 2026 fusion offtake claim from an aggregator is **unverified**
- **Market-based accounting criticism:** Google's claimed 12% cut in data-centre energy emissions relies on market-based accounting. Location-based emissions rose more sharply. — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-sustainability-operations/data-center-climate-emissions-special-report-google-amazon-meta-microsoft)
- **Gas lock-in:** Microsoft's post-report deals include **gas-powered data centres and a 20-year agreement with Chevron**. — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/07/02/google-amazon-emissions-report-data-centres/). Dominion's gas build and delayed coal retirements (section 2) are another example.
- **Efficiency-as-greenwashing critique:** a June 2026 arXiv paper, "Plateau That Never Comes", argues efficiency claims in data centres and AI can become greenwashing. — [arXiv 2606.04214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04214)

### Inferences
- With fewer buyers but larger volumes, the PPA market is now in effect a **hyperscaler market**. Their procurement strategy sets the pace for new US renewables, but it is increasingly paired with gas for speed and firmness.
- Redirecting existing nuclear output to data centres (Susquehanna, Vistra, Clinton) raises the classic **additionality** objection. Other grid users may backfill with fossil power. Restarts and uprates are the exceptions.

## 6. Efficiency counterpoints: PUE, chips, DeepSeek, Jevons

### Takeaway
Facility efficiency has plateaued. The industry-average PUE is **1.52** and the capacity-weighted figure is **1.36** (Uptime, 2026), with gains coming mainly from new large sites. Per-query energy is falling fast (Google: 0.24 Wh per median Gemini prompt). Total consumption keeps rising anyway, which is the textbook Jevons pattern.

### Cited Findings
- **Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2026** (fielded April–May 2026): average **PUE 1.52**. The capacity-weighted average is **1.36**, and that figure has improved faster since 2024 as newer, larger facilities come online. Legacy sites and cooling constraints slow overall progress. — [Uptime 2026 survey PDF](https://uptimeinstitute.com/uptime_assets/77a6191bef6c85cb4ac9bfb169ddc313cae517030b48709697b1f80bc10edc90-uptime-institute-global-data-center-survey-results-2026.pdf); [Uptime Intelligence](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/uptime-institute-global-data-center-survey-2026); [Uptime: PUE advantage of larger data centres](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/growing-pue-advantage-larger-data-centers)
- **Per-query efficiency:** Google reports a median Gemini text prompt uses **0.24 Wh** (Aug 2025 disclosure). — [Sopra Steria commentary](https://www.soprasteria.com/insights/details/industrial-ai-why-everything-now-happens-at-the-edge-ai-and-the-jevons-paradox-can-efficiency-backfire)
- **Jevons evidence:** an arXiv paper finds Google's data-centre electricity rose **3.7 times from 2016 to 2022**, versus **2.5 times** for global data-centre energy, despite efficiency gains. It describes a feedback loop in which efficiency improvements expand system size. — [arXiv 2411.11540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11540)
- IEA scenarios include a "High Efficiency" case alongside "Lift-Off" (2025 report). Its central case still roughly doubles demand by 2030. — [IEA Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai) (background); [IEA 2026 Key Questions](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3179f7f8-01f6-4dd6-bffa-c9f7b73f1dc9/KeyQuestionsonEnergyandAI.pdf)
- Critique: efficiency claims can function as greenwashing when absolute consumption grows. — [arXiv 2606.04214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04214)

### Inferences
- PUE has little left to give, since 1.36 capacity-weighted is near practical limits for air- and liquid-cooled builds. Future efficiency gains must come from chips and models. The DeepSeek episode (Jan 2025) showed model efficiency **lowers cost per token**, but the forecaster upgrades since then (EPRI +60%, LBNL upward, BNEF) imply it did not dent aggregate demand projections. This is consistent with Jevons.
- Per-prompt metrics such as 0.24 Wh are small, but agentic and reasoning workloads multiply tokens per task, so per-prompt efficiency is a poor proxy for total load.

- Chip-level efficiency data (for example Nvidia Blackwell/Rubin performance per watt versus Hopper) were not retrieved.
- IEA 2026 "High Efficiency" scenario numbers for 2030/2035 were not retrieved because the IEA PDF was blocked.
