## 1. EU data centre rules: EED Article 12 reporting, the 2026 rating scheme, minimum performance standards, CADA, the Taxonomy, and national rules (Germany, Ireland)

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the EU is moving from reporting to rating to (proposed) mandatory minimums. Two EED reporting cycles (2024, 2025) are done. On 21 Sept 2026 the Commission adopted a delegated act creating an A–G sustainability label for data centres of 500 kW or more, with first labels expected in 2027. It also opened a consultation on binding minimum performance standards, which closes 14 Dec 2026, with a legislative proposal expected in Q2 2027. Meanwhile the June 2026 Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) proposal aims to *triple* EU data centre capacity. The "rulebook" is therefore arriving at the same time as an industrial push to build faster.

### Cited Findings

**EED Article 12 reporting (the baseline)**
- The recast Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791 introduced mandatory public reporting for data centres with power demand above 500 kW (installed IT power) — [B&D Law summary](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/european-commission-advances-data-center-sustainability-ratings-and-consults-on-minimum-performance-standards/)
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364 sets the reporting scheme (KPIs: energy, PUE, water/WUE, renewable share, waste heat). Reports go into a European database. The first reporting deadline was 15 Sept 2024, then annually (15 May from 2025). — [EUR-Lex 2024/1364](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1364/oj). *Note: the dates come from background knowledge of the regulation. The official text URL was not fetched in this session, so verify.*
- **2025 Commission technical study (AIT, Borderstep, EY; July 2025) on the first (2024) cycle:** about 770 facilities reported, around 36% of the estimated 2,000+ eligible EU data centres. EU-wide weighted average renewable energy factor was 0.87. Many sites operate above the 1.3–1.4 PUE benchmark — [Publications Office PDF, July 2025 Assessment](https://op.europa.eu/o/opportal-service/download-handler?identifier=83be4c3e-5c79-11f0-a9d0-01aa75ed71a1&format=PDF&language=en&productionSystem=cellar); renewables headline: [IIR news "Renewables represent 87%…"](https://iifiir.org/en/news/renewables-represent-87-of-energy-consumption-in-eu-data-centres)
- **Commission report COM(2026) 500 (21 Sept 2026) on energy efficiency of EU data centres:** average 2024 PUE of 1.36. 681 data centres reported PUE values deemed reliable. About half of reporting data centres exceed a recommended PUE baseline of 1.5 and/or a WUE baseline of 0.4. Small facilities (500–1,000 kW) average a PUE of 1.64, and larger sites are more efficient — [COM(2026) 500 on BUILD UP](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/39-2026-48_Report%20on%20the%20energy%20efficiency%20of%20data%20centres%20in%20the%20EU.pdf) (seen via search snippet only; full text not fetched)
- A secondary write-up of the Commission dashboard for the 2025 reporting period reports an energy-weighted PUE of 1.27 across 773 sites in 11 countries. Germany has most sites but about a third of reported energy — [scienceshot.com (secondary)](https://scienceshot.com/post/eu-data-centre-pue-2025-dashboard-figures). *This conflicts in method (energy-weighted vs simple average) with the 1.36 figure. Do not compare directly.*
- Data centres consumed roughly 3% of EU electricity/energy in 2024 — [2eu.brussels](https://2eu.brussels/en/news/data-centres-already-use-3-of-eu-electricity-and-brussels-is-preparing-stricter-rules); [EU Law Analysis blog on CADA](http://eulawanalysis.blogspot.com/2026/07/the-effectiveness-of-cloud-and-ai.html)

**Rating scheme / label (adopted 21 Sept 2026)**
- On 21 Sept 2026 the Commission adopted a delegated regulation creating a common EU rating scheme for data centres. It gives information on energy and water use and on grid contributions such as waste-heat reuse. First labels for individual data centres are expected in 2027 — [European Commission news, 21 Sept 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/making-data-centres-energy-efficient-thanks-new-eu-rating-system-2026-09-21_en); [Commission press release IP/26/1667](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1667/IP_26_1667_EN.pdf)
- Act reference C(2026) 3472 final, dated Brussels 21.9.2026 — [Commission document C(2026) 3472](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b073ba9b-82a8-45cb-b8ba-78dad149e941_en?filename=C_2026_3472_1_EN_ACT_part1_v9.pdf). One secondary source cites a different number, "(EU) 2026/7000" — [B&D Law](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/european-commission-advances-data-center-sustainability-ratings-and-consults-on-minimum-performance-standards/). The reference is unresolved.
- Design: covers data centres of 500 kW installed IT power or more. Grades A–G on energy effectiveness and water efficiency, plus indicators on energy sourcing, grid flexibility and waste-heat reuse readiness. It supplements EED 2023/1791 and amends reporting regulation 2024/1364. It faces a 2-month Parliament/Council scrutiny period (extendable by 2), during which they can object but not amend — [Linklaters Sustainable Futures](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102o5fr/eu-commission-adopts-data-centre-sustainability-rating-scheme) (snippet)
- Labels are generated automatically from data already reported under 2024/1364. The Commission says this adds no administrative burden — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-proposes-mandatory-sustainability-label-for-data-centres)
- Entry into force possibly by end-2026 — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/eu-fuhrt-gemeinsames-bewertungssystem-und-nachhaltigkeitskennzeichnung-fur-rechenzentren-ein). An earlier July 2026 draft had set application from 15 Aug 2027, and that date may have changed — [Linklaters earlier post](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102ne37/sustainability-labelling-for-european-data-centres)
- The ratings are meant to feed into the proposed CADA and the EU Taxonomy climate delegated acts — [Freshfields "From reporting to rating"](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/sustainability/from-reporting-to-rating-the-eus-next-steps-for-data-centres-102o345)

**Minimum performance standards (MPS), the "Data Centre Energy Efficiency Package"**
- On the same day (21 Sept 2026) the Commission opened a call for evidence and public consultation on minimum performance standards for new or retrofitted data centres, covering energy efficiency, water use, waste heat reuse, grid services and other criteria, to "phase out the worst-performing technologies and practices". It closes 14 Dec 2026 — [B&D Law](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/european-commission-advances-data-center-sustainability-ratings-and-consults-on-minimum-performance-standards/); [regulatory-compliance.eu, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.regulatory-compliance.eu/2026/10/14/eu-data-centres-in-europe-public-consultation-on-minimum-performance-standards/)
- A legislative proposal on MPS is expected in Q2 2027 — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/sustainability/from-reporting-to-rating-the-eus-next-steps-for-data-centres-102o345)
- A DG ENER preparatory study on MPS began end-January 2026 and runs about 24 months — [EC Energy, MPS preparatory study page](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/resources/preparatory-studies/minimum-performance-standards-eu-data-centres_es)
- Draft PUE targets floated in secondary sources: operational PUE below 1.5 for existing sites by 2030, design PUE below 1.3 for new builds — [datalok.io (secondary)](https://www.datalok.io/en/blog/data-center-energy-label-eu-rating.html). *Not confirmed in an official text.*

**Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA), proposal of 3 June 2026**
- Proposed 3 June 2026 within a Tech Sovereignty Package (with Chips Act 2.0). Procedure 2026/0138(COD). Still a proposal as of Oct 2026 — [EP Legislative Observatory summary](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-document-summary/pdf?id=1903654); [Covington Inside Global Tech, 11 June 2026](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/06/11/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act-in-depth/)
- Aim: at least triple EU data centre capacity within 5–7 years. Each Member State designates at least one "data centre acceleration zone" with streamlined permitting, aggregated baseline permits and a maximum 12-month permit timeline. The Commission can designate "strategic projects" with preferential access to EU funding — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/data-centres-and-energy-consumption-evolving-eu-regulatory-landscape-and-outlook-2026); [Schjødt](https://schjodt.com/news/data-centers-the-cloud-and-ai-development-act)
- Critics point to energy, water and planning constraints. The proposal pairs acceleration with efficiency, cooling and power-management requirements — [EU Law Analysis, July 2026](http://eulawanalysis.blogspot.com/2026/07/the-effectiveness-of-cloud-and-ai.html)

**EU Taxonomy**
- Data centres are activity 8.1 "Data processing, hosting and related activities" in the Climate Delegated Act. Substantial-contribution criteria require implementing the relevant practices of the European Code of Conduct on Data Centre Energy Efficiency (with reference to alternative best practices) — [JRC Assessment Framework for Data Centres](https://e3p.jrc.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-09/jrc_assessment_framework_final_v2.pdf); [Commission draft notice on Taxonomy delegated acts](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b799db63-a034-4023-9f77-3e9a69be4de9_en?filename=241129-draft-commission-notice-eu-taxonomy-delegated-acts_en.pdf)
- On 17 March 2026 the Commission published draft revisions of the Climate and Environmental Delegated Acts (consultation to 14 April 2026) with widespread TSC revisions — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ifrg/2026/EU-taxonomy-TSC-amendments.html); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/esg/2026-posts/eu-taxonomy-overhaul-commission-publishes-draft-revisions-to-climate-and-environmental-delegated-acts); [Nordea](https://www.nordea.com/en/news/eu-taxonomy-flash-update-on-the-2026-review-and-changes-to-the-climate-delegated-act)

**Germany: Energieeffizienzgesetz (EnEfG, 2023) and the pending 2026 amendment**
- Current law (EnEfG 2023) requires 100% renewable electricity for covered data centres from 1 Jan 2027. An amendment before the Bundestag would push this to 2030 — [Environment+Energy Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/german-data-center-renewable-rule-may-shift-to-2030,142880). Conflicting: another source says renewable-accounting rules "remain unchanged from 2027" — [Taylor Wessing, Apr 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/enefg-novelle-ueberblick)
- The draft amendment relaxes PUE limits for existing data centres from 1.5 to 1.6 (from 1 July 2027) and from 1.3 to 1.4 (from 1 July 2030) — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/enefg-novelle-ueberblick); [Orrick, Apr 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/German-Energy-Efficiency-Act-Draft-Amendment-What-it-means-for-Companies-and-Data-Centres)
- For new data centres (operating from 1 July 2026), sources conflict. One says the 1.2 target is raised to 1.3. Another says the cabinet kept 1.2 but allowed four years instead of two to reach it — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/novelle-des-energieeffizienzgesetzes-und-des-gesetzes-%C3%BCber-energiedienstleistungen-%E2%80%93-ein-%C3%BCberblick); [scienceshot hearing summary](https://scienceshot.com/post/enefg-hearing-data-centres-expert-statements). **[OLDER, under current law]:** PUE of 1.2 or below for new builds and a 10% waste-heat reuse requirement for sites operating from 1 July 2026 — [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/2025/11/07/germany-enefg-pue-1-2-waste-heat-data-centers-july-2026/)
- Legislative status: first Bundestag reading reported 24 Sept 2026, hearing 5 Oct 2026. If not passed before 1 Jan 2027, the 2027 renewable mandate takes effect at least temporarily — [scienceshot](https://scienceshot.com/post/enefg-hearing-data-centres-expert-statements); [Environment+Energy Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/german-data-center-renewable-rule-may-shift-to-2030,142880)

**Ireland: CRU data centre connection policy (Dec 2025)**
- The CRU published its final Large Energy User connection policy for data centres in December 2025 (dated 12 Dec 2025 by one source). Data centres above 10 MVA MIC must provide new onsite or proximate dispatchable generation or storage that participates in the SEM — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/cru-publishes-long-awaited-final-policy-on-data-centre-connections/); [CRU news](https://www.cru.ie/about-us/news/new-electricity-connection-policy-for-data-centre/)
- Developers must source 80% of electricity from new Irish renewables within six years — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/ireland/energy-and-natural-resources/cru-publishes-long-awaited-final-policy-on-data-centre-connections); [gridreadiness.com](https://gridreadiness.com/grid-data-center-ireland)
- Critique (Prof. Hannah Daly, UCC): in practice the most available option is on-site gas generation, and the 80% test relies on annual, not temporal, matching — [hannahdaly.ie, 12 Dec 2025](https://hannahdaly.ie/2025-12-12-CRU-data-centre-policy/)

### Inferences
- The EU's sequence (report 2024–25, rate 2027, set minimums from about 2028+) is the clearest example globally of the "rulebook coming". Because the label is auto-generated from reported data, non-reporting becomes visible, and the roughly 36% first-year coverage suggests compliance gaps that the label may expose.
- There is a policy tension: CADA (triple capacity, 12-month permits) versus MPS and labels, and Germany is loosening EnEfG thresholds in 2026. That supports a narrative that efficiency rules are being softened at the margin even as disclosure hardens.
- Ireland's rule shows "additionality" and "dispatchability" requirements can push data centres toward on-site gas. That links regulation directly to the greenwashing piece (B).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch COM(2026) 500 or C(2026) 3472 full texts (network-blocked), so exact label thresholds, application date and Official Journal publication are unverified.
- No confirmed figure on how many data centres reported in the second (May 2025) cycle from an official source. Only a secondary dashboard figure (773) was found.
- The 2026 Taxonomy drafts' specific changes to activity 8.1 were not found.
- Final outcome of the German EnEfG amendment (Bundestag vote) not found as of 30 Oct 2026.

## 2. EU corporate disclosure and claims rules: CSRD/ESRS after Omnibus I, the Green Claims Directive, and the Empowering Consumers Directive (ECGT) offset-claim ban

### Takeaway
Omnibus I (in force March 2026) cut CSRD scope by about 80%, to companies with more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover, from FY2027. Big Tech EU entities stay in scope, but many suppliers drop out. The Green Claims Directive has been blocked since June 2025 and is not law. However, the Empowering Consumers Directive (EU) 2024/825 has applied since **27 Sept 2026**. It bans product-level "climate neutral / carbon neutral / CO2 neutral" claims based on offsetting, regardless of credit quality, and requires net-zero-type future claims to be backed by a detailed, verifiable implementation plan.

### Cited Findings
**CSRD / Omnibus I**
- Council adopted the final Omnibus I text on 24 Feb 2026. Published in the OJ on 26 Feb 2026, in force 18 March 2026 (one source says 19 March) — [Regulation Tomorrow, Feb 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/02/omnibus-i-csrd-and-cs3d-simplification-council-of-european-union-adopts-final-text-t-simplify-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/eu-council-approves-omnibus-i-directive)
- New CSRD thresholds: more than 1,000 employees AND more than €450m net turnover (both required). Non-EU groups: a separate test including above €200m EU turnover for the subsidiary or branch. New thresholds apply from FY2027. Wave-1 companies that fall out of scope are exempt for FY2025 and FY2026, subject to transposition — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus); [dcycle](https://dcycle.io/blog/csrd-reporting-timeline-post-omnibus/)
- Scope reduced by about 80% — [financialregulations.eu (secondary)](https://financialregulations.eu/blog/eu-omnibus-csrd-simplification-2026)
- CSDDD: more than 5,000 employees and more than €1.5bn turnover, application deferred to July 2029 — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus)
- Value-chain cap: companies with fewer than 1,000 employees can refuse information requests beyond the voluntary SME standard — [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/02/omnibus-i-csrd-and-cs3d-simplification-council-of-european-union-adopts-final-text-t-simplify-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/)
- *Conflict flag:* one blog states the thresholds as 1,000+ employees with €50m turnover / €25m assets. This appears to be an error — [emistra.io](https://www.emistra.io/blog/csrd-omnibus-directive-2026-new-thresholds)

**Green Claims Directive (COM(2023) 166)**
- Status "Blocked". The final trilogue was cancelled in June 2025 after the Commission signalled intent to withdraw. Sources conflict on whether it was formally withdrawn, and the Commission's 2026 work programme reportedly still lists it as pending — [regreener.earth](https://www.regreener.earth/blog/the-eu-green-claims-directive:-a-short-summary); [getbindu](https://getbindu.com/blog/green-claims-directive); [ecoclaim.eu](https://www.ecoclaim.eu/green-claims-directive). *Recommend wording: "shelved/blocked since June 2025, not law".*

**Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EU) 2024/825 (EmpCo/ECGT)**
- Applies from 27 Sept 2026. Transposition deadline was 27 March 2026, and not all Member States transposed on time — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-new-rules-on-green-claims-apply-from-27-september-2026)
- Bans product-level claims that a product has neutral, reduced or positive GHG impact based on offsetting ("climate neutral", "CO2 neutral", "carbon neutral", "climate positive"), regardless of credit quality. Buying credits is not prohibited, and corporate-level communication is not caught by this specific blacklist item — [Senken](https://www.senken.io/academy/regulation-and-standards/empowering-consumer-directive-empco); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-new-rules-on-green-claims-apply-from-27-september-2026)
- Future environmental performance claims (e.g. net zero by 2030) may be misleading unless supported by a detailed, realistic implementation plan with verifiable targets and independent monitoring. Generic claims ("eco-friendly", "climate-friendly") are restricted unless recognised excellent environmental performance can be shown — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-new-rules-on-green-claims-apply-from-27-september-2026)
- Penalty regimes are set nationally. Some sources cite fines of at least 4% of annual turnover for widespread infringements (via the Consumer Protection Cooperation framework) — [ecoclaim.eu (secondary)](https://www.ecoclaim.eu/blog/is-greenwashing-still-illegal-eu-2026)
- Apple said it would stop using the carbon-neutral label for Apple Watch to comply with the upcoming EU rules — [ESG Dive, Aug 2025](https://www.esgdive.com/news/carbon-neutral-apple-watch-not-carbon-neutral-says-german-court/758993/)

### Inferences
- The ECGT, not the Green Claims Directive, is now the operative EU anti-greenwashing law. For tech, the immediate hit is on *product* claims (devices, "carbon neutral cloud region" type marketing to consumers). Corporate "net zero by 2030" pledges are exposed via the future-claims clause if no credible plan exists.
- The Omnibus reduces Scope 3 data flowing from smaller suppliers to large tech firms, which may make value-chain targets harder to evidence.

### Gaps
- No confirmed list of which Member States had transposed ECGT by Sept 2026.
- No enforcement action under ECGT against a tech firm found (it has only applied for about 5 weeks).

## 3. United States: SEC rule, California SB 253/261, state data centre bills, large-load tariffs (Texas SB 6, AEP Ohio), federal EO 14318, FERC

### Takeaway
Federal climate disclosure is dead in practice. The SEC stopped defending its 2024 rule in March 2025 and proposed rescission on 29 May 2026. The action is in the states: California SB 253 first Scope 1–2 reports are due **10 Nov 2026**, while SB 261 is enjoined. Data centre regulation in the US is mostly about **grid cost allocation and reliability**, not efficiency: Texas SB 6, the AEP Ohio 85% minimum-demand tariff, FERC show-cause orders, and a wave of 2026 state energy and water disclosure laws (California, New Jersey, Nebraska, Virginia). Federal policy (EO 14318) actively accelerates data centre build-out on federal land.

### Cited Findings
**SEC**
- **[SUPERSEDED/ABANDONED]** The SEC voted in March 2025 to end its defence of the March 2024 climate disclosure rules. The Eighth Circuit held the case in abeyance (24 Apr 2025), then indefinitely (12 Sept 2025). The SEC notified the court on 7 May 2026 it would propose rescission. On 21 May 2026 a motion to lift abeyance was denied. The rescission proposal was issued 29 May 2026. The rule never took effect — [Mintz, Mar 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2026-03-17-eighth-circuit-holds-sec-climate-rule-litigation-abeyance); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/sec-proposes-rescission-of-climate-related-disclosure-rules/); [TheCorporateCounsel.net, June 2026](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2026/06/sec-proposes-to-rescind-its-controversial-climate-related-disclosure-rules.html); [Harvard EELP tracker](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/financial-regulation-climate-change-and-climate-related-risk-disclosure/)

**California SB 253 (GHG emissions) / SB 261 (climate risk)**
- CARB Board approved the initial regulation on 26 Feb 2026. OAL approved it and filed it with the Secretary of State on 12 Oct 2026, effective on filing — [AOPL blog (secondary)](https://aopl.tech/blog/california-sb-253-reporting-scope-1-and-2-data-quality-before-limited-assurance); [Sullivan & Cromwell, Mar 2026](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/March/California-Air-Resources-Board-Approves-Initial-Regulations-SB-253-SB-261); [Morgan Lewis, Oct 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/10/californias-climate-disclosure-regulations-receive-final-approval-ahead-of-november-reporting-deadline)
- First SB 253 deadline: **10 Nov 2026**, for Scope 1 and 2 only (moved from 10 Aug after the Board withdrew and clarified the regulation in June 2026). No Scope 3 in 2026. Companies may use data they already had or were collecting as of 5 Dec 2024 for the first report — [Greenberg Traurig, Oct 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/10/california-sb-253-carb-sets-nov-10-2026-deadline-for-scope-1-and-scope-2-emissions-reports); [Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/carbs-final-climate-disclosure-rule-sets-sb-253-deadline-leaves-sb-261-paused/)

CARB previewed limited assurance on Scope 1–2 from 2027 — [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/california-climate-disclosure-laws-carb-finalizes-its-initial-rulemaking-resets-the-2026-deadline-and-previews-the-2027-framework)
- Penalties up to $500,000 per reporting year. CARB says it won't penalise good-faith first reports. Scope 3 penalties (2027–2030) apply only for non-filing — [taxbakers (secondary)](https://taxbakers.com/sustainability/sb-253-penalties-fees/); [Cooley, Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-26-californias-sb-253-and-sb-261-developments-and-litigation)
- First CARB fee notices expected around 10 Dec 2026 — [Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/carbs-final-climate-disclosure-rule-sets-sb-253-deadline-leaves-sb-261-paused/)
- **SB 261:** Ninth Circuit injunction pending appeal granted 18 Nov 2025 (SB 261 only, SB 253 denied). Oral argument 9 Jan 2026. Merits decision still pending as of the latest sources (Aug 2026). CARB will set a new SB 261 date after the appeal — [MoFo, Nov 2025](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-ninth-circuit-enjoins-enforcement-of-california-climate-disclosure-law-sb-261); [Pillsbury on oral argument](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/ninth-circuit-challenge-california-climate-disclosures.html); [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/california-climate-disclosure-laws-carb-finalizes-its-initial-rulemaking-resets-the-2026-deadline-and-previews-the-2027-framework)

**State data centre energy/water disclosure (2026 sessions)**
- **California:** Newsom signed a seven-bill data centre package in Sept 2026. AB 2619 requires annual water-use reporting (total, direct, cooling type) at business-licence renewal. ### Inferences

## 4. Asia-Pacific: Singapore, China, Japan, India (data centre policy, CCTS), Australia (mandatory climate disclosure)

### Takeaway
Asia-Pacific regulators use **capacity allocation and efficiency benchmarks** rather than disclosure. Singapore's DC-CFA2 requires PUE of 1.25 or below and at least 50% green power. China mandates PUE of 1.25 or below for new large sites (1.2 in hub nodes) and 80% green power in national hubs. Japan's Energy Conservation Act data centre benchmark (PUE 1.4 by FY2030) applies from April 2026. India still has no notified national data centre policy (drafting restarted Oct 2026). The CCTS compliance market covers heavy industry, not data centres. Australia's mandatory climate reporting (AASB S2) is live, with the first Group 1 reports lodged in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Singapore**
- Green Data Centre Roadmap aspiration: all DCs at PUE 1.3 or better at 100% IT load over about 10 years, and at least 300 MW of additional capacity near-term. It is an aspiration, not a statute — [AKCP, July 2026](https://www.akcp.com/2026/07/23/singapore-data-center-pue-roadmap/); [Morgan Lewis, Mar 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/singapore-announces-data-center-capacity-allocation-call)
- DC-CFA2 (launched about Dec 2025 / early 2026, closed 31 March 2026): at least 200 MW. Requires PUE of 1.25 or below at full IT load, BCA-IMDA Green Mark for Data Centres 2024 Platinum, compliance with SS 715:2025, and at least 50% power from eligible green pathways (e.g. low-carbon ammonia, hydrogen, biomethane, imports) — [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/singapore-s-second-data-centre-call-for-application-from-pilot-to-power-play); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/singapore-announces-data-center-capacity-allocation-call)
- One source reports that on 21 Aug 2026 four operators (Digital Realty, Equinix, Keppel DC, STT GDC) each received 50 MW at a Jurong Island low-carbon park. Another source says awards are pending — [introl.com (secondary)](https://introl.com/blog/singapore-green-data-center-mandate-dc-cfa2-2026). *Unverified; check EDB/IMDA.*
- A proposed bill reportedly would require an IMDA licence and facility PUE standards for operators of 3 MW or more critical IT load. Status unverified — [arusgrid.com (secondary)](https://arusgrid.com/policy/singapore)

**China**
- "East Data, West Computing" launched Feb 2022: 8 national computing hubs, 10 data centre clusters — [be-cause.earth](https://www.be-cause.earth/blog/china-cloud-data-centres-east-data-west-computing); [GIZ policy briefing 2022](https://www.plattform-i40.de/IP/Redaktion/EN/Downloads/Publikation/China/GIZ_Policy-Briefing_202208.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1)

**Japan**
- Under the Energy Conservation Act, data centre operators using 1,500 kL crude-oil-equivalent or more per year (about 16,000 MWh) are designated. **Australia**
- Mandatory climate reporting (Corporations Act / AASB S2). Group 1 started with FYs beginning on or after 1 Jan 2025, Group 2 from 1 July 2026, Group 3 from 1 July 2027. Scope 3 from the second reporting year. Two mandatory scenarios (1.5°C and well above 2°C). ### Inferences
- APAC's model ties *permission to build* to efficiency and green power (Singapore allocation, Chinese hub quotas, Japanese benchmarks). Arguably this has more teeth than EU or US disclosure, but it relies on PUE, which does not capture carbon intensity of supply or embodied emissions.
- For Big Tech in Australia, AASB S2 Scope 3 disclosure plus ASIC's demonstrated greenwashing litigation creates real legal exposure for net-zero claims.

## 6. Offset-based claims under legal and regulatory attack: lawsuits and advertising regulators

### Takeaway
Courts are split. A US federal judge dismissed the Apple Watch "carbon neutral" class action (Feb 2026, for lack of a factual basis), while a German court (Frankfurt Regional Court, Aug 2025) banned Apple's "CO2-neutral" Watch ads because offset leases didn't last long enough. Germany's BGH Katjes ruling (June 2024) set the "explain it in the ad" standard, and EU law now bans offset-based product neutrality claims outright from Sept 2026. The UK CMA has had direct fining powers (up to 10% of global turnover) since April 2025 and issued supply-chain green-claims guidance in Jan 2026. Australia's ASIC and ACCC have a string of multi-million-dollar greenwashing penalties.

### Cited Findings
- **US, Dib v. Apple (N.D. Cal.):** Judge Noël Wise granted Apple's motion to dismiss on 20 Feb 2026 (Apple Watch Series 9 "carbon neutral"). Plaintiffs relied on their own satellite-imagery analysis without expert or agency support. Leave to amend by 13 March 2026. EDF filed an amicus *supporting* Apple — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/apple-prevails-us-greenwashing-suit-carbon-neutral-claims/); [EDF](https://www.edf.org/media/apple-watch-carbon-neutral-court-ruling-sets-guardrails-greenwashing-litigation); [Green Building Law Update, Mar 2026](https://www.greenbuildinglawupdate.com/2026/03/articles/greenwashing/a-bellwether-greenwashing-ruling-apple-watch-is-carbon-neutral-even-if-it-is-not/). The original complaint was filed in 2025 — [Business & Human Rights Centre](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/etats-unis-7-acheteurs-poursuivent-apple-en-justice-pour-d%C3%A9clarations-trompeuses-sur-la-neutralit%C3%A9-carbone-de-ses-montres/)
- **Germany, DUH v Apple (LG Frankfurt, 26 Aug 2025):** injunction against advertising Apple Watch models as a "CO2-neutral product". Offsets from Paraguayan eucalyptus plantations had leases running only to about 2029, while consumers expect compensation to last until about 2050. The "Carbon Neutral" logo itself was allowed. Fines up to €250,000 per violation. Appealable — [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/carbon-neutral-apple-watch-not-carbon-neutral-says-german-court/758993/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/08/26/apple-watch-carbon-neutral-germany/)
- **Germany, BGH Katjes (I ZR 98/23, 27 June 2024):** an ad using "klimaneutral" is misleading unless it explains in the ad itself whether neutrality comes from reduction or offsetting. A QR code or link is insufficient, and reduction and offsetting are not equivalent — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/hintergruende/h/bgh-izr9823-werbung-klimaneutral-katjes-irrefuehrung); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6b9901fd-11f2-4db7-bf20-bda7a0a2d4a3)
- **UK:** the DMCC Act 2024 lets the CMA directly fine up to 10% of global turnover (or £300k if higher) for consumer-law breaches, including misleading green claims, in force since 6 April 2025 (one source says Jan 2027, likely an error). On 22 Jan 2026 the CMA published guidance making businesses responsible for supplier claims they repeat. The 2021 Green Claims Code still applies. The ASA (CAP Code) has no fining power — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/green-police-expanding-cmas-enforcement-powers-on-greenwashing-in-the-uk/); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/supply-chain-green-claims-uk-cma-signals-enforcement-escalation); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/consumer-protection-in-the-spotlight-cma-flexes-its-new-enforcement-powers)
- **Australia:** see section 4 (ASIC: Mercer $11.3m, Vanguard $12.9m, Active Super $10.5m; ACCC: Clorox $8.25m).
- **France / Netherlands:** a Paris court reportedly ordered TotalEnergies to remove carbon-neutrality marketing (Oct 2025), and an Amsterdam court found KLM "Fly Responsibly" ads misleading (2024) — [ecoclaim.eu tracker (secondary; verify)](https://www.ecoclaim.eu/blog/eu-greenwashing-fines-tracker-2026)

### Inferences
- US courts demand evidentiary rigour from plaintiffs, so consumer class actions are a weak tool. The EU now bypasses this with a per se ban. For tech companies, EU (and UK CMA) exposure is far higher than US exposure.
- Apple's dropping of the Watch label suggests the EU ban is already changing tech marketing practice before any enforcement.

### Gaps
- Whether Dib plaintiffs refiled and whether Apple appealed the Frankfurt judgment: not found.
- No UK ASA or Dutch ACM ruling specifically against a tech company's offset or "carbon neutral" claim was found in this session.
- No Carbon Market Watch or ClientEarth tech-specific complaint was found (searches returned none).

## 7. "AI for climate" claims vs evidence

### Takeaway
The widely repeated claim that AI could cut 5–10% of global emissions by 2030 traces to a 2021 BCG blog extrapolating from client work. It was repeated in a Google-commissioned 2023 BCG report and is criticised as undocumented. A Feb 2026 report found no case of generative AI producing material, verifiable emission cuts. The IEA's more modest estimate is used as the counterweight.

### Cited Findings
- The 5–10% figure originated in a 2021 BCG blog post extrapolated from client experience and was repeated in a 2023 BCG report commissioned by Google. Extrapolating from projects to global emissions "lacks any scientific grounding" — [Luccioni et al., "Misinformation by Omission", arXiv 2506.15572 (June 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15572v1); [ESG News on Google/BCG](https://esgnews.com/google-and-bcg-pioneering-ai-powered-climate-solutions/)
- Feb 2026 report "The AI Climate Hoax" (published on beyondfossilfuels.org; authorship as reported): "The evidence for massive climate benefits of AI is weak, whilst the evidence of substantial harm is strong." It found no example where generative AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) led to "material, verifiable and substantial" emission reductions — [Beyond Fossil Fuels report PDF](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AI-for-climate-claims-Report_FEB-2026_FINAL-2-16.pdf); [Euronews, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/02/17/ai-greenwashing-most-of-big-techs-ai-climate-promises-fall-flat-study-finds)
- The IEA has suggested AI could cut emissions by up to about 5% by 2035 via energy-sector applications (as reported by Euronews) — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/02/17/ai-greenwashing-most-of-big-techs-ai-climate-promises-fall-flat-study-finds)
- Commentary: Google kept citing the claim in the same report showing a 48% emissions rise — [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/business/ai-wrecked-big-techs-climate-goals-and-now-theyve-got-to-come-up-with-a/)
- Former Microsoft staff (Enabled Emissions) argue that AI sold to oil and gas for extraction is a major uncounted climate impact — [CSO Futures](https://www.csofutures.com/news/why-are-former-microsoft-employees-raising-the-alarm-about-ai-emissions/)

### Inferences
- Under ECGT, an unsubstantiated "AI will cut X% of emissions" consumer-facing claim could be challenged as a misleading generic or future claim. This is a new greenwashing frontier beyond offsets.

### Gaps
- The IEA "Energy and AI" (2025) primary estimate was not fetched. Verify the exact framing before use.

## 8. Investors, ratings and standards: NewClimate CCRM, SBTi, CDP, GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision

### Takeaway
No Big Tech firm receives a "reasonable" or "high" integrity rating from NewClimate (2025 tech CCRM). Apple was the only one at "moderate". SBTi status is uneven: Apple, Microsoft and Google have targets set, Amazon only "committed", and Google lacks a validated net-zero target (per an unofficial tracker). The biggest structural fight is the GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision on hourly matching and deliverability, which would undercut REC-based "100% renewable" claims. 82% of corporate respondents opposed it, and the ISB has proposed a phased approach with a further consultation in Q2 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **NewClimate CCRM 2025 (tech, June 2025):** assessed Amazon, Apple, Google, Meta and Microsoft (the five largest by 2023 revenue).
