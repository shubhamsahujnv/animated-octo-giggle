## 1. Hyperscaler water consumption, water-positive pledges and disclosure

### Takeaway
Every hyperscaler that discloses water use reports rising absolute use, while each also highlights better efficiency (WUE) and replenishment progress. Google's 2025 consumption was about 10.9 billion gallons, up 34% year on year. Amazon disclosed a fleet-wide data-centre withdrawal total for the first time, about 2.5 billion gallons in 2025. Disclosure is still mostly not site-level, which is the main investor and community complaint.

### Cited Findings
**Google (2026 Environmental Report, covering calendar 2025; published about late June 2026)**
- Water consumption rose 34% to about 10.9 billion gallons in 2025. That is more than double 2021 levels, and data centres drove most of the increase — [Axios, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/30/google-ai-boom-emissions-power-use) (figure taken from the search summary; full text could not be fetched); the same figure is repeated by [Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-data-center-water-usage-statistics/), an aggregator.
- Google reportedly replenished about 7.7 billion gallons (about 78% of consumption) through 165 stewardship projects in 97 watersheds — [Axis Intelligence (aggregator)](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-data-center-water-usage-statistics/). Caution: aggregators also give 7.7bn gallons as a 2024 number and give various withdrawal totals, so verify against Google's own report.
- Site example, possibly from an older report: Council Bluffs, Iowa is cited at about 3.9 million gallons per day withdrawn and 2.8 million per day consumed — [Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-data-center-water-usage-statistics/).

**Microsoft (FY2025, July 2024 to June 2025; 2026 Environmental Sustainability Report)**
- The 2026 report says Microsoft is working "in some cases" toward near-zero water consumption, using recycled and reclaimed water and next-generation designs "that can eliminate most water use for cooling". The water-positive-by-2030 goal is reaffirmed — [Microsoft 2026 ESR PDF](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Sustainability-Report-PDF.pdf).
- About 90% of Microsoft's owned fleet in 2025 operated with low- to zero-water cooling systems, according to Microsoft's blog of 24 Jun 2026 — [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/06/24/inside-microsofts-two-decade-push-to-cut-water-intensity-while-scaling-for-growth/) (from the search summary; fetch blocked).
- Microsoft reports cutting data-centre water-use intensity by 25% as of 2025, against a target of 40% by 2030 — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/microsoft-cuts-datacentre-water-use-by-25-in-fy25).
- In FY25 Microsoft reportedly replenished more water than it withdrew for the first time, with more than about 14 million m³ replenished — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/microsoft-cuts-datacentre-water-use-by-25-in-fy25). One trade outlet frames this as reaching water-positive five years early — [Insurance Journal/Reuters, Apr 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/04/08/864987.htm). Caution: "replenished more than withdrawn" in one year is not the same as the formal 2030 water-positive accounting.
- Microsoft has used direct air cooling with evaporative assist since 2008; it adds water only above about 29.4°C (85°F) — [Sustainability Magazine](https://sustainabilitymag.com/news/how-microsoft-cuts-water-use-while-scaling-data-centres).
- I found no reliable FY25 absolute withdrawal or consumption total. The roughly 1.7bn gallon figure in circulation is from an earlier year — [Insurance Journal/Reuters](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/04/08/864987.htm).

**Amazon/AWS (2025 Sustainability Report, published mid-2026)**
- First fleet-wide total: data centres withdrew about 2.5 billion gallons (about 9.4 to 9.5 billion litres) in 2025. Earlier reports gave only WUE ratios — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amazon-discloses-25b-gallons-of-data-center-water-use-in-2025); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-achieves-75-of-goal-to-be-water-positive-in-data-centers-by-2030/).
- Global average WUE was 0.12 L/kWh in 2025, a 52% improvement since 2021 — [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-achieves-75-of-goal-to-be-water-positive-in-data-centers-by-2030/).
- AWS was 75% of the way to water positive by 2030 in 2025, up from 53% in 2024 — [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-achieves-75-of-goal-to-be-water-positive-in-data-centers-by-2030/); [Mexico Business News (the 53% figure)](https://mexicobusiness.news/ecommerce/news/aws-reaches-53-water-positive-goal).

**Meta (latest found is the 2025 Environmental Data Index, covering 2024; older data)**
- 2024 total withdrawal was 5,637 megalitres and total consumption 3,123 ML. Data centres accounted for 4,145 ML withdrawn and 2,974 ML consumed. Data-centre WUE was 0.19 L/kWh — [Meta 2025 Environmental Data Index](https://sustainability.atmeta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Meta_2025-Environmental-Data-Index.pdf).
- In 2024 Meta withdrew 1,704 ML, and consumed 748 ML, in areas of high or extremely high baseline water stress — [Meta 2025 Environmental Data Index](https://sustainability.atmeta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Meta_2025-Environmental-Data-Index.pdf).
- Reuters reported total use up 51%, from 3,726 ML in 2020 to 5,637 ML in 2024 — [Insurance Journal/Reuters, Apr 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/04/08/864987.htm).
- Meta's data-centre electricity use was 14,975 GWh, and use at leased data centres nearly doubled — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/meta-data-center-electricity-consumption-hits-14975gwh-leased-data-center-use-nearly-doubles/).

**Investor pressure**
- In April 2026 investors pressed Amazon, Microsoft and Google for site-level water and power data for US data centres — [Insurance Journal/Reuters, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/04/08/864987.htm); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/we-havent-seen-them-disclosing-enough-about-their-water-consumption-and-the-impact-on-the-local-community-amazon-microsoft-google-and-more-pressed-on-data-center-water-and-power-use).

### Inferences
- The industry's water story has two sides. Intensity (L/kWh) is falling fast while absolute volumes rise, because AI load growth outpaces the efficiency gains. Google's +34% in one year is the clearest data point.
- Water-positive claims rely on replenishment projects that may sit in different watersheds from the data centres drawing the water. That offers little comfort to a specific aquifer such as Santiago, Tucson or Aragon. The site-level disclosure push is aimed at exactly this gap.
- The company figures use different metrics (withdrawal vs consumption), units (gallons vs ML), scopes (owned vs leased) and periods (calendar vs fiscal year), so they should not be ranked side by side without caveats.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025 water figure from Meta was found; its 2026 report was not located.
- No verified FY25 absolute withdrawal or consumption figure for Microsoft was found.
- Google's 2026 report PDF was not checked directly; the 10.9bn gallon and 78% replenishment figures come via Axios and aggregators.

## 2. AI per-query water estimates, indirect water and the controversy

### Takeaway
Per-prompt water estimates range from about 0.26 mL (Google, on-site cooling only, median Gemini text prompt) to roughly 10 to 50 mL (UC Riverside, including water used at power plants). The dispute is mostly about scope: whether indirect water from electricity generation counts. US national data shows the indirect share is about 12 times the direct cooling share.

### Cited Findings
- The UC Riverside study "Making AI Less Thirsty" (Shaolei Ren et al., 2023; older data) is widely misquoted as "500 mL per ChatGPT query". It actually estimates about 500 mL for every 10 to 50 responses, depending on location and timing, or roughly 10 to 50 mL per response — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/thirsty-generative-ai-boom-poses-growing-problem-big-tech-rcna128301); [PromptsLove fact-check](https://promptslove.com/blog/how-much-water-does-ai-use/).
- Google's August 2025 technical paper put a median Gemini text prompt at 0.26 mL of water (about "five drops"), along with about 0.24 Wh of energy — [The Register, 22 Aug 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/22/googles_gemini_water/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/theyre-just-hiding-the-critical-information-google-says-its-gemini-ai-sips-a-mere-five-drops-of-water-per-text-prompt-but-experts-disagree-with-its-findings/).
- Ren's criticism: Google compared its on-site-only median with his averages, which include indirect water, so it is an apples-to-oranges comparison. Ren said Google was "just hiding the critical information" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/theyre-just-hiding-the-critical-information-google-says-its-gemini-ai-sips-a-mere-five-drops-of-water-per-text-prompt-but-experts-disagree-with-its-findings/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/22/googles_gemini_water/).
- Counter-criticism: commentator Andy Masley agrees Google's comparison was misleading but argues Ren's original numbers were themselves overstated. This is opinion — [Andy Masley blog](https://blog.andymasley.com/p/an-example-of-what-i-consider-a-misleading).
- One secondary source says Ren later revised his GPT-4 estimate to about 15 mL per query (about 5 mL on-site). Unverified — [TechJournal](https://techjournal.org/does-ai-use-water-data-centers).
- US national baseline from LBNL's 2024 report (2023 data; older): about 17 billion gallons of direct cooling water in 2023, versus about 211 billion gallons of indirect water through electricity generation. Direct use could double or quadruple by 2028 — [The Conversation via AIhub, Sep 2025](https://aihub.org/2025/09/24/data-centers-consume-massive-amounts-of-water-companies-rarely-tell-the-public-exactly-how-much); [CivilBeat](https://www.civilbeat.org/2025/08/data-centers-consume-massive-amounts-of-water-companies-rarely-say-exactly-how-much/).
- An IEA estimate puts about 60% of data-centre water use as indirect, from power generation — [PC Gamer, citing the IEA](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/theyre-just-hiding-the-critical-information-google-says-its-gemini-ai-sips-a-mere-five-drops-of-water-per-text-prompt-but-experts-disagree-with-its-findings/). Note that this differs from LBNL's roughly 12:1 indirect-to-direct ratio for the US. The difference comes from scope and grid mix.

### Inferences
- For content piece A, the most defensible framing is a range with its scope stated: about 0.26 mL (direct, median) up to tens of mL (direct plus indirect, average, location-dependent). "500 mL per query" should be flagged as a myth.
- Indirect water depends heavily on the grid. Thermoelectric (coal, gas, nuclear) cooling is water-intensive, while wind and solar PV use almost none. A data centre's choice of power source can therefore matter more for water than its choice of cooling.

### Gaps
- No primary IEA document on data-centre water was retrieved.
- Ren's revised estimates could not be verified against a primary source.

## 3. Data centres in water-stressed regions (US, Latin America, Europe, India, South-East Asia, UK)

### Takeaway
Water conflicts are hyper-local. In each place, a facility that is negligible nationally competes with households in a stressed basin during drought: Tucson, Texas, Santiago, Canelones, Aragon, Hyderabad, Chennai, Johor and Batam. Regulators are responding with water-source conditions such as reclaimed-water mandates and tier bans, not national caps.

### Cited Findings
**Arizona: Tucson "Project Blue" (Beale Infrastructure)**
- Tucson refused city water; the City Manager said the project was ineligible for city resources. The city revoked a contractor's water meter permit and demanded 2 acre-feet back (May 2026) — [Tucson Sentinel](https://www.tucsonsentinel.com/local/report/050526_project_blue_water/).
- Arizona water officials approved two wells that could draw up to 31 million gallons a year under existing groundwater rights. The applicant was Bobcat Tucson Water LLC (May 2026) — [KJZZ, 29 May 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/fronteras-desk/2026-05-29/project-blue-data-center-could-use-up-to-31-million-gallons-of-water-per-year); [AZPM](https://news.azpm.org/s/103423-arizona-water-officials-approve-wells-tied-to-project-blue-data-center/).
- Amazon reportedly backed out as end user in December 2025 — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/amazon-backs-out-of-project-blue-data-center-campus-in-arizona-report/).
- Pima County and Johns Hopkins released a health impact study in September 2026, warning that large data centres could strain or contaminate supplies — [KJZZ, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/fronteras-desk/2026-09-07/1-year-after-approving-project-blue-pima-county-releases-data-center-health-impact-study).

**Texas**
- HARC's white paper "Thirsty Data and the Lone Star State" (January 2026) estimates Texas data centres used about 25 billion gallons in 2025, counting cooling and power generation. That is about 0.4% of state use, in a year of extreme drought. It projects 29 to 161 billion gallons a year by 2030 (0.5 to 2.7%) — [HARC](https://harcresearch.org/news/texas-data-center-boom-could-consume-up-to-161-billion-gallons-of-water-annually-by-2030/); [WaterWorld](https://waterworld.com/water-utility-management/news/55352295/texas-data-center-expansion-poses-emerging-risks-to-water-resources-report-finds).
- The 2026 regional water plans that feed the 2027 State Water Plan do not include forecast data-centre demand, and Texas has no water-use reporting requirement — [HARC](https://harcresearch.org/news/texas-data-center-boom-could-consume-up-to-161-billion-gallons-of-water-annually-by-2030/).

**Chile (Google, Cerrillos/Santiago; older, 2020 to 2024)**
- The original plan was reported at about 169 L/s, or more than 7 billion litres a year, from a stressed aquifer. An environmental court partially reversed the permit and ordered climate-change effects to be considered. Google switched to air cooling. Local group MOSACAT argues the indirect water footprint from energy remains — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/chile-partially-reverses-google-data-center-permit-over-water-use-concerns/); [Havana Times](https://havanatimes.org/features/chile-google-switches-from-water-to-air-to-cool-data-center/); [Mongabay, Nov 2023](https://news.mongabay.com/2023/11/the-cloud-vs-drought-water-hog-data-centers-threaten-latin-america-critics-say/).

**Uruguay (Google, Canelones; older, 2023)**
- During the country's worst drought since 1950, court-obtained documents showed cooling towers would need about 7.6 million litres (2 million gallons) of potable water a day. Google later revised the design toward air cooling, and the project status is unclear — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2023/11/the-cloud-vs-drought-water-hog-data-centers-threaten-latin-america-critics-say/); [Rest of World, 2026](https://restofworld.org/2026/ai-datacenters-water-resistance-global/).

**Spain: Aragon**
- AWS pledged almost $40bn for several campuses; Microsoft and Blackstone are also building. Aragon's fast-track permits cut the public objection period. Ecologistas en Acción filed what is believed to be Spain's first lawsuit against a major data-centre development, challenging the fast-track granted to Amazon. Hundreds have protested in Zaragoza. The regional government calls the water impact "imperceptible" — [NPR, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/22/nx-s1-5991641/ai-data-centers-spain-environmental-impact); [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/fasttrack-permits-turn-spains-aragon-into-a-70-billion-data-centre-magnet-at-what-cost-4933596).
- A SourceMaterial investigation found Amazon's three proposed Aragon sites licensed for about 755,720 m³ a year, and that Amazon asked in December (2024) to raise water use at its three existing sites by 48% — [SourceMaterial](https://www.source-material.org/amazon-microsoft-google-trump-data-centres-water-use/).

**India**
- Most Indian data centres use evaporative cooling. A 100 MW evaporatively cooled facility can use about 0.8 to 2 million litres a day — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/thirsty-data-centres-thin-regulation-who-is-watching-indias-water).
- Mumbai/Navi Mumbai and Chennai hold about 70% of Indian data-centre capacity (2025). Chennai hit "Day Zero" in 2019, with reservoirs dry. Hyderabad faces a projected municipal shortfall of about 870 to 909 million litres a day by 2027, with the Gachibowli IT corridor groundwater most affected — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/water/india-is-using-water-to-build-the-machines-that-tell-us-we-are-running-out-of-it); [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/environment/the-cloud-that-drains-the-ground-india-data-centres-groundwater-levels-water-crisis). The two shortfall figures differ.
- None of India's roughly 15 state data-centre policies require centralised water disclosure or mandatory water-stress mapping as a clearance condition. A weak El Niño monsoon was expected in 2026 — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/thirsty-data-centres-thin-regulation-who-is-watching-indias-water); [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/the-cloud-runs-on-water).
- One analysis projects that 60 to 80% of Indian data centres will face high water stress this decade — [Reccessary](https://www.reccessary.com/en/news/india-ai-data-center-water-crisis). Water stress came up at the AI Impact Summit 2026 in New Delhi — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/amp/story/national/ai-impact-summit-2026-water-stress-from-data-centres-a-cause-for-concern).

**Malaysia: Johor**
- In November 2025 Johor stopped accepting applications for Tier 1 and 2 data centres. Officials said these use up to about 50 million litres a day, about 200 times the roughly 200,000 L/day of Tier 3 and 4 sites. ("Tier" is the Uptime reliability tier; the ratio is the officials' claim.) Even where water is needed, it must be reclaimed water rather than Syarikat Air Johor supply, and sites are restricted to industrial zones. Johor had approved 51 projects: 17 operational, 11 under construction and 23 approved — [NST, Nov 2025](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2025/11/1324188/johor-shuts-door-water-guzzling-data-centres-tightens-approval-rules); [W.Media](https://w.media/johor-stops-approving-tier-1-and-tier-2-data-centers-due-to-water-overuse/).
- MITI told Parliament that, as of January 2026, no investment had been deferred over water — [Climate Ready RE brief](https://briefs.climatereadyre.com/p/johor-data-centers-how-tier-bans). A June 2026 opinion piece called for a national moratorium — [FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/opinion/2026/06/26/impose-moratorium-on-new-data-centres-until-citizens-needs-are-guaranteed). The Diplomat framed it as a right-to-water issue in April 2026 — [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/04/whose-water-powers-the-cloud-data-centers-and-the-right-to-water-in-johor/).
- Malaysia saw its first protest targeting a data centre over pollution and water — [Let's Data Science](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/malaysia-draws-first-data-center-protest-over-pollution-wate-4ae43038).

**Indonesia: Batam**
- Residents protested, including with a road blockade, over worsening clean-water access linked to data-centre expansion (October 2026) — [Jakarta Post, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2026/10/08/batam-residents-protest-data-center-expansion-as-water-shortages-worsen).

**UK: AI Growth Zones**
- Water UK told Parliament's Environmental Audit Committee that the AI Growth Zone plans are "fatally flawed", because water planning excludes data centres from demand forecasts — [Computing, 2026](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/datacentre/water-shortage-could-sink-uk-s-ai-datacentre-ambitions); [Novara Media, 21 Jul 2026](https://novaramedia.com/2026/07/21/britain-does-not-have-enough-water-for-planned-ai-data-centre-boom/).
- Growth Zone applicants must show water access sufficient for at least 500 MW of AI infrastructure — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-growth-zones/ai-growth-zones-open-for-applications). Culham in Oxfordshire was the first zone; one source lists 349 MW there. A North Lanarkshire (Scotland) zone was announced on 29 Jan 2026 — [Barbour ABI](https://barbour-abi.com/ai-growth-zones-uk-and-their-data-centres/).
- A reported 84% of proposed UK data centres would sit in water-stressed areas (unverified) — [The Canary, Jan 2026](https://www.thecanary.co.uk/uk/analysis/2026/01/06/ai-data-centres-water-stressed-areas/).

**Global siting pattern**
- About two-thirds of data centres built since 2022 are in arid or drought-stressed areas, according to a study cited by Defenders of Wildlife — [Defenders of Wildlife, Sep 2026](https://defenders.org/blog/2026/09/data-center-boom-upon-us-what-will-it-mean-water-dwelling-animals).

### Inferences
- The regulatory response in the Global South is a water-source condition (reclaimed water in Johor, air cooling forced in Chile), not a ban. India has no equivalent disclosure or siting rule, which makes it the clearest regulatory gap in the Global South.
- Fast-track permitting (Aragon) and weakly regulated groundwater (Arizona's existing rights, Texas) are where the water fights are sharpest.

- No reliable per-site water figures for Indian hyperscaler campuses were found.

## 4. Cooling technologies: evaporative vs air vs liquid/immersion; zero-water designs

### Takeaway
Cooling choice trades water against energy. Evaporative cooling saves electricity but consumes water. Dry and air cooling saves water but raises energy use, and therefore indirect water. Closed-loop direct-to-chip liquid cooling, driven by AI rack density, enables near-zero on-site water. Adoption is still a minority: about 22% of operators per Uptime's 2025 survey.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft's zero-water AI design (announced in 2024) uses closed-loop chip-level cooling that recirculates water without evaporation. Microsoft says it avoids about 125,000 m³ a year per facility. It applies to new designs only — [Microsoft blog, 25 Jul 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-cloud/blog/2024/07/25/sustainable-by-design-transforming-datacenter-water-efficiency/); [ESG News](https://esgnews.com/microsoft-unveils-zero-water-datacenter-design-to-slash-cooling-water-use/); [Microsoft 2025 ESR](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2025-Microsoft-Environmental-Sustainability-Report.pdf).
- Microsoft's CEO said new AI data centres use about as little water a year as a restaurant. Critics note this does not address its 500+ existing data centres — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/microsoft-ceo-says-new-ai-data-centers-use-as-little-water-annually-as-a-restaurant-closed-loop-cooling-system-aims-to-slash-consumption-from-millions-of-gallons-as-ai-infrastructure-faces-mounting-environmental-scrutiny).
- Uptime Institute's Cooling Systems Survey 2025 (July 2025): direct liquid cooling (DLC) adoption remains "slow and steady", and most operators still use air — [Uptime, UII Data Report 181](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/dlc-adoption-remains-slow-and-steady). Secondary summaries put perimeter air cooling at about 75% of respondents and DLC at about 22% (verify) — [Taskade wiki](https://www.taskade.com/wiki/infrastructure/liquid-cooling).
- Rack density, not sustainability, drives DLC: 69% cite higher rack density and 48% high-powered servers. The share citing sustainability fell to 22%, the lowest since 2021 (November 2025) — [Uptime UII Update 431](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/fewer-operators-cite-sustainability-dlc-driver). Uptime also argues "DLC will not come to the rescue of data center sustainability" — [Uptime](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/dlc-will-not-come-rescue-data-center-sustainability).
- On the trade-off: Google's switch to air cooling in Chile cut water use, but experts note that air cooling raises energy consumption — [Havana Times](https://havanatimes.org/features/chile-google-switches-from-water-to-air-to-cool-data-center/). Blackstone says its Aragon cooling will not use water — [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/fasttrack-permits-turn-spains-aragon-into-a-70-billion-data-centre-magnet-at-what-cost-4933596).
- In practice, WUE ranges from Amazon's 0.12 L/kWh (2025) to Meta's 0.19 L/kWh (2024) — [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/amazon-achieves-75-of-goal-to-be-water-positive-in-data-centers-by-2030/); [Meta EDI](https://sustainability.atmeta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Meta_2025-Environmental-Data-Index.pdf).
- Reclaimed and recycled water is an alternative source. A Chennai facility recycles all its cooling-tower wastewater, and analysts urge using treated wastewater in stressed regions — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/thirsty-data-centres-thin-regulation-who-is-watching-indias-water).
- The EU will grade data centres A to G on separate energy and water scales from August 2027 (proposed), which creates a regulatory signal on water — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/sustainability/from-reporting-to-rating-the-eus-next-steps-for-data-centres-102o345).

### Inferences
- "Zero water" claims usually mean zero evaporative water on site during operation. They exclude construction, humidification, potable use and indirect power-plant water. Content should define the term.
- Liquid cooling also raises outlet temperatures (warm water at about 40 to 60°C is typical). That makes heat reuse easier, so it links piece A to piece B. This is an inference from engineering norms; it was not sourced in this research.

### Gaps
- No verified fleet-wide statistics on immersion-cooling adoption were found.
- The exact water-saving percentages of Uptime's 2025 survey were not extracted.

## 5. Community opposition: scale, flagship fights, noise, generators and tax incentives

### Takeaway
Opposition is now a material investment risk. Data Center Watch counted about $130bn of US projects blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 and $68bn in Q2 2026. Indiana and Georgia lead in local moratoria. Virginia's Digital Gateway was killed in court. xAI's Memphis and Southaven turbines are the flagship air-pollution and environmental-justice case. Electricity bills and tax-break costs have turned a local land-use fight into a state and national affordability issue.

### Cited Findings
**Scale (Data Center Watch, US)**

The group counts about 843 opposition groups in 49 states (secondary summary) — [Superpower Daily](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/data-center-watch-counts-45-u-s-projects-delayed-or-blocked-by-opposition). **Arizona** — see Project Blue in section 3.

**Indiana**
- More than half of Indiana counties now restrict data centres (21 Oct 2026) — [WFYI](https://www.wfyi.org/statewide/2026-10-21/over-half-of-indiana-counties-now-restrict-data-centers).

- All statewide bills failed in the 2026 session: HB 1012 (moratorium) and SB 408/410 (repealing the tax exemption). DeKalb extended its pause to 30 Mar 2027. **Tax incentives**
- Good Jobs First (April 2026): 14 states do not disclose revenue lost to data-centre breaks, and Georgia, Virginia and Texas each lose at least $1bn a year — [Stateline, 15 Apr 2026](https://stateline.org/2026/04/15/many-states-dont-report-losses-from-data-center-tax-breaks-study-says/).

**Electricity prices for residents (PJM)**
- PJM capacity prices rose from $28.92/MW-day (2024/25) to $329.17 (2026/27, at the cap) and $333.44/MW-day in the next auction — [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/data-centers/pjm-record-capacity-costs-rising-bills); [IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/projected-data-center-growth-spurs-pjm-capacity-prices-factor-10).

- Counterpoint: Rutgers' New Jersey State Policy Lab found no statistically significant effect on bills at the level where rates are set ("mostly not. Yet.") — [Rutgers](https://policylab.rutgers.edu/publication/are-data-centers-raising-your-electric-bill-mostly-not-yet/). SemiAnalysis argues the capacity auction drives most of the roughly 15% bill rise in PJM — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/are-ai-datacenters-increasing-electric).

**International grid moratoria and conditions**
- **Ireland:** The CRU's Large Energy User connection policy, decision paper CRU/2025/236 (December 2025), applies to data centres only. Sites of 10 MVA or more must provide dispatchable on-site or nearby generation or storage at least equal to their maximum import capacity, and System Operators must weigh grid constraints, effectively replacing the de facto Dublin moratorium in place since 2021/22. Lexology's summary also lists new renewables of at least 80% of annual demand, but this was not confirmed in primary text — [CRU decision paper](https://cruie-live-96ca64acab2247eca8a850a7e54b-5b34f62.divio-media.com/documents/CRU2025236_Large_Energy_User_connection_policy_decision_paper.pdf); [DLA Piper, Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/new-irish-large-energy-users-connection-policy); [Philip Lee](https://www.philiplee.ie/re-balancing-the-digital-bargain-irelands-new-cru-large-energy-user-connection-policy/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e73e6b8d-5d58-4a19-98d2-4c5e8fdb818c).
- **Singapore:** The moratorium of 2019 to 2022 was lifted with conditions. The second Call for Application (DC-CFA2, launched 1 Dec 2025, closed 31 Mar 2026) offers at least 200 MW, contributing to the Green DC Roadmap's 300 MW or more. It requires PUE of 1.25 or better at 100% load, Green Mark Platinum, and at least 50% green energy pathways — [IMDA factsheet](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/factsheets/2025/launch-of-second-data-centre); [Morgan Lewis, Mar 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/ja/pubs/2026/03/singapore-announces-data-center-capacity-allocation-call).
- **Amsterdam:** A 2019 to 2020 one-year moratorium was followed by a PUE of 1.2 for new builds, regional power budgets, and draft rules capping municipal data-centre expansion at about 670 MVA, reportedly to 2030 (unconfirmed) — [Greenberg Traurig, Mar 2024](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/3/challenges-in-the-dutch-data-center-market); [DPL News](https://dplnews.com/?p=61483).
- **Frankfurt:** The June 2022 Data Centre Masterplan zones the city into "suitable", "restricted" and "exclusion" areas and adds a city-wide waste-heat concept (older) — [DLA Piper, Jul 2022](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2022/07/update-on-frankfurt-data-centre-masterplan). About 1,020 MW was live in Frankfurt as of September 2026 — [Datacentres.com](https://www.datacentres.com/news/frankfurt-data-centre-market-1-020-mw-live-as-germany-s-waste-heat-mandate-colli-slot1-2026-09-07).
- **Europe more widely:** Protests have spread to Switzerland, with a resistance camp at Beringen — [Bluewin](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/switzerland/protests-against-ai-data-centers-reach-switzerland-3268074.html).

### Inferences
- The opposition's coalition is broad: environmental-justice groups (Memphis), affluent exurban homeowners (Prince William, Loudoun), farmers (Indiana) and fiscal conservatives (tax-break critics). That breadth explains its bipartisan local success while statewide bills fail, as in Georgia.
- On-site gas turbines, used to beat grid queues (xAI, Vantage VA2), are creating a new air-permit front. The Virginia case shows that "backup" diesel can become de facto prime power during a failure, with regulators saying they lack authority.
- Outside the US, the dominant tool is conditional permission rather than outright bans: Ireland's self-supply rule, Singapore's PUE and green-energy auction, Amsterdam's power budgets.

### Gaps
- The exact holding and date of the court ruling on the DOJ intervention in the xAI case are unverified.
- The text of Ireland's 80% renewables obligation was not confirmed in the primary CRU text.
- Further UK AI Growth Zone sites (North East status, the OpenAI pause) were not confirmed.
- Specific noise-ordinance standards across jurisdictions were not compiled.

## 6. Heat reuse and circularity: EU EED, German EnEfG, Nordic district heating, incentives

### Takeaway
Europe is turning waste heat from a nice-to-have into a regulated metric. The EU's EED reporting, with an A-to-G label proposed from 2027, adds a "waste heat reuse ready" status. Germany's EnEfG requires 10, 15 and 20% heat reuse for new sites, but a June 2026 cabinet amendment loosens the PUE limits and adds exemptions. The Nordics show it works at scale where district heating networks exist. Yet only 67 EU data centres reported any heat reuse in the first EU reporting round.

### Cited Findings
**EU Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791, Art. 12 and Delegated Regulation 2024/1364**
- Operators report KPIs (energy, PUE, water, waste-heat reuse, renewables) to an EU database under the March 2024 delegated regulation — [European Commission, 15 Mar 2024](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-adopts-eu-wide-scheme-rating-sustainability-data-centres-2024-03-15_en).
- On 21 Sep 2026 the Commission adopted a delegated regulation creating a common EU rating scheme for data centres above 500 kW, now in a two-month scrutiny period by Parliament and Council. It is expected to take effect by end-2026 — [European Commission, 21 Sep 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/making-data-centres-energy-efficient-thanks-new-eu-rating-system-2026-09-21_en); [Council doc ST-13443-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-13443-2026-INIT/en/pdf).
- From August 2027 (proposed), automatically generated labels will grade A to G on separate energy and water scales, with a new "waste heat reuse ready" status. Only 67 data centres reported some waste-heat reuse in the first reporting period. A consultation on minimum performance standards closes on 14 Dec 2026, with a legislative proposal expected in Q2 2027 — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/sustainability/from-reporting-to-rating-the-eus-next-steps-for-data-centres-102o345); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-proposes-mandatory-sustainability-label-for-data-centres).
- Note: separately, EED Art. 26(6) requires data centres above 1 MW to use waste heat or other waste-heat recovery applications unless this is technically or economically infeasible. This is from my knowledge of the EED text; it was not re-verified in this session's sources.

**Germany: Energieeffizienzgesetz (EnEfG, in force November 2023), as currently enacted**
- Scope: data centres with at least 300 kW non-redundant connected load — [Stratum CGI](https://stratumcgi.de/insights-energieeffizienzgesetz-rechenzentren/); [TÜV](https://consulting.tuv.com/aktuelles/datacenter/enefg-im-rechenzentrum).
- PUE: 1.2 or below for sites commissioned from 1 Jul 2026. Existing sites must reach 1.5 by 1 Jul 2027 and 1.3 by 1 Jul 2030 — [klamm.de](https://www.klamm.de/news/energieverbrauchseffektivitaet-von-12-der-grenzwert-des-energieeffizienzgesetzes-gilt-fuer-rechenzentren-die-4N1313385.html); [MVV](https://b2b.mvv.de/blog/energieeffizienzgesetz-f%C3%BCr-rechenzentren-rztm).
- Energy reuse factor (ERF) for new sites: at least 10% if commissioned from 1 Jul 2026, 15% from 1 Jul 2027 and 20% from 1 Jul 2028. Exemption: sites feeding at least 50% of their heat into a heat network with consumption under 7.5 GWh. Renewable electricity: 50% from 2024 and 100% from 2027 under current law — [MVV](https://b2b.mvv.de/blog/energieeffizienzgesetz-f%C3%BCr-rechenzentren-rztm); [Stratum CGI](https://stratumcgi.de/insights-energieeffizienzgesetz-rechenzentren/). The 50%/100% renewables split is from my background knowledge of the act; the sources confirm only that the 100% deadline exists and is being pushed back.
- Amendment (Novelle): the federal cabinet approved it on 24 Jun 2026. It raises the scope threshold (to an installed IT capacity threshold), relaxes PUE limits (reported as 1.6 to mid-2027 and 1.4 to 2030 for existing sites), keeps the 10/15/20% heat quotas with new exemptions, and gives three more years for 100% renewables. The Bundesrat (Drucksache 388/26) asked for the waste-heat rules to be strengthened. Passage was expected in autumn 2026 and was unconfirmed as of 30 Oct 2026 — [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/enefg-novelle-bringt-entlastung-fuer-rechenzentren); [Taylor Wessing, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/enefg-novelle-ueberblick); [cloudmagazin, Jul 2026](https://www.cloudmagazin.com/2026/07/06/enefg-novelle-rechenzentren-pflichten-gelockert/); [Bundesrat Drs. 388/26](https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2026/0388-1-26.pdf); [ComputerBase](https://www.computerbase.de/news/netzpolitik/energieeffizienzgesetz-regierung-will-energieregeln-fuer-rechenzentren-lockern.99997/).
- Industry association eco calls a blanket heat-reuse obligation unworkable, while Green and Left MPs warn that valuable heat will go unused — [cloudmagazin](https://www.cloudmagazin.com/2026/07/06/enefg-novelle-rechenzentren-pflichten-gelockert/).

**Nordic and other district-heating examples**
- **Microsoft and Fortum, Espoo/Kirkkonummi (Finland):** heat-pump plants at Hepokorpi (Espoo) and Kolabacken (Kirkkonummi) started on 6 May 2026, initially running on ambient-air heat pumps and e-boilers. Microsoft's data-centre heat phases in from 2027. At completion it should cover about 40% of the roughly 2 TWh a year of district-heat demand for about 250,000 users. Earlier reports cited about 350 MW — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fortum-starts-operations-at-two-heat-pump-plants-tied-to-microsoft-district-heating-scheme/); [pv magazine, 7 May 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/07/large-scale-heat-pumps-utilize-excess-heat-from-microsoft-data-centers-in-finland/); [DataCenterSweden](https://datacentersweden.se/news/fortum-espoo-kirkkonummi-heat-pumps-2026-05/); [Eurelectric](https://www.eurelectric.org/stories/microsoft-data-centers-provide-40-of-fortums-electricity-based-district-heating-in-espoo-finland/).
- **Stockholm Exergi / Stockholm Data Parks (Sweden):** the Open District Heating platform buys data-centre heat as a tradable product. It connects 30+ data centres from 16 providers, and heat is said to be worth about SEK 2m per MW per year to sellers — [Eurelectric](https://www.eurelectric.org/stories/stockholm-exergi-data-parks-shows-the-potential-for-scaling-the-reuse-of-waste-heat-when-it-becomes-a-tradable-product/).
- **Meta, Odense (Denmark):** supplies district heat for about 11,000 households (some sources say 12,000+) — [Energy Solutions (secondary)](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/data-center-waste-heat-district-heating).
- **Frankfurt:** the masterplan includes a city-wide heat concept to tackle technical and commercial barriers — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2022/07/update-on-frankfurt-data-centre-masterplan).

**Incentives and carbon credits**
- Stockholm's model is the clearest market incentive: heat sold as a product. I found no verified carbon-credit methodology specifically for data-centre heat reuse in this research.

### Inferences
- Heat reuse depends on district heating networks and heat pumps. It works in the Nordics, and the EnEfG exemption for network-feeding sites acknowledges this. In hot, water-stressed Global South markets (India, Johor, Arizona) there is little heat demand, so circularity there means water reuse instead (reclaimed water, closed loops).
- Germany's loosening, at a time when only 67 EU sites report any heat reuse, suggests regulatory ambition is colliding with economics. The EU labels from 2027 may become the main pressure lever instead.

### Gaps
- No primary figures were found on avoided emissions or MWh of heat reused, EU-wide.
- No carbon-credit or certificate scheme for data-centre heat reuse was found. This is a key gap for the "carbon credits" angle.
- Final Bundestag passage of the EnEfG amendment was not confirmed.

## 7. Biodiversity and land use

### Takeaway
Land-use impacts appear mostly at project level: campuses of 1,000 acres or more on farmland, wetland fill, and rezoning next to battlefields or forests. Systematic biodiversity studies are only starting to appear, mainly on freshwater.

### Cited Findings
- Project footprints include Amazon's New Carlisle, Indiana campus (about 1,200 acres), farmland conversion in Nebraska for Meta and Google, and the Digital Gateway rezoning (about 2,100 acres) — [arXiv 2608.09882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09882).
- About 10 acres of wetlands are to be filled at New Carlisle (advocacy) — [Hoosier Environmental Council](https://www.hecweb.org/2025/06/28/measuring-the-environmental-cost-of-artificial-intelligence-and-their-data-centers/).
- A February 2026 screening study maps where high data-centre water-scarcity footprints overlap with aquatic biodiversity across the US — [ScienceDirect, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772735126000399).
- Defenders of Wildlife (September 2026) flags habitat loss and fragmentation, and risks to water-dwelling species — [Defenders of Wildlife](https://defenders.org/blog/2026/09/data-center-boom-upon-us-what-will-it-mean-water-dwelling-animals); [The Animal Reader, Jul 2026](https://www.theanimalreader.com/2026/07/10/how-ai-data-centers-are-affecting-wildlife/).
- In Aragon, Botorrita residents learned of a project only when survey markers appeared, an example of rural land change with little consultation — [NPR, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/22/nx-s1-5991641/ai-data-centers-spain-environmental-impact).
- Caveat: an average figure of about 40 acres per data centre in circulation is undated and secondary, and is far below current AI campus sizes — [TechTarget](https://techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/feature/The-increasing-concern-of-data-center-land-acquisition).

### Inferences
- Land and biodiversity concerns are usually bundled with farmland loss and rural character in US opposition (Indiana bans), rather than argued on ecological grounds alone.

### Gaps
- No global or national estimate of the total land area converted for data centres was found.
- No peer-reviewed terrestrial biodiversity study specific to data centres was found.
