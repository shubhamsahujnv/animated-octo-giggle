## 1. State of the voluntary carbon market (VCM): size, value 2024–2026, and integrity scandals

### Takeaway
The VCM is small and flat by value. Ecosystem Marketplace (EM) put 2024 transaction value at about $535M (down 29%). Sylvera and MSCI put 2025 retirement value at about $1.0–1.4B. Volume is falling while spend shifts toward higher-rated credits. Trust was damaged by a string of exposés: Verra REDD+ (2023), Kariba, cookstove over-crediting, and the C-Quest fraud charges (Oct 2024). Peer-reviewed meta-analysis suggests fewer than 1 in 6 issued credits reflect real reductions.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and value (methods differ widely, so the series are not comparable)**
- **[2024 data, EM 2025 report]** EM's *State of the VCM 2025* put 2024 total market value at about **$535M, down 29% from 2023**. That is similar to 2020's value, but it came from only **84 MtCO2e**, about 40% of 2020 volume. Secondary summaries of the same report give transaction volumes **down 25%** and average prices **down 5.5%** — [EM SOVCM 2025 PDF](https://3298623.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/3298623/SOVCM%202025/Ecosystem%20Marketplace%20State%20of%20the%20Voluntary%20Carbon%20Market%202025.pdf); also cited in [Stanford Law white paper, May 2026](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2026_SLS-Eco_Limitations-in-Carbon-Markets-White-Paper_final.pdf)
- **[Late 2025 survey]** In EM's survey of developers and brokers, **63%** said buyer interest was unchanged vs six months earlier and **47%** reported no price change — [EM, VCM Demand Outlook in 2026](https://www.ecosystemmarketplace.com/articles/vcm-demand-outlook-in-2026/)
- I found no 2026 edition of EM's *State of the VCM* (covering 2025 data) — see Gaps.
- **[2025 data, Sylvera]** Retirements fell **4.5% to 168 million credits**, while market value rose **~6% to $1.04B**. High-quality credits (rated BB+ and above) made up **50% of retirements and 70% of spend** — [Sylvera State of Carbon Credits 2025](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/sylvera-state-of-carbon-credits-2025-market-shifts-from-volume-to-value)
- **[2025 data, MSCI]** The primary carbon-credit market was about **$1.4B**, valued as retired volume times estimated purchase price. MSCI said global retirements **rose 3% y/y**, matching 2021's record. This contradicts Sylvera's 4.5% decline, a difference in methodology — [MSCI, Carbon credits come of age in 2025](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/carbon-credits-come-of-age-in-2025)
- **[2025 data, AlliedOffsets]** AlliedOffsets says total market value **"surpassed $10 billion"** for the first time since it began tracking in 2020. Its methodology is not explained in what I found, and the figure is an order of magnitude above EM, Sylvera and MSCI — [AlliedOffsets 2025 review](https://hubspot-landing.alliedoffsets.com/voluntary-carbon-market-2025-review)
- **[2025 data, Carbon Direct]** VCM credit retirements **fell 7% in 2025** — [Carbon Direct 2026 State of the VCM](https://www.carbon-direct.com/voluntary-carbon-market/2026)
- **[Q1 2026, Sylvera]** Retirements fell **8% to 51 million**. Value was **$290M vs $309M** in Q1 2025 — [Sylvera Q1 2026 snapshot](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/q1-2026-carbon-data-snapshot)
- **[H1 2026, Sylvera]** H1 2026 retirement value was **$548M vs $524.2M** in H1 2025 — [Sylvera carbon market trends](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/carbon-market-trends) (via search summary of its Q2 2026 snapshot)
- Commercial market-research firms quote much larger figures (e.g., "$2.83B in 2026"). These are not comparable to transaction-value series — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6076317/voluntary-carbon-credit-market-report)

**Integrity scandals and evidence**
- **[Jan 2023] Guardian / Die Zeit / SourceMaterial on Verra REDD+.** A nine-month investigation found that **more than 90%** of Verra's rainforest offset credits (in the projects studied) were likely "phantom credits". Analysis cited in the coverage indicated that **up to 94%** of the credits had no climate benefit. Named buyers included Gucci, Salesforce, BHP, Shell, easyJet and Pearl Jam. Verra called the findings "patently unreliable" — [Carbon Herald summary](https://carbonherald.com/the-guardian-investigation-of-verra-carbon-offsets-shows-90-are-worthless/); [Carbon Economist on Verra's rebuttal](https://pemedianetwork.com/carbon-economist/articles/voluntary-markets-offsets/2023/offsets-standards-body-refutes-media-s-redd-claims)
- **Kariba REDD+ (Zimbabwe; South Pole as former carbon-asset manager).** Verra estimated **15,220,520 "excess credits"**, versus South Pole's own estimate of about **27 million**. Verra asked holders of about **4.9 million** credits to cancel them voluntarily, and South Pole announced cancellation of **2.5 million**. Verra "requested compensation" from developer Carbon Green Investments (CGI), which then **withdrew the project from Verra's registry**. CarbonPlan (Oct 2025) called this a "profound structural flaw" and said Verra's buffer pool was not designed to absorb excess crediting at this scale. Retired Kariba credits remain valid on the registry — [CarbonPlan, Oct 2025](https://carbonplan.org/research/kariba-excess-credits); [REDD-Monitor](https://reddmonitor.substack.com/p/152-million-fake-carbon-credits-were?open=false); [South Pole statement](https://www.southpole.com/news/response-to-verras-final-update-on-its-section-6-review-into-kariba-redd)
- **[Nov 2024] Probst et al., *Nature Communications*.** Fewer than **16%** of credits issued to the projects studied represent real emission reductions. Rates by type: **11%** for cookstoves, **68%** for HFC-23 (the best). Wind and improved forest management showed no statistically significant reductions. The meta-study covers **14 studies of 2,346 projects** — [VU Amsterdam record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/systematic-assessment-of-the-achieved-emission-reductions-of-carb/); [Öko-Institut](https://www.oeko.de/publikation/systematic-assessment-of-the-achieved-emission-reductions-of-carbon-crediting-projects/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/e3ff0db656564f889739ce9531b53363); [Max Planck press release](https://www.mpg.de/23737687/climate-impact-of-carbon-crediting-projects-substantially-overestimated)
- **[Jan 2024] Gill-Wiehl, Kammen and Haya, *Nature Sustainability*, on cookstoves.** The sample (about 40% of cookstove credits) was over-credited **9.2x**. Extrapolated to all credits from the methodologies studied, the factor is about **10.6x**. Gold Standard's metered methodology came closest, at about **1.5x** — [Earth.com](https://earth.com/news/cookstove-emissions-are-underestimated-by-a-factor-of-10); [Berkeley Carbon Trading Project](https://dev.gspp.berkeley.edu/berkeley-carbon-trading-project/cookstoves). A further 2026 paper, "Pervasive overcrediting" (Notre Dame-hosted PDF, Aug 2026), is listed — [PDF](https://strand.nd.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2026-08/Pervasive_overcrediting_maintext.pdf)
- **[Oct 2024] C-Quest Capital (CQC) fraud charges.** Former CEO **Kenneth Newcombe** and former head of carbon accounting **Tridip Goswami** were indicted in New York (SDNY) on wire fraud and commodities fraud. Prosecutors allege they manipulated cookstove data to inflate credits between 2021 and 2023. The most serious charge carries up to **20 years**. Newcombe denies the allegations. The **SEC** settled a cease-and-desist order with CQC, and the **CFTC** filed a civil complaint the same day. CQC itself had disclosed "over-issuance of millions of carbon credits" — [Bloomberg, 2 Oct 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-10-02/former-c-quest-ceo-newcombe-charged-with-carbon-credit-fraud); [Channel 4 News](https://www.channel4.com/news/carbon-credits-pioneer-charged-with-fraud-after-channel-4-news-probe); [REDD-Monitor](https://reddmonitor.substack.com/p/over-issuance-of-millions-of-carbon); [Follow the Money](https://www.ftm.eu/articles/us-charges-against-carbon-offsetting-boss-highlight-wider-industry-problems)

### Inferences
- The "value" of the market depends heavily on the data provider: about $0.5B (EM, 2024 transactions), about $1.0–1.4B (Sylvera/MSCI, 2025 retirements), or over $10B (AlliedOffsets, unclear method). Content should name the provider and method every time.
- Across 2024–2026, volumes are falling while value per credit rises and quality-rated credits take a growing share of spend. This "flight to quality" is a direct market response to the 2023–2024 scandals.
- The tech-sector CDR spending in Section 4 is now comparable in size to, or larger than, the whole avoidance-credit VCM by annual contract value. Removals and avoidance are diverging into separate markets.

### Gaps
- EM's 2026 *State of the VCM* (2025 data) was not found. It may be unpublished or simply not indexed.
- Current status of the Newcombe/Goswami criminal case (trial, plea or verdict) as of Oct 2026 is not confirmed. One aggregator claims CQC was "found guilty", which conflicts with other reports and is not verified.
- Whether CGI ever compensated Verra for the Kariba excess credits is not confirmed.

## 2. Integrity frameworks: ICVCM CCPs, VCMI, SBTi, Article 6 (COP29/COP30), CORSIA, CFTC, EU CRCF

### Takeaway
The supply-side (ICVCM) and demand-side (VCMI, SBTi) rulebooks matured in 2025–2026. VCMI requires CCP-labelled credits from 1 Jan 2026. SBTi's Net-Zero Standard v2.0 (June 2026) still bars credits from counting toward targets, but signals mandatory removal purchases from 2035. Article 6.4 (the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism) was operationalised across COP29 and COP30, with a contested, flexible permanence standard. The EU's CRCF methodologies for permanent removals entered into force in May 2026. The US went the other way: the CFTC withdrew its carbon-credit derivatives guidance in Sept 2025.

### Cited Findings
**ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (CCP)**
- **[2025]** ICVCM conditionally approved three cookstove methodologies: GS TPDDTEC v4.0, GS Metered & Measured v1.2, and Verra VM0050 v1.0. Conditions: fNRB (fraction of non-renewable biomass) must use a conservative **30% default** or MoFuSS-derived values, and TOOL30 is to be withdrawn in 2026. The **GS Simplified methodology was rejected** — [RMI explainer](https://rmi.org/resources/technical-explainer-clean-and-improved-cookstove-carbon-credits/); [Carbon Market Watch, Mar 2025](https://carbonmarketwatch.org/2025/03/12/icvcm-hits-on-promising-recipe-for-cookstoves-methodologies/)
- Calyx Global found that **none of 203** Calyx-rated cookstove and biodigester projects were CCP-eligible under the extra conditions. Veyt concluded the approval "will not lead to more CCP-labelled credits" — [Calyx Global](https://calyxglobal.com/research-hub/research/how-the-ICVCM-CCP-labels-are-raising-the-quality-of-cookstoves-and-household-biodigester-projects/); [Veyt](https://veyt.com/icvcm-s-cookstove-methodologies-approval-will-not-lead-to-more-ccp-labelled-credits/)
- REDD+: Verra **VM0048** is approved, as is **ART-TREES** jurisdictional REDD+. Older project-based REDD+ methodologies are not approved. One secondary source counts **13 CCP-eligible programmes and 45 CCP-approved methodologies as of Aug 2026** (not verified against ICVCM tables) — [ReGreener](https://www.regreener.earth/blog/the-5-best-ccp-labeled-carbon-credits-of-2026); [ICVCM first CCP labels](https://icvcm.org/integrity-council-announces-first-high-integrity-ccp-labelled-carbon-credits-as-assessments-continue/)

**VCMI**
- The **Scope 3 Action Code of Practice** was published in **April 2025**. It targets companies with validated Scope 1–2 targets that are off-track on Scope 3, and requires high-quality credits covering **at least the entire Scope 3 "gap"**. It is a "Code of Practice", not a verified claim — [VCMI Scope 3 Action Code PDF](https://vcmintegrity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/VCMI-Scope-3-Action-Code-of-Practice.pdf); [VCMI consultation report](https://vcmintegrity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/VCMI-Beta-Scope-3-Claim-Public-Consultation-Report.pdf)
- Under the VCMI Claims Code, companies must retire **CCP-approved credits from 1 January 2026** — [VCMI Claims Code factsheet](https://vcmintegrity.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Claims-Code-Factsheet.pdf)
- Versions conflict: one blog says Claims Code **v3.2 was published Sept 2026**, while another lists **v3.1 (Aug 2025)** as current (not verified with VCMI) — [ReGreener](https://www.regreener.earth/blog/vcmi-claims-code); [GreenCalculus](https://greencalculus.com/standards/vcmi-claims-code-of-practice/)

**SBTi**
- The **Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2.0 was published 11 June 2026**, with validations from **1 Feb 2027**. One source says it becomes mandatory for new submissions from 1 Feb 2028 — [Senken](https://www.senken.io/blog/sbti-net-zero-standard-v2-what-changes-for-corporate-carbon-credit-strategy-2026); [EcoAct](https://eco-act.com/blog/sbti-corporate-net-zero-standard-version-2-0/)
- **Credits cannot count toward Scope 1, 2 or 3 targets.** Avoidance and reduction credits, sink-protection credits and removals are recognised under a voluntary **"Ongoing Emissions Responsibility" (OER)** track — [Senken](https://www.senken.io/blog/sbti-net-zero-standard-v2-what-changes-for-corporate-carbon-credit-strategy-2026); [Sentinel Earth buyer guide](https://sentinelearth.com/post/sbti-net-zero-standard-v2-carbon-credit-eac-buyers/)
- **Removals:** v2.0 signals a future **mandatory removals requirement from 2035** for "Category A" companies. It starts at **1% of ongoing Scope 1+2+3 emissions** and rises linearly to **100% by the net-zero year** (no later than 2050). Within that, a long-lived removals share starts at about **10%** — [Climeworks guide, June 2026](https://climeworks.com/guide/sbtis-corporate-net-zero-standard-v20-and-what-it-means-for-removals); [Climeworks PDF](https://climeworks.com/uploads/documents/climeworks_sbti_v2.0_june_2026.pdf)
- **EACs:** one buyer guide says v2.0 gives credits, removals and EACs (energy attribute certificates) "distinct roles", and that credits cannot be subtracted from the inventory. I did not find SBTi's exact EAC/Scope 3 rule (see Gaps) — [Sentinel Earth](https://sentinelearth.com/post/sbti-net-zero-standard-v2-carbon-credit-eac-buyers/)

**Paris Agreement Article 6**
- **[COP29, Baku, Nov 2024]** On day one, the presidency pushed through Article 6.4 Supervisory Body standards on methodologies and removals. The full Article 6 package (6.2, 6.4 and 6.8) was adopted **23 Nov 2024** and presented as "full operationalisation". Carbon Market Watch warned of "cowboy carbon markets", and NGOs called it the "offsets COP". Experts warned 6.4 could let monoculture plantations count as removals — [Carbon Brief, COP29 nature outcomes](https://carbonbrief.org/cop29-key-outcomes-for-food-forests-land-and-nature-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-baku); [Oxford Smith School](https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/news/article-6-focus-outcomes-cop29); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/insights/2024/12/carbon-trading-and-article-6-at-cop29)
- **[COP30, Belém, Nov 2025]** The Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM) was further operationalised, with standards on baselines, additionality, leakage, suppressed demand and **non-permanence/reversals** — [Morgan Lewis, Dec 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/12/cop30-key-outcomes-for-carbon-markets). A push for stricter reversal-risk rules was rejected. Permanence is now set per methodology, which is seen as favouring nature-based credits — [Climateseed](https://climateseed.com/blog/key-takeaways-cop30)
- **CDM transition:** Carbon Brief reports a **six-month extension to June 2026** for CDM projects to transition, potentially letting **up to ~760 MtCO2e** more credits in. The CDM is to close by the end of 2026 — [Carbon Brief COP30 outcomes](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop30-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-belem). About **$26.8M** is to be transferred from the CDM to fund Article 6.4 (single source, not verified with UNFCCC) — [Sylvera COP30 debrief](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/cop30-carbon-markets-article-6-outcomes); [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/document/key-negotiations-related-outcomes-of-the-un-climate-conference-in-belem/)
- Article 6.2 bilateral deals face enhanced scrutiny through structured feedback at future COPs. The EU's 2040 climate-law revision may allow part of its target to be met with Article 6 credits — [Sylvera](https://www.sylvera.com/blog/cop30-carbon-markets-article-6-outcomes); [South Pole](https://www.southpole.com/blog/cop30-shows-progress-and-pressure-on-climate-finance)

**CORSIA (aviation; competes for the same high-integrity supply)**
- **[Sept 2026, IATA]** Eligible unit supply for the First Phase (2024–26) is "well short" of the **200–250 million units** needed by **31 Jan 2028** (cancellation deadline) — [CAPA](https://centreforaviation.com/news/iata-corsia-eeu-supply-growing-but-falling-well-short-of-units-needed-by-2028-1373032)
- **[Aug 2026]** 42 CORSIA-compatible Letters of Authorization covered about **72 million units**, up from 40M at the start of 2026. That leaves an estimated **130–180 million** units still needed. An ICAO deck (June 2026) gives **~36 Mt** from 10 countries and a gap of **134–200 Mt** — [carboncredits.com](https://carboncredits.com/econetix-rwe-corsia-carbon-credit-shortage/); [ICAO CLAC deck](https://clac-lacac.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CE109_Pres01.pdf); [EDF blog, Jul 2026](https://blogs.edf.org/energyexchange/2026/07/27/corsia-at-a-crossroads-the-next-year-will-determine-its-course.md); [Sylvera CORSIA Countdown, Jun 2026](https://info.sylvera.com/hubfs/CORSIA-Countdown-Report_Sylvera-June2026.pdf?hsLang=en)

**US CFTC**
- The CFTC **withdrew** its 15 Oct 2024 guidance on listing voluntary carbon credit derivatives on **10 Sept 2025** (Federal Register, 15 Sept 2025). It said existing CEA rules suffice, the guidance had "limited value", and it could cause confusion. CATF said the withdrawal weakens integrity assurance — [CFTC release 9119-25](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9119-25); [Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/cftc-withdraws-guidance-on-listing-voluntary-carbon-credit-derivatives-what-a-difference-an-election-makes/); [CATF, Sept 2025](https://www.catf.us/2025/09/cftc-withdrawal-of-carbon-market-guidance-will-weaken-market-integrity-and-undermine-climate-mitigation-warns-catf/)

**EU CRCF (Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming certification)**
- The Commission adopted the first permanent-removal methodologies on **3 Feb 2026**, billed as the "world's first voluntary standard for permanent carbon removals". This is **Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/285**, published in the Official Journal on 17 Apr 2026 and **in force 7 May 2026**. It covers **DACCS, BioCCS (BECCS) and biochar**. Biochar is credited on carbon remaining after **200 years**, with activity periods of up to 5 years. Enhanced rock weathering (ERW), ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE) and direct ocean capture are future candidates — [European Commission news, 3 Feb 2026](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/news-other-reads/news/eu-sets-worlds-first-voluntary-standard-permanent-carbon-removals-2026-02-03_en); [EC certification methodologies page](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming/certification-methodologies_en); [Biomassafeiten](https://www.biomassafeiten.nl/en/2026/05/07/New-EU-framework-for-permanent-carbon-removal-enters-into-force/)
- A carbon-farming delegated act (agriculture/agroforestry on mineral soils, peatland rewetting, afforestation) was reportedly adopted in **July 2026** as C(2026)4666. Puro.earth has applied for CRCF scheme recognition — [CRCFProjects](https://crcfprojects.com/crcf/); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/energy/2026-posts/a-new-eu-framework-for-carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming)
- **[17 Jul 2026, proposal only]** The Commission's EU ETS revision proposes using auctioned allowances to **centrally buy CRCF-certified BioCCS and DACCS removals for 2031–2040**. Biochar is excluded — [CRCFProjects feed](https://crcfprojects.com/feed/)
- One secondary source says EU consumer law (the Empowering Consumers / Green Claims rules) bans offset-based "carbon neutral" product claims from **27 Sept 2026** (not verified with an official EU source) — [ReGreener](https://www.regreener.earth/blog/vcmi-claims-code)

### Inferences
- For tech companies, the frameworks now point the same way: credits are a "beyond value chain" contribution, not a subtraction from the inventory (SBTi v2, VCMI). Removals become a growing requirement toward net zero (the SBTi 2035 ramp). Neutrality claims are increasingly restricted (EU consumer law). This supports Google's 2024 move away from "carbon neutral" and the shift by Microsoft and others toward removals.
- CORSIA's First Phase shortfall of 130–200 Mt competes directly with corporate buyers for authorised, high-integrity avoidance credits. That should push prices up and limit cheap supply for tech claims.
- The COP30 permanence compromise (reversal rules set per methodology) leaves nature-based removals under Article 6.4 open to the same permanence critiques that apply in the VCM.

### Gaps
- The exact SBTi v2.0 treatment of EACs and of credits for Scope 3 (beyond "credits cannot count toward targets") was not confirmed from SBTi primary text.
- I could not verify a current ICVCM count of CCP-approved methodologies from ICVCM itself.
- Whether the GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision (see Section 6) has been finalised by Oct 2026 is unconfirmed.

## 3. Which tech companies buy avoidance vs removals, how they use them in claims; Google's exit from "carbon neutral"; Microsoft's carbon-negative goal

### Takeaway
Microsoft (carbon negative by 2030) is overwhelmingly a removals buyer. Its emissions are rising sharply with AI (FY2025: about 20.3 MtCO2e, +25% y/y). Google stopped buying cheap avoidance offsets to claim operational carbon neutrality, disclosed in its July 2024 report. It now buys removals toward net zero by 2030 and frames them as contributions. Amazon mixes jurisdictional REDD+ avoidance (LEAF/Pará) with DAC removals and resells offtake access to partners. Meta buys mainly nature-based removals to cover residual emissions.

### Cited Findings
**Google**
- Google is "no longer claiming to be carbon neutral". Its **2024 Environmental Report (July 2024)** disclosed that it stopped mass purchases of cheap offsets, which had underpinned the claim since **2007**. The focus moved to **net-zero by 2030** — [Bloomberg, 8 Jul 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-07-08/google-is-no-longer-claiming-to-be-carbon-neutral); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/environment-and-energy/google-is-no-longer-claiming-to-be-carbon-neutral)
- Context: Google's emissions rose **nearly 50% over five years**, driven by AI and data centres — [Fortune, 10 Jul 2024](https://www.fortune.com/2024/07/10/ai-has-destroyed-googles-promise-of-carbon-neutrality-with-emissions-rising-50-over-the-last-five-years)
- Timing conflict: some outlets say operational neutrality ceased "from 2023" (i.e., the 2023 reporting year), while the public disclosure came in July 2024. Both can be true: the 2023 inventory year was the first without neutrality offsets — [w.media](https://w.media/?p=22960) vs [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-07-08/google-is-no-longer-claiming-to-be-carbon-neutral)

**Microsoft**
- **[2026 Environmental Sustainability Report, covering FY2025]** Total emissions were about **20.3 MtCO2e, up from 16.2 Mt in FY2024 (+25%)**, attributed mainly to datacentre expansion. Microsoft also stopped using "non-additional" unbundled RECs, which raised reported emissions — [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/microsofts-data-center-expansion-drove-25-emissions-spike-2025-environmental-sustainability-report/825082/); [Biochar Today](https://biochartoday.com/news/microsoft-2026-environmental-sustainability-report-details-25-percent-emissions-spike-driven-by-global-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure-expansion/); [Microsoft report PDF](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Sustainability-Report-PDF.pdf)
- Microsoft still targets **carbon negative by 2030**. In 2025 it added **29 CDR projects across 10 pathways**, expected to deliver over **45 Mt** over about three decades. Another outlet describes the same figure as agreements with **21 companies** — [Gasworld](https://www.gasworld.com/story/ai-boom-strengthens-case-for-co2-removal-as-microsoft-emissions-rise-25/2254373.article/); [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/3-things-learned-microsofts-carbon-removal-report/)
- Microsoft's 2026 CDR review paper reports offtakes for about **80 Mt of additional CDR since its previous review in 2023** — [green.earth](https://www.green.earth/news/microsoft-signs-offtakes-for-80-million-tonnes-of-removals); [Microsoft, Updating our Carbon Removal Portfolio](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/Updating-our-Carbon-Removal-Portfolio.pdf)

**Amazon**
- **[Sept 2024]** Amazon joined Bayer, BCG, Capgemini, H&M and the Walmart Foundation in the LEAF Coalition deal with Pará state (Brazil): **5 million jurisdictional REDD+ credits at $15/t (~$180M)** for avoided deforestation in 2023–2026. The US, UK and Norway backstop part of it. The deal was conditional on Indigenous consultation. - There is a clear split. Microsoft, Google and Meta now frame purchases as removals for residual emissions or net zero. Amazon still invests in large-scale jurisdictional avoidance (REDD+) alongside DAC.

## 5. Issues with DAC (cost, energy, DOE hubs, 45Q), permanence and MRV debates, buffer pools

### Takeaway
DAC remains costly ($500–1,000/t) and energy-hungry, and flagship plants are late or under-performing. Climeworks' Mammoth removed just 675 t in H1 2026 against a 36,000 t/yr nameplate capacity. Stratos's full start-up has slipped to end-2026. US policy is mixed: the 2025 OBBBA kept 45Q at $180/t and gave DAC-to-EOR parity. DOE cancelled and then restored the two flagship DAC hubs (April 2026), but disbursements remain minimal.

### Cited Findings
**Cost and performance**
- **Climeworks Mammoth (Iceland):** nameplate **36,000 t/yr**. It captured just over **100 t** in its first full year. Net removal was **119 t in H1 2025** and **675 t in H1 2026**. Climeworks says upgraded units hit a peak of 1.37 t/day, and operating cost per tonne fell over 50% (no absolute figure given). It cut staff in 2025 (reports say **22%** or **10%**) — [Gasworld](https://www.gasworld.com/story/climeworks-upgrades-dac-tech-at-iceland-facility-after-low-co2-capture-record/2259464.article/); [Net Zero Compare](https://netzerocompare.com/articles/climeworks-increases-co2-removal-at-mammoth-as-operating-costs-fall); [Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/climeworks-is-cutting-22%25-of-staff-as-us-climate-backlash-hits-carbon-removal/89379470); [The Chemical Engineer](https://www.thechemicalengineer.com/news/climeworks-to-cut-10-of-staff-amid-slowdown-in-cleantech-confidence/)
- **Stratos (1PointFive/Occidental, Ector County, TX):** designed for up to **500,000 t/yr**. Originally due end-2024, it is now "end of 2026" per Occidental (E&E News, Aug 2026). As of Mar 2026, remaining steps included pellet reactor ramp-up, calciner commissioning and starting CO2 injection — [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/worlds-largest-carbon-removal-plant-to-open-at-the-end-of-2026/); [RBN Energy](https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/analyst-insight/stratos-dac-project-target-q2-2026-startup); [Decarbonfuse](https://decarbonfuse.com/posts/stratos-dac-delay-inside-the-holdup-at-the-world-s-largest-carbon-capture-plant)
- **Energy:** the theoretical minimum is about **250 kWh/tCO2**, and real systems use **4–8x** that. Carbon Engineering's process is cited at about **8.8 GJ thermal plus over 360 kWh electric per t**. Figures vary widely by source — [EHN](https://www.ehn.org/carbon-capture-technology-faces-cost-and-scale-challenges-2668904124.html); [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/05/15/climeworks-dac-fiscal-collapse-the-brutal-reality-of-pulling-carbon-from-the-sky/)

**US policy under the 2025 administration**
- **45Q (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Pub. L. 119-21, July 2025):** keeps **$85/t** for point-source capture and **$180/t** for DAC with geologic storage. It creates **parity for utilisation and EOR**, so DAC-to-EOR rises from $130 to $180/t. It adds Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions and keeps transferability. Critics call EOR parity a subsidy for oil — [Global CCS Institute](https://www.globalccsinstitute.com/u-s-preserves-and-increases-45q-credit-in-one-big-beautiful-bill-act/); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-updates-to-section-45q-tax-credits-for-ccs-and-publicly-traded-partnerships-for-clean-energy.pdf); [Payne Institute](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/keeping-up-with-carbon-key-changes-for-45q-tax-credits-under-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-and-possible-impacts/); [Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/newsroom/making-enhanced-oil-recovery-great-again-obba-45q-tax-credit-shift-cuts-ep-costs-by-40)
- **DOE DAC hubs ($3.5B programme):** in **Oct 2025**, DOE terminated at least 10 of 21 DAC hub projects. An internal list seen by Heatmap marked **South Texas (Occidental)** and **Project Cypress (Louisiana; Heirloom/Climeworks)** as terminated. In **Apr 2026**, DOE confirmed both would **retain funding**. Awards were $500M (South Texas) and $550M (Cypress), but only the first **$50M tranche each** has been paid. The FY27 budget proposes cutting $15.2B of IIJA funds, likely including unobligated hub money. USAspending shows no new funds or contract changes in recent months (CATF, May 2026) — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/politics/doe-dac-hubs); [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/trump-administration-preserves-federal-funding-for-us-dac-and-hydrogen-hubs/); [CATF, May 2026](https://www.catf.us/2026/05/continued-uncertainty-department-energy-circulates-latest-retain-modify-awards-list/); [Carbon180](https://carbon180.org/blog/charting-dac-hubs-progress-two-years-later/)

**Permanence, MRV and buffer pools**
- Kariba showed that a developer can **withdraw from the registry**, leaving excess credits uncompensated. CarbonPlan argues Verra's buffer pool was not designed for excess crediting at this scale — [CarbonPlan](https://carbonplan.org/research/kariba-excess-credits)
- COP30 adopted a flexible reversal standard under Article 6.4, with permanence set per methodology rather than a strict requirement — [Climateseed](https://climateseed.com/blog/key-takeaways-cop30)
- The EU CRCF credits biochar only for carbon expected to remain after **200 years**, and its permanent category is limited to DACCS, BioCCS and biochar. Forestry and soils fall under carbon farming, with time-limited liability — [EC](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming/certification-methodologies_en); [CRCFProjects](https://crcfprojects.com/crcf/)
- Delivery risk is now a mainstream concern. Microsoft and Relae highlighted CDR delivery risk at Climate Week NYC 2026, and Climeworks has partnered with Swiss Re on insurance — [carboncredits.com](https://carboncredits.com/microsoft-and-relae-put-cdr-delivery-risk-in-focus-at-climate-week-nyc-2026/); [Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/climeworks-swiss-re-insurance-carbon-removal/89379470)

### Inferences
- DAC is the most permanent and most measurable pathway, but it currently delivers tiny volumes at high cost. Its main policy support in the US (45Q) now rewards EOR equally, which weakens its climate case as "removal" for buyers who care about net removal.
- Data-centre buyers face a trade-off between **cheap, large-volume ARR** (reversal risk, buffer-pool dependence) and **expensive, scarce DAC/BECCS** (delivery and timing risk). Microsoft's portfolio leans on BECCS as the middle path.
- Delays at Mammoth and Stratos mean contracted DAC tonnes for 2025–2027 are unlikely to be delivered on schedule. This is a key MRV and claims risk for anyone counting them toward 2030 targets.

### Gaps
- No primary measured energy-per-tonne figure for Mammoth or Stratos was found.
- Buffer-pool sizing figures (e.g., Verra AFOLU buffer percentages, reversals drawn down) were not retrieved this session.
- BECCS-specific integrity critiques (biomass sourcing, e.g., Drax and Stockholm Exergi forest-biomass debates) were not researched in this pass.

## 6. Emerging: credits tied to data-centre heat reuse or clean energy, energy attribute certificates, Article 6 deals involving tech, India's CCTS

### Takeaway
I found no evidence of operational carbon-credit products or Article 6 deals tied specifically to data-centre heat reuse or to tech companies. The live emerging battleground is **energy attribute accounting**: the GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision, with Amazon and Meta's "Emissions First" impact-based approach vs hourly matching. Microsoft is piloting data-centre waste heat to power DAC. India's CCTS does not cover data centres.

### Cited Findings
- **GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision (2026):** big tech is split. The **Emissions First Partnership** (Amazon, Meta, Salesforce and others) backs impact or consequential accounting that rewards clean power in dirtier grids. It would accept hourly matching only within "deliverable market boundaries", with a phase-in and recognition of legacy contracts. A group of **64** companies, NGOs and developers asked that hourly matching stay voluntary — [Kitco/Reuters wire, 26 May 2026](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-26/big-tech-splits-over-proposed-shake-clean-energy-accounting)
- The proposal under discussion would require **hourly matching for companies above 5 GWh/yr**, with monthly or annual matching for smaller ones. Nothing was formally decided at the time of writing — [Minimum](https://www.minimum.com/post/the-emerging-shape-of-new-scope-2-rules)
- A Jan 2026 NGO letter backs hourly and local matching and says Meta's Louisiana data centre is driving new gas generation — [EcoStandard letter](https://ecostandard.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026_Letter_GHGP-Scope2.pdf)
- Meta launched "Emissions First" in **2022** with Akamai, Amazon and others — [3BL Media / Meta](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/putting-emissions-first-meta-leads-new-joint-approach-electricity-greenhouse-gas-accounting)
- Microsoft dropped "non-additional" unbundled RECs in FY2025, raising its reported emissions — [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/microsofts-data-center-expansion-drove-25-emissions-spike-2025-environmental-sustainability-report/825082/)
- **Heat reuse:** Microsoft is piloting data-centre waste heat to power DAC and says the system can use **70–85%** of the waste heat — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-pilots-use-of-data-center-waste-heat-to-power-direct-air-capture-systems/)
- An EU-published analysis (Mar 2026) says AI data-centre waste heat could drive DAC or water purification. However, using all of one data centre's heat would need a DAC facility larger than any yet demonstrated — [European Commission, Environment](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/ai-data-centre-waste-heat-could-be-used-water-purification-and-carbon-capture-2026-03-30_en)
- An ASME investment analysis found heat-reuse NPV is "highly favorable" when carbon credits (at $85/t) are included, and positive only for data centres of **2 MW or more** without credits [published about early 2025] — [ASME J. Sustainable Buildings](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/sustainablebuildings/article/6/1/011002/1210426/Data-Center-Waste-Heat-Reuse-An-Investment)
- Germany's Energy Efficiency Act (EnEfG) requires new data centres to reuse **10% of waste heat from 1 Jul 2026**, rising to 15% in 2027 and 20% in 2028 (secondary source) — [Vela Solaris](https://www.velasolaris.com/en/data-center-heat-reuse/)
- **India CCTS:** the compliance sectors listed (counts conflict: 8, 9 or 13) are heavy industries such as aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, fertiliser, iron and steel, pulp and paper, petrochemicals, refining and textiles. The outlier 13-sector list adds railways, commercial buildings and ports. **No source lists data centres.** The **offset mechanism** (from Dec 2023) lets non-obligated entities earn Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs) under BEE-approved methodologies. About **8** are approved, including green hydrogen, pumped hydro, industrial energy efficiency and mangroves — [IETA business brief, Jul 2025](https://www.ieta.org/uploads/wp-content/Resources/Busines-briefs/2025/IETA_Business_Brief-India_July_final-one.pdf); [Metacorp](https://www.metacorp.in/detailed-procedure-for-offset-mechanism); [Carbon Market Network](https://carbonmarketnetwork.com/blog/indias-carbon-credit-trading-scheme-ccts-explained/)
- Google's first India CDR deal was the Varaha biochar offtake (100,000 t). It is a voluntary purchase, not part of CCTS — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-spends-100-million-on-carbon-removal-in-past-year-triple-its-target/)

### Inferences
- For data centres, the emerging fight is about **how clean electricity is counted** (Scope 2 hourly matching vs "emissions-first" impact accounting), not about new credit types. The outcome will move tech companies' reported emissions more than any offset purchase.
- If the EU ETS or CRCF (DACCS/BioCCS) and German heat-reuse mandates combine, heat-to-DAC could eventually create certifiable removals linked to data centres. That is speculative, and there is no evidence of issued credits yet.
- India's data-centre boom sits outside CCTS compliance. Indian data-centre operators could only engage through the voluntary offset mechanism (e.g., buying CCCs) or the international VCM.

### Gaps
- No Article 6.2 or 6.4 deal involving a data-centre operator or Big Tech buyer was found. A targeted search would be needed (e.g., Singapore or Switzerland ITMO deals with tech buyers).
- No examples were found of issued carbon credits for data-centre heat reuse (e.g., under Gold Standard or Verra methodologies).
- Whether the GHG Protocol Scope 2 revision was finalised by Oct 2026, and on what terms, is not confirmed.
- Whether India's CCTS plans to extend to commercial buildings or data centres is not confirmed by a primary BEE or MoP source.
