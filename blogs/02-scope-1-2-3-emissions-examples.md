# Scope 1, 2 and 3 Emissions Explained: 15 Real-World Examples

If you've started measuring your company's carbon footprint, you've met the three "scopes." They come from the **Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol**, the most widely used standard for corporate emissions accounting, and they sort every emission source by how directly your business controls it.

Here's a plain-English breakdown with examples you'll recognise from your own operations.

---

## Scope 1: Direct emissions you own or control

Scope 1 covers greenhouse gases released **directly** from sources your company owns or controls.

### 1. Company-owned vehicles
Diesel and petrol burned by your delivery vans, sales cars, forklifts or trucks.

### 2. On-site boilers and furnaces
Natural gas, LPG, coal or oil burned to heat buildings or run industrial processes.

### 3. Backup generators
Diesel generators that run during power cuts are easy to forget, but they count.

### 4. Refrigerant leaks
Air conditioners, chillers and cold-storage units can leak HFCs. Many of these gases are thousands of times more potent than CO₂, so small leaks matter.

### 5. Process emissions
Chemical reactions in manufacturing that release greenhouse gases directly, for example in cement, steel, chemicals or glass production.

---

## Scope 2: Indirect emissions from purchased energy

Scope 2 covers emissions from generating the **electricity, steam, heat or cooling you buy**. The emissions happen at the power plant, but your consumption causes them.

### 6. Grid electricity for offices and factories
The most common Scope 2 source: every kWh you buy from the grid.

### 7. Purchased steam or district heating
Common in industrial parks and some urban areas.

### 8. Purchased cooling
Chilled water from a district cooling network.

> **Tip:** The GHG Protocol asks companies to report Scope 2 two ways. *Location-based* uses the average emissions of the local grid. *Market-based* reflects the specific energy contracts you choose, such as renewable energy purchases. Reporting both gives a fuller picture.

---

## Scope 3: Everything else in your value chain

Scope 3 covers **all other indirect emissions**, both upstream (suppliers) and downstream (customers and end-of-life). The GHG Protocol groups them into **15 categories**. For many businesses, Scope 3 is by far the biggest share of the total footprint.

### 9. Purchased goods and services
The emissions embedded in raw materials, components, packaging and services you buy. For many manufacturers and retailers, this is the largest single category.

### 10. Upstream transportation and distribution
Third-party freight bringing materials to your sites.

### 11. Business travel
Flights, trains, hotels and rental cars for work trips.

### 12. Employee commuting (and remote work)
How your team gets to work, plus the energy used by people working from home, if you choose to include it.

### 13. Waste generated in operations
Landfill, incineration and treatment of the waste your sites produce.

### 14. Use of sold products
Energy your products use during their lifetime. This is huge for makers of appliances, vehicles and machinery.

### 15. End-of-life treatment of sold products
What happens to your products and packaging when customers throw them away.

---

## Quick reference: Scope 1 vs Scope 2 vs Scope 3

| | Scope 1 | Scope 2 | Scope 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it covers** | Direct emissions from owned or controlled sources | Purchased electricity, steam, heat and cooling | All other value chain emissions |
| **Level of control** | High | Medium | Low to medium (influence) |
| **Typical data source** | Fuel logs, refrigerant records | Utility bills, energy contracts | Supplier data, spend data, logistics and travel records |
| **Reporting** | Usually required | Usually required | Increasingly expected |

---

## Why getting the scopes right matters

- **Credibility:** Misclassified or missing sources undermine your report with auditors and investors.
- **No double counting:** Clear boundaries stop the same emission being counted twice.
- **Better decisions:** Knowing where emissions come from shows you where reductions are cheapest and fastest.
- **Customer requests:** Your Scope 1 and 2 are your customers' Scope 3. Accurate data helps them, and helps you stay their supplier.

### FAQ

**Is Scope 3 reporting mandatory?**
It depends on your jurisdiction, sector and size. Requirements are expanding in several regions, and many large customers already ask suppliers for Scope 3-related data. Check the rules that apply to you, or [ask our team](https://thegreensolve.com/contact.php).

**Which scope is usually the largest?**
For most companies, especially in retail, consumer goods, technology and services, Scope 3 is the largest. Heavy industry often has large Scope 1 emissions too.

**Do I have to measure all 15 Scope 3 categories?**
Start by screening all 15 to see which are relevant and significant for your business, then focus your data collection on the biggest ones.
