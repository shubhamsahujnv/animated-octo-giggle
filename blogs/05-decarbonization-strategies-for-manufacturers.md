# 10 Practical Decarbonization Strategies for Manufacturers

Manufacturing is one of the most energy- and emissions-intensive sectors. That also makes it one of the sectors with the most room to improve. The good news is that many decarbonization measures **pay for themselves** through lower energy and material costs.

Here are ten practical strategies, ordered roughly from quick wins to long-term transformation.

---

## 1. Start with an accurate baseline

You can't reduce what you haven't measured. A Scope 1, 2 and 3 baseline shows which sites, processes and suppliers drive your emissions, so you put money where it has the most impact.

## 2. Hunt down energy waste

Low-cost operational fixes often deliver the fastest results:

- Fix compressed air leaks, a notorious and often invisible energy drain
- Turn off idle machines and lighting outside production hours
- Optimise HVAC schedules and set points
- Insulate steam lines, valves and hot surfaces

## 3. Upgrade to high-efficiency equipment

When equipment reaches end of life, replace it with efficient options:

- High-efficiency motors and **variable speed drives (VSDs)** on pumps, fans and compressors
- LED lighting with occupancy and daylight sensors
- Modern, right-sized boilers and chillers

## 4. Recover and reuse waste heat

Many processes release large amounts of heat. Heat exchangers and heat recovery systems can capture it to preheat water, combustion air or other process streams, cutting fuel use directly.

## 5. Switch to renewable electricity

Reduce Scope 2 emissions through:

- **On-site solar** on rooftops, car parks or unused land
- **Power purchase agreements (PPAs)** with renewable generators
- **Green tariffs or renewable energy certificates** where direct options aren't available

## 6. Electrify process heat where possible

Fossil-fuelled heat is a major source of Scope 1 emissions. Industrial heat pumps, electric boilers and induction heating are now viable for many low- and medium-temperature processes. Combined with clean electricity, they can cut emissions sharply.

## 7. Fix refrigerant leaks and choose low-GWP alternatives

Refrigerant leaks can have an outsized climate impact. Put in regular leak detection, and when replacing equipment, choose systems that use **low-global-warming-potential refrigerants**.

## 8. Use less material, and lower-carbon material

For many manufacturers, purchased materials are the largest Scope 3 category. Options include:

- Designing products to use less material
- Increasing recycled content
- Cutting scrap and rework through process optimisation
- Sourcing lower-carbon alternatives, such as recycled metals or lower-carbon cement and plastics

## 9. Engage your suppliers

You can't decarbonise your value chain alone. Ask key suppliers for emissions data, include carbon criteria in procurement and work with them on reduction targets. Start with the suppliers behind most of your Scope 3 footprint.

## 10. Explore carbon credit pathways, the right way

Some decarbonization projects, such as energy efficiency upgrades, fuel switching or renewable installations, may qualify to generate **carbon credits**. That can create an extra revenue stream that improves project payback.

> **Keep it credible:** Carbon credits don't erase the emissions in your inventory. Use them as part of a broader decarbonization strategy, never as a substitute for real reductions.

---

## Turning strategy into action

The most successful manufacturers treat decarbonization as an ongoing programme, not a one-off project:

1. **Measure:** Build and maintain an accurate emissions inventory
2. **Prioritise:** Rank opportunities by carbon impact, cost and payback
3. **Act:** Implement, starting with quick wins to build momentum
4. **Track:** Watch results continuously and report progress openly

### FAQ

**What's the quickest way for a factory to cut emissions?**
Energy efficiency, such as fixing compressed air leaks, adding variable speed drives and optimising heating and cooling, usually gives the fastest, cheapest reductions.

**Does decarbonization increase costs?**
Some measures need upfront investment, but many, especially efficiency upgrades, pay back through lower energy bills. Carbon credits and incentives can improve the economics further.

**Where should manufacturers start with Scope 3?**
Start with purchased goods and materials and with transportation. These are usually the largest categories, and supplier engagement there has the most effect.
