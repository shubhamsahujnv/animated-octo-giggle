# 10 Signs Your Business Has Outgrown Carbon Accounting Spreadsheets

Most companies start measuring their carbon footprint in a spreadsheet. That's fine for a first estimate. But as investors, customers and regulators ask for more detail, spreadsheets start to fail, often without anyone noticing.

If any of the signs below sound familiar, it may be time to move to a dedicated **carbon accounting platform**.

---

## 1. Your emissions data lives in a dozen different places

Fuel invoices sit with finance, electricity bills with facilities, travel data with HR, and supplier data in someone's inbox. Each reporting cycle starts with a scavenger hunt.

A carbon accounting platform connects to the business tools you already use and collects activity data automatically. Your inventory is built from one source of truth, not copy-and-paste.

## 2. Only one person understands "the carbon file"

If your footprint depends on one colleague's spreadsheet, formulas and memory, that's a single point of failure. When that person changes roles or leaves, your baseline goes with them.

## 3. You can't explain how a number was calculated

Auditors, lenders and enterprise customers increasingly ask, "Where did this figure come from?" If you can't trace a tonne of CO₂e back to the activity data and emission factor behind it, your report is hard to defend.

## 4. Your Scope 3 section is mostly blank (or mostly guesses)

For many businesses, Scope 3, the indirect emissions in the value chain, is the largest part of the footprint. It's also the hardest to measure in a spreadsheet because it spans purchased goods, logistics, business travel, commuting, waste and use of sold products.

Purpose-built tools give you structured methods for each Scope 3 category and help you move from spend-based estimates to supplier-specific data over time.

## 5. Emission factors haven't been updated since you first built the model

Emission factors change. Grid electricity gets cleaner, databases are revised, and methods improve. Hard-coded factors in old spreadsheets quietly make your numbers less accurate every year.

## 6. Customers are sending you sustainability questionnaires

When large buyers start asking for your product carbon footprint or your Scope 1 and 2 emissions, they're usually working on their own Scope 3. Fast, consistent answers can help you win and keep contracts.

## 7. You need to align with a recognised standard

Saying "we measured our emissions" isn't enough anymore. Stakeholders want to know if your inventory follows the **GHG Protocol** or **ISO 14064-1**. Platforms built around these standards enforce consistent boundaries, categories and methods.

## 8. Reporting season takes weeks instead of days

If building your annual sustainability report means late nights reconciling tabs, the process is costing you time you could spend on reduction work.

## 9. You can't see progress month to month

A once-a-year footprint tells you where you've been, not where you're going. Continuous tracking shows whether your efficiency projects, renewable energy purchases or supplier changes are working, while there's still time to adjust.

## 10. You're planning net-zero targets or carbon credits

Setting a credible reduction target, or checking whether a project could earn carbon credits, needs a reliable, auditable baseline. You can't manage what you can't measure, and you can't claim what you can't prove.

### FAQ

**Is a spreadsheet ever enough for carbon accounting?**
For a very small business doing a first rough estimate, sometimes. Once you need Scope 3, regular updates, audit-ready figures or customer reporting, a dedicated platform saves time and reduces risk.

**What's the difference between carbon accounting and ESG reporting?**
Carbon accounting measures greenhouse gas emissions. ESG reporting is broader and covers environmental, social and governance performance. Your carbon inventory is usually the core input to the "E" in ESG.

**How long does it take to switch from spreadsheets?**
It depends on how many sites and data sources you have, but connecting existing systems means most of the heavy lifting is automated after setup.
