# 7 Steps to Build an ISO 14064-Aligned Carbon Baseline for Your Factory

Every credible climate plan starts with one thing: a **baseline**. It's the reference point you measure future reductions against. It's also the foundation for net-zero targets, customer reporting and any carbon credit project you might pursue.

For manufacturers, building a baseline can feel overwhelming. You have multiple sites, energy-intensive processes and long supply chains. These seven steps make it manageable and keep it aligned with **ISO 14064-1**, the international standard for organisation-level greenhouse gas inventories.

---

## Step 1: Define your organisational boundary

Decide which facilities and entities your inventory covers. ISO 14064-1 and the GHG Protocol allow two main approaches:

- **Control approach:** Include 100% of emissions from operations you control, either financially or operationally.
- **Equity share approach:** Include emissions in proportion to your ownership stake.

Choose one, write it down and apply it consistently. Most manufacturers choose operational control.

## Step 2: Set your reporting boundary and emission sources

List every emission source inside that boundary and classify it:

- **Direct (Scope 1):** Fuel combustion in boilers, furnaces, kilns and on-site vehicles, process emissions and refrigerant leaks
- **Energy indirect (Scope 2):** Purchased electricity, steam, heat and cooling
- **Other indirect (Scope 3):** Purchased materials, inbound and outbound logistics, waste, business travel, use and end-of-life of sold products

A site walk-through with plant engineers often finds sources nobody has on paper, like a standby generator or a forgotten process gas.

## Step 3: Choose your base year

Pick a recent, representative year with reliable data. Avoid years distorted by unusual events like major shutdowns or one-off production spikes.

Also write a **recalculation policy**: the rules for when you'll restate the baseline, for example after an acquisition, a divestment or a significant change in methodology.

## Step 4: Collect activity data

This is where most of the effort goes. Gather:

- Fuel purchase records and meter readings
- Electricity and utility bills for every site
- Refrigerant top-up and maintenance logs
- Production volumes, so you can calculate intensity metrics later
- Procurement spend and quantities by material
- Freight, travel and waste records

**Tip:** Connecting your ERP, utility and procurement systems directly to a carbon accounting platform cuts manual data entry and reduces errors.

## Step 5: Apply appropriate emission factors

Convert activity data into tonnes of CO₂-equivalent (tCO₂e) using recognised emission factors. Use factors that fit your geography, energy source and year. Record the source and version of every factor you use.

## Step 6: Assess uncertainty and data quality

ISO 14064-1 expects you to understand how reliable your numbers are. For each major source, note whether the data is:

- Measured, such as metered fuel
- Calculated from reliable records, such as invoices
- Estimated, such as spend-based Scope 3

Flag the weakest areas. Improving them is your data roadmap for future years.

## Step 7: Document everything and prepare for verification

A baseline is only as credible as its documentation. Keep a GHG inventory report that records your boundaries, base year, methods, factors, assumptions and exclusions.

If you plan to make public claims, set targets or pursue carbon credits, consider **third-party verification** to ISO 14064-3. A clean audit trail makes verification faster and cheaper.

---

## What your baseline unlocks

Once your baseline is in place, you can:

1. **Set reduction targets** grounded in real data
2. **Prioritise decarbonization projects** by cost and impact
3. **Answer customer and investor requests** with confidence
4. **Screen projects for carbon credit pathways**, where energy efficiency, fuel switching or process changes may qualify

> **A note on carbon credits:** Credits don't remove the emissions in your own inventory. They work best as one part of a wider decarbonization strategy, not a replacement for cutting emissions at source.

### FAQ

**What's the difference between ISO 14064 and the GHG Protocol?**
They're closely aligned. The GHG Protocol is a widely used corporate accounting framework. ISO 14064 is an international standard with formal requirements that suit third-party verification. Many companies design their inventory to meet both.

**How long does it take to build a baseline?**
For a single-site manufacturer with organised records, a few weeks. Multi-site operations with complex supply chains take longer. Automation shortens it a lot.

**Should Scope 3 be in my baseline?**
Yes, if it's significant, and for most manufacturers it is. Start with the largest categories, such as purchased materials and logistics, and refine over time.
