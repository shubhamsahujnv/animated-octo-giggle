## 1 · AI's power bill (01-ai-power-bill)

AI's electricity use grew 50% in a single year.

The IEA's 2026 numbers:
→ AI-focused data centres: +50% in 2025
→ All data centres: 485 TWh in 2025, ~950 TWh projected by 2030
→ That's close to 3% of the world's electricity

Chips are getting 30–40% more efficient every year. Demand is growing faster.

What it means if you run a plant: grid connections get contested, your Scope 2 factor gets harder to predict, and your own AI and cloud use belongs in Scope 3.

Measure first. Then decide.

## 2 · AI's water bill (02-ai-water-bill)

Every AI answer has a water bill.

In India, about two-thirds of data centre capacity sits in Mumbai and Chennai. Both cities are already water-stressed.

→ ~150 billion litres used in 2025
→ ~358 billion litres projected by 2030
→ No major state data centre policy requires operators to report water use

Meanwhile, India's top listed companies already report water under BRSR.

You can't manage what you don't measure.

## 3 · AI vs net zero (03-ai-vs-net-zero)

AI is breaking Big Tech's climate maths.

→ Meta: net emissions up 45% in 2025, and it's rethinking the timing of its 2030 net-zero goal
→ Google: supply-chain (Scope 3) emissions up 25%, driven by hardware and data centre construction

The footprint isn't just the electricity. It's the steel, concrete and chips, and that's where targets quietly break.

Three lessons for any company with a target:
01 Model growth and targets together
02 Measure Scope 3 early
03 Cut emissions first, offset what's left

Know your baseline before it moves.
