## 1 · Who pays for AI's power? (01-who-pays-for-ai-power)

AI's electricity has to be paid for by someone. On America's largest grid, part of the bill is landing on households and factories.

→ PJM capacity price: $28.92 → $329.17 per MW-day in two years (11×)
→ The market monitor attributed $9.3B of the $14.7B 2025/26 cost (about 63%) to data centre demand
→ Estimated +$16/month on Ohio bills and +$18/month in western Maryland

For manufacturers, your capacity charge can be set by just five peak hours a year. Shave the peak, cut the cost.

## 2 · Data centres are throwing away free heat (02-data-centre-waste-heat)

Almost every kilowatt-hour a server uses turns into heat. In the EU, only 1.9% of that heat gets reused.

→ If half were reused, it could heat 4 million European homes
→ Germany now requires new data centres to reuse 10% of their waste heat from 2026, rising to 20% from 2028
→ Tallaght, Dublin: a heat network running on AWS waste heat saved 1,100 tonnes of CO₂ in its first year

Your plant probably wastes heat too. Recovered heat that replaces gas or coal is a measurable Scope 1 cut.

## 3 · Big Tech is going nuclear (03-big-tech-goes-nuclear)

Microsoft is restarting a reactor at Three Mile Island. Google has signed for small modular reactors. Amazon and Meta have their own nuclear deals.

Why? AI runs 24/7. Solar and wind don't.

The catch: buying an existing plant's power doesn't add clean energy, it just moves it. And most new reactors are still years away.

Three questions to ask about any "clean power" claim:
01 Is it new or existing capacity?
02 What do location-based and market-based Scope 2 say?
03 Can you use less first?
