## 06 · Your clean-power certificates may soon need a timestamp

Buying a year of solar certificates can cover your night shift, on paper.

The GHG Protocol is proposing three tests for clean-power claims:
→ Hourly: matched hour by hour, not over the year
→ Deliverable: from the grid region where you use the power
→ Incremental: from new clean capacity (strictest method)

It's still a proposal. The consultation closed in January 2026, and a survey on a two-method approach ran Jul–Sep 2026. Industry pushback is strong.

Why AI matters: data centres draw as much power at 3 a.m. as at noon.

Get ready now: collect hourly meter data, report location-based and market-based Scope 2, and check which grid region your certificates come from.

## 07 · The AI boom is running on gas turbines

The grid can't connect data centres fast enough, so some are building their own power.

→ 41 gas turbines (about 1.2 GW) approved for xAI in Southaven, Mississippi
→ Turbines ran near Memphis before permits were in place
→ January 2026: EPA closed the loophole they relied on
→ SpaceX plans to spend $2.8bn on gas turbines for its data centres over three years
→ Virginia: 180+ air permits for data centre diesel backup generators, almost all just under the 100-ton limit. From July 2026, new permit applications must meet Tier 4-equivalent limits.

Your engines count too. Backup generators are Scope 1, so log every test run.

## 08 · What if data centres could switch off for a few hours a year?

A Duke University study found the US grid could take on:
→ 76 GW of new load if data centres curtail 0.25% of the year (about 22 hours)
→ 98 GW at 0.5%
→ 126 GW at 1%

Google already has 1 GW of demand response written into utility contracts.

The catch: in grids with little renewable power, flexible data centres could keep fossil plants running longer.

Factories have done this for decades. Find your flexible loads and shift them to cleaner hours.

The cleanest megawatt is the one you move.
