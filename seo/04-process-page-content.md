## H1 — Our custom software development process: five stages, each with a defined exit

Most vendors describe a process that is really a sales diagram. This is the actual one. Each of
the five stages below has a defined exit condition and a deliverable you keep, whether or not
you continue to the next stage.

That last point is the one that matters. You are never holding a half-finished asset you cannot
use because the engagement stopped.

## H2 — Why a defined process matters more than a methodology

Target `software development process methodology` (880 / SD 34) here, and answer the question
buyers are actually asking — not "are you agile" but "will this land on time and will I know if
it is not".

Short section, three or four paragraphs. The argument: agile and waterfall are both fine; what
fails projects is undefined exits, so nobody can tell whether a stage finished. We fix the exit
conditions instead of the ceremony.

## H2 — The five stages of our software development process

Covers `software development process stages` (320) and `steps in software development process`
(170). Keep this as a scannable ordered list before the detail — that structure is what wins
list snippets.

### H3 — Stage 1: Discover

**Exit:** a written scope and a cost envelope.
**You keep:** the scope document and the cost model.

What happens: stakeholder interviews, constraint mapping, a review of what already exists, and
a hard look at whether the thing you asked for is the thing you need. Typically one to two weeks.

Discovery ends with a number. Not a range with a disclaimer — a cost envelope with the
assumptions written down, so you can tell when something changes.

[More on discovery →](/process/discover)

### H3 — Stage 2: Architect

**Exit:** an architecture decision record.
**You keep:** the ADR, the diagrams and the infrastructure plan.

Technology choices, data model, integration boundaries, security posture and the cloud
architecture. Every significant decision is recorded with the alternatives considered and why
they were rejected — so in eighteen months, when someone asks why, there is an answer.

This is also where cost is decided. [Cloud cost is an architecture
problem](/blog/cloud-cost-is-an-architecture-problem) explains why that conversation cannot wait
until the bill arrives. If you are modernising rather than starting fresh, the
[refactor, rebuild or replace](/blog/legacy-modernization-refactor-rebuild-replace) decision
happens here.

[More on architecture →](/process/architect)

### H3 — Stage 3: Build

**Exit:** a working increment every two weeks, demoed.
**You keep:** the code, in your repository, from day one.

Two-week increments against a working demo, not a status report. Trunk-based, reviewed, with
CI from the first commit. You have commit access throughout — there is no big reveal at the end,
because there is nothing being hidden until then.

[More on build →](/process/build)

### H3 — Stage 4: Harden

**Exit:** security, performance and reliability targets met and evidenced.
**You keep:** the test suites, load profiles and the security review.

Load testing, security review, failure-mode testing, observability and runbooks. This is the
stage most vendors skip, and it is the reason their software works in a demo and falls over in
week three. Zero-trust can be done
[without bringing delivery to a halt](/blog/zero-trust-without-stopping-delivery).

[More on hardening →](/process/harden)

### H3 — Stage 5: Scale & operate

**Exit:** handover complete, your team running it, or us operating it under an agreed SLA.
**You keep:** everything. All code, all IP, all infrastructure definitions, all documentation.

Capacity planning, cost optimisation, on-call, and the knowledge transfer that makes your team
genuinely independent of us. If you would rather we kept operating it, we will — but it is your
choice, not a dependency we engineered.

[More on scale and operate →](/process/scale-and-operate)

## H2 — Process questions, answered

FAQPage schema.

### H3 — Is this agile or waterfall?

Agile inside each stage, staged overall. The stage gates exist so you can stop, not so we can
invoice.

### H3 — What if we want to stop after discovery?

Then you stop, and you keep the scope and the cost envelope. That is the point of the exits.

### H3 — How long does each stage take?

Discovery one to two weeks. Architecture one to three. Build in two-week increments against
scope. Hardening two to four. Give real numbers here rather than "it depends".

### H3 — Who do we actually talk to?

The senior engineers doing the work. There is no account manager layer.

### H3 — What happens at handover?

Covered in [scale and operate](/process/scale-and-operate): documentation, runbooks, knowledge
transfer sessions and a defined support taper.

### H3 — Can you work inside our existing process?

Yes — that is what [specialist augmentation](/engagement-models/staff-augmentation) is for.
