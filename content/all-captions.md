## LinkedIn caption

By 2030, global data centres could consume more electricity than Japan does today.

The IEA estimates data centres used ~415 TWh in 2024 (about 1.5% of global electricity) and projects ~945 TWh by 2030, with AI as the main driver.

India is part of this story: about 1.5 GW of capacity in 2025 (MeitY), roughly half of it in Mumbai, and projections of 4.5 GW+ across the top seven cities by 2030 (Colliers).

## Instagram caption

The world threw away 62 million tonnes of electronics in a single year. Only about 1 in 5 tonnes was properly recycled. ♻️

Data centres add to the pile: servers, drives, switches and batteries are replaced every few years. Inside them is both treasure (gold, copper) and trouble (lead, mercury).

The hopeful part: India's E-Waste Rules make producers responsible for recycling through registered recyclers. Extend, reuse, recover, recycle.

Save this, and share it with your IT team.

#EWaste #CircularEconomy #DataCentres #Recycling #Sustainability #India #GreenTech #TheGreenSolve

## LinkedIn caption

62 million tonnes of e-waste were generated globally in 2022, and only 22.3% was documented as properly recycled (UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024). India's reported e-waste nearly doubled between 2017–18 and 2024–25 (CPCB data).

Data centre hardware is part of this stream. India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, in force since April 2023, set Extended Producer Responsibility targets met only through CPCB-registered recyclers.

Four questions sustainability teams can ask IT partners:
• Do you offer end-of-life take-back?
• Are your recyclers registered on the CPCB portal?
• How is data securely wiped?
• Can refurbished equipment meet some of our needs?

# Carousel 04: The data centre efficiency score (PUE)

**Hook (cover):** "One number tells you how much energy a data centre wastes."
**Format:** 9 slides, 1080×1350. Instagram: `png/`. LinkedIn: `carousel.pdf` as a document post.

## Instagram caption

One number tells you how much energy a data centre wastes: PUE. 📊

PUE = total facility energy ÷ energy used by the computers. 1.0 is perfect. The world average has been stuck above 1.5 for six years. The best facilities get close to 1.1.

India's heat and humidity make it harder, but smart design closes the gap: contained airflow, warmer setpoints, liquid cooling and efficient power systems.

And remember: PUE isn't the whole story. Ask about water and renewable share too.

#DataCentres #EnergyEfficiency #PUE #Sustainability #India #GreenTech #ClimateAction #TheGreenSolve

## LinkedIn caption

Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) is the most widely used data centre efficiency metric: total facility energy divided by IT equipment energy.

• At 2.0, every unit of computing power needs one more unit for cooling and losses
• At 1.1, it needs a tenth
• Globally, the weighted average has hovered above 1.5 for six years (Uptime Institute, 2025)

In India's hot, humid hubs, design choices matter even more.

But PUE alone can mislead: an efficient facility can still run on coal power or use a lot of water. We recommend asking for three numbers in every contract: measured annual PUE, water use (WUE) and renewable electricity share.

**Hook (cover):** "Can India's cloud run on India's sunshine?"

## Instagram caption

Can India's cloud run on India's sunshine? ☀️

India has 300 GW+ of clean power capacity, led by 164 GW of solar, and added a record 55 GW in a single year.

The catch: data centres never sleep, and the sun does. The answer is a mix of solar, wind and storage, plus rules that make buying green power easier.

India has the sun, the wind and the rules. Now the cloud needs to plug in. 🔌

#SolarEnergy #RenewableEnergy #DataCentres #CleanEnergy #India #EnergyTransition #GreenTech #TheGreenSolve

## LinkedIn caption

India's non-fossil capacity reached 300.5 GW by 31 July 2026, more than 54% of installed capacity, with solar at 164.6 GW (MNRE). FY 2025–26 saw a record 55.3 GW of clean capacity added.

For data centres, the Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 lowered the eligibility threshold from 1 MW to 100 kW, set a 15-day approval window and enabled banking of surplus green energy.

The open challenge is round-the-clock supply. Annual renewable matching is a good start; 24×7 hourly matching is the gold standard, and it drives investment in wind and storage.

Three questions for your cloud provider: what share of your power in India is renewable? Is it matched annually or hourly? And through which route?

## LinkedIn caption

The internet is thirsty, and in India that matters.

Data centres turn almost all of their electricity into heat, and many rely on water to remove it. A 2025 Planet Tracker analysis (reported by Climate Fact Checks) found up to 50 Indian data centres sit in regions of extremely high water stress, including major hubs like Chennai and Mumbai.

The solutions already exist:
• Treated wastewater instead of fresh water
• Air-cooled and closed-loop cooling that need far less water
• BEE's 2023 cooling guidelines, which recommend treated water for cooling-tower make-up

As India's data centre capacity grows, water-wise cooling should be a baseline design choice, not an afterthought.
