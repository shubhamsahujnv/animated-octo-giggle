# Data centres & the environment: Fact checks and FAQs

India-focused, hopeful, evidence-led copy for the TGS series. Every number below has already been used and sourced in the published carousels and videos. Re-check figures on the day of posting, because energy and capacity data update often.

**Suggested formats**
- **Fact checks:** a "Myth or Fact?" carousel (one myth per slide: claim → verdict → the facts → source) or 20–30 s reels (hook = the myth, reveal = the verdict).
- **FAQs:** an "Ask The Green Solve" carousel series (one question per slide), LinkedIn text posts, or a pinned FAQ highlight on Instagram.

## Part 1: Fact checks (Myth vs Fact)

### 1. "The cloud isn't a real place."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
The "cloud" is thousands of physical buildings full of servers, running 24×7. India alone has about 1.5 GW of data centre capacity, and roughly half of it is in Mumbai.
*Sources: MeitY (2025); CBRE (Sept 2025).*

### 2. "Data centres use most of the world's electricity."
**Verdict: ⚠️ Exaggerated**
In 2024, data centres used about 1.5% of global electricity (around 415 TWh). That's small today, but the IEA expects it to more than double to about 945 TWh by 2030.
*Source: IEA, Energy and AI (2025).*

### 3. "AI is only a small part of the data centre story."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
The IEA names AI as the biggest driver of data centre electricity growth to 2030. Electricity use by AI-focused (accelerated) servers is projected to grow about 30% a year in its base case.
*Source: IEA, Energy and AI (2025).*

### 4. "Data centres don't use water."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
Almost all the electricity servers use turns into heat, and many data centres use water to remove it. A 2025 analysis found up to 50 Indian data centres in regions of extremely high water stress.
*Source: Planet Tracker (2025), as reported by Climate Fact Checks.*

### 5. "Cooling a data centre always means using fresh drinking water."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
Treated wastewater, air-cooled systems and closed-loop cooling can cut freshwater use sharply. India's Bureau of Energy Efficiency (2023) guidelines recommend treated water for cooling-tower make-up.
*Source: Bureau of Energy Efficiency (2023).*

### 6. "India's electricity is now mostly clean."
**Verdict: ⚖️ Half true**
More than 54% of India's installed power capacity is now non-fossil (300.5 GW as of 31 July 2026). But capacity isn't generation: coal still produces most of India's electricity, especially at night.
*Source: MNRE (31 July 2026).*

### 7. "Old servers just end up in landfill."
**Verdict: ⚖️ Not necessarily**
Globally, only 22.3% of e-waste was formally recycled in 2022. In India, the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 (in force since April 2023) make producers meet recycling targets through CPCB-registered recyclers.
*Sources: UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024; MoEFCC, E-Waste Rules 2022.*

### 8. "A low PUE means a data centre is green."
**Verdict: ⚠️ Misleading**
PUE measures energy efficiency only: how much power goes to overhead such as cooling. A very efficient facility can still run on coal power or use a lot of water. The global average PUE has hovered above 1.5 for six years.
*Source: Uptime Institute, Global Data Center Survey 2025.*

### 9. "Buying renewable energy means a data centre runs on clean power 24×7."
**Verdict: ⚠️ Not always**
Many buyers match renewable purchases annually, so total clean energy bought equals total use over the year. Running on clean power in every hour (24×7 matching) is harder and is the gold standard, because solar doesn't produce at night.

### 10. "Only giant companies can buy green power in India."
**Verdict: ❌ Outdated**
India's Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022 cut the eligibility threshold from 1 MW to 100 kW, set a 15-day approval window and allow surplus green energy to be banked. State rules vary.
*Source: Ministry of Power, Green Energy Open Access Rules, 2022.*

### 11. "India's data centre boom is just hype."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
Demand is real: 958 million active internet users (IAMAI–Kantar, 2025), 24.07 billion UPI transactions in September 2026 alone (NPCI), data protection rules phasing in through 2027, and the ₹10,372 crore IndiaAI Mission. Capacity in the top seven cities is projected to pass 4.5 GW by 2030 (Colliers).

### 12. "India can't power data centres with renewables at scale."
**Verdict: ❌ Myth**
India added a record 55.3 GW of clean capacity in FY 2025–26, including 44.6 GW of solar, and has passed 60% of its 500 GW non-fossil target for 2030. The challenge is round-the-clock supply, which wind, storage and hybrid contracts are addressing.
*Source: MNRE (2026).*

## Part 2: FAQs

**1. What is a data centre?**
A building full of computers (servers) that store, process and deliver the data behind banking, streaming, cloud apps and AI, every second of every day.

**2. How much electricity do data centres use?**
About 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity. The IEA projects about 945 TWh by 2030, slightly more than Japan's total electricity use today. *(IEA, Energy and AI, 2025)*

**3. How big is India's data centre sector?**
About 1.5 GW of capacity in 2025 (MeitY). Mumbai hosts roughly half, followed by Chennai, Delhi-NCR and Bengaluru (CBRE, Sept 2025). Capacity in the top seven cities is projected to exceed 4.5 GW by 2030 (Colliers).

**4. Why is India building so many data centres?**
Five forces: more people online, everyday life going digital, rules to keep data in India, the AI race, and international subsea cables landing in coastal cities like Mumbai and Chennai.

**5. Why do data centres need so much cooling?**
Servers turn almost all the electricity they use into heat. Without cooling they would overheat and fail. In India's hot, humid hubs, cooling systems work harder for more of the year.

**6. Do data centres use water?**
Many do, mainly for cooling. That matters in water-stressed cities. Treated wastewater, air-cooled and closed-loop systems can greatly reduce freshwater use.

**7. What is PUE, and what is a good score?**
Power Usage Effectiveness = total facility energy ÷ IT equipment energy. 1.0 is perfect. The global average is a little above 1.5 (Uptime Institute, 2025), and the most efficient large facilities report figures close to 1.1.

**8. What is WUE?**
Water Usage Effectiveness: litres of water used per unit of IT energy. It's best read alongside PUE and renewable electricity share.

**9. Can data centres in India run on renewable energy?**
Increasingly, yes. The main routes are long-term green power contracts via open access, captive or group-captive solar and wind plants, on-site solar, and green tariffs from local utilities where offered.

**10. What happens to old data centre hardware?**
Servers, drives, switches and backup batteries are replaced every few years. Best practice is to extend their life, reuse or refurbish, recover parts, and recycle only through registered recyclers after secure data wiping.

**11. Does AI make data centres less sustainable?**
AI increases electricity demand. Three levers help: more efficient chips, right-sized models for everyday tasks, and clean power.

**12. What should sustainability teams ask their data centre or cloud providers?**
- Measured annual PUE (not just the design figure)
- Water use and water source
- Share of renewable electricity in India, and whether it's matched annually or hourly
- End-of-life take-back and use of CPCB-registered recyclers

**13. What can individuals do?**
Individual habits help at the margin, for example clearing unused cloud storage and streaming at sensible quality. The biggest gains come from companies and policy: clean power, efficient cooling, water-wise design and circular hardware. Ask the brands you use what they're doing.
