### H1: Offshore vs onshore software development: an honest comparison

If you're in the US or Australia, an offshore team can cost a fraction of a local one. You've probably also heard the stories: missed deadlines, silence, code nobody can maintain. Both are true. Whether offshore works depends far more on *how* the team works than on *where* it sits.

*Full disclosure: much of Pixorama's engineering team is in India. We'll tell you when offshore is the wrong choice too.*

---

### H2: Definitions

- **Onshore:** a team in your own country.
- **Nearshore:** a team in a nearby country with similar hours. For the US, that's Latin America. For Australia, it's often South-East Asia or New Zealand.
- **Offshore:** a team further away with a bigger time difference. For US and Australian clients, India is the most common offshore location.

---

### H2: Where offshore goes wrong, and why

The offshore horror stories have predictable causes:

1. **Juniors doing senior work.** A senior person sells the project and juniors build it.
2. **No ownership.** The vendor delivers tickets, not outcomes. Nobody's accountable for the result.
3. **Silent problems.** Issues aren't raised until a deadline is missed.
4. **Code held hostage.** Code lives in the vendor's repo until final payment.
5. **Lowest-price selection.** The cheapest bid usually wins, then pays for itself in rework.

None of these are about geography. They're about how the vendor is set up, and they happen onshore too.

---

### H2: How to make offshore work

If you go offshore, insist on:

- **Named senior people** you meet before signing, with replacement terms in the contract
- **Code in your repository** from day one
- **Working software every two weeks**, not status reports
- **A daily overlap window** for stand-ups and questions
- **Written-first communication**: clear tickets, PR descriptions, weekly written updates
- **One accountable lead** you can call
- **IP assignment and governing law** you're comfortable with

### H2: When onshore is the better choice

Offshore isn't always right. Choose onshore (or nearshore) when:

- The work needs **constant real-time collaboration** across your whole working day.
- **Regulation or contracts require** work, data or people to be onshore.
- You need people **physically on site** regularly.
- Your internal team has **no capacity to manage remote work** at all, and you can't hand over ownership either.

---

### H2: The hybrid model

Many companies end up with a blend: an onshore product owner or tech lead, with an offshore engineering team. Or an offshore team with overlap hours, plus occasional in-person workshops. It keeps the cost advantage while reducing communication risk.

### H2: Frequently asked questions

**Is offshore software development cheaper?**
Usually, yes, often significantly for comparable seniority. Rework from a poor vendor can erase the saving, which is why vendor selection matters more than location.

**Is code quality worse offshore?**
Not inherently. Quality depends on the team's seniority and practices. Ask to see a pipeline, a code review and production work they still run.

**How do you handle time-zone differences?**
A daily overlap window for live conversations, plus written-first communication so work continues across the gap.

**What about IP and security?**
Make sure the contract assigns IP to you and the code lives in your repo. Ask about access controls, devices and security practices.

**Is nearshore better than offshore?**
Nearshore gives more overlap at a higher cost. If you need real-time collaboration all day, it can be worth it. If async-friendly delivery works for you, offshore usually gives more for the budget.
