## Page copy

### H1: Staff augmentation vs a project team vs managed services: which do you need?

All three bring outside engineers into your work. The difference is **who manages the work and who's accountable for the result.** Get that wrong and you either end up managing contractors you don't have time for, or handing over control you needed to keep.

---

### H2: The one-line difference

- **Staff augmentation:** you get people. *You* manage the work and own the outcome.
- **Project team (dedicated team or fixed-scope project):** you get a team that owns delivery. *They* manage the work, and you set priorities and accept the result.
- **Managed services:** you hand over an ongoing function, such as running infrastructure or support, under an SLA. *They* own the function.

---

### H2: Side-by-side

| | Staff augmentation | Project team | Managed services |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What you buy** | Engineer time and skills | A delivered outcome or roadmap | An operated function with an SLA |
| **Who directs daily work** | You | The vendor's lead | The vendor |
| **Who owns delivery risk** | You | Vendor (mostly) | Vendor |
| **Your management effort** | High | Medium | Low |
| **Control over how it's done** | Full | Shared | Low |
| **Speed to start** | Fastest (~2 weeks) | 3 to 4 weeks | Varies |
| **Commercials** | Monthly per person | Monthly per team, or milestones | Monthly fee per service level |
| **Knowledge stays with** | Your team, if transfer is planned | Shared, via documentation and handover | Vendor, unless you insist |
| **Best for** | Filling skill gaps in a working team | Building a product or major feature | Running stable, well-defined operations |

### H2: When staff augmentation is right

- You have **strong technical leadership** and a working delivery process.
- You're missing **specific skills** (AI, cloud, security, mobile) or **capacity** for a peak.
- You want to **keep full control** of architecture and priorities.
- You want **knowledge to stay in-house** after the engineer leaves.

**Watch out for:** augmentation without internal leadership. If nobody on your side can direct and review the work, augmented engineers drift, and you've bought hours rather than outcomes.

### H2: When a project team is right

- You need a **product, platform or major feature delivered**, and don't have the team to deliver it.
- You want **one accountable partner** rather than managing individuals.
- Your scope is **defined** (fixed-scope project) or a **roadmap that will run for months** (dedicated team).

**Watch out for:** handing over without staying involved. You still need a product owner to set priorities and accept work.

→ [Dedicated squads](/engagement-models/dedicated-squads) · [Outcome-based projects](/engagement-models/outcome-based-projects)

### H2: When managed services are right

- The function is **stable and well-defined**, such as infrastructure operations, monitoring or application support.
- You want **predictable cost and an SLA** rather than engineers.
- It's **not a differentiator** you need to control closely.

**Watch out for:** lock-in. Make sure documentation, access and runbooks stay with you, so you can bring it back in-house or switch vendors.

### H2: A quick decision guide

1. **Do you have someone who can direct engineers day to day?**
   No → project team or managed services. Yes → continue.
2. **Is the work building something new, or running something that exists?**
   Running → managed services. Building → continue.
3. **Do you want to control *how* it's built, or just *what* gets built?**
   How → staff augmentation. What → project team.

---

### H2: Cost: which is cheapest?

Per month, **staff augmentation** usually has the lowest commitment, because you pay only for the people. But total cost includes your own management time and the risk you carry. A **project team** costs more per month and carries more of the delivery risk for you. **Managed services** are priced for predictability.

The cheapest option is the one that matches your management capacity. The most expensive mistake is augmentation with nobody to lead it.

---

### H2: Switching between models

Models aren't permanent. Common paths we see:

- **Augmentation → dedicated team** as two or three engineers grow into a self-managing squad
- **Project → managed services** after launch, when the system needs running rather than building
- **Project team → your team** with planned handover, runbooks and paired on-call

### H2: Frequently asked questions

**What's the difference between staff augmentation and managed services?**
With staff augmentation you manage the engineers and own the outcome. With managed services the provider runs a defined function for you under an SLA and owns the outcome.

**Is staff augmentation the same as outsourcing?**
It's one form of it. "Outsourcing" covers any work handed to an outside firm. Staff augmentation is the version where you keep control and the engineers work inside your team.

**Is a dedicated development team the same as staff augmentation?**
No. A dedicated team comes with its own leadership and owns delivery. With augmentation, the engineers report to you.

**Which is best for a startup?**
Startups without a technical leader usually do better with a project team or a [fractional CTO](/engagement-models/advisory-architecture) plus a small squad. Startups with a strong CTO often augment.
