### H2: How we compared them

We looked at seven things that decide whether a voice agent works in production:

1. **Latency:** how quickly it responds on a real phone call
2. **Grounding:** whether it answers from your knowledge, and admits when it doesn't know
3. **Actions and integrations:** booking, CRM, helpdesk, webhooks
4. **Guardrails and hand-off:** control over what it says, and clean transfer to people
5. **Observability:** transcripts, traces, cost per call, testing changes safely
6. **Pricing transparency:** whether the per-minute price is all-in
7. **Who it's built for:** developers, businesses or agencies

### H2: What actually matters when you choose

**1. Test on a real phone line, not a browser demo.** Latency on a mobile call is different from a web demo.

**2. Ask it something it shouldn't know.** A good agent says "I don't know, let me take a message." A bad one invents an answer.

**3. Get an all-in price.** Ask whether speech, LLM and telephony are included, how minutes are rounded and what transfers cost. [Our list of questions →](/products/pxo-ai/pricing)

**4. Look at a failed call.** Ask the vendor to show you how you'd find out *why* a call went wrong.

**5. Check outbound compliance features** if you plan to make calls. [Why it matters →](/products/pxo-ai/use-cases/outbound)

**What is the best AI voice agent?**
It depends on who you are. Developers often prefer API-first platforms like Vapi or Retell AI, non-technical teams prefer no-code builders like Synthflow, and voice-quality-led use cases suit ElevenLabs. If grounding and observability matter most, look at PXO AI. Test any of them on your own calls before deciding.

**What is the best AI receptionist for a small business?**
