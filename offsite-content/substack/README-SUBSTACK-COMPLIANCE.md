# Read this before publishing anything in this folder

Substack's Content Guidelines (updated 29 September 2026), under **Marketing and
Promotion**, say:

> "Substack is intended for high quality editorial content, not conventional
> email marketing. We don't permit publications whose primary purpose is to
> advertise external products or services, drive traffic to third party sites,
> distribute offers and promotions, **enhance search engine optimization**, or
> similar activities."

And: *"Brands and commercial organizations publishing on Substack may be subject
to additional verification."*

**This means a Substack created to pass links to pixoramagroup.com is a
guidelines violation on its face.** Substack links are also `nofollow`, so such
a publication would carry the compliance risk without the SEO benefit.

These ten issues are therefore written as a genuine publication with its own
editorial reason to exist. The rules applied:

1. **Each issue stands alone as something worth reading** even if every link
   were removed. Test: delete the links. Is it still a good newsletter? If no,
   rewrite the issue.
2. **One to two links to pixoramagroup.com per issue, maximum**, placed as a
   reference inside an argument — the way a columnist cites their own earlier
   work. Never a list of links, never a call to action to visit the site.
3. **No issue is a summary of a main-site post.** Each makes an argument the
   main site does not.
4. **No lead-generation mechanics.** No gated content, no "book a call", no
   offers, no promotional sequences.
5. **Publish under a real named author**, not the company name. The main site
   already credits a real author with a LinkedIn profile — use real bylines.
   A personal publication by a named practitioner is both more credible and
   further from the "commercial organization" verification trigger.
6. **Honest subscriber acquisition only.** No importing contact lists —
   the guidelines explicitly prohibit importing purchased, scraped or harvested
   addresses, and prohibit adding people without consent.

## What this track is actually for

Not links. Audience, referral traffic, brand search (GSC already shows 1,355
brand impressions), and a durable owned channel. Those are real returns and they
justify the work on their own terms.

If the goal is link equity, the Hashnode track is the one that provides it, and
this effort would be better spent there. That trade-off is stated plainly in
`../STRATEGY.md` §6.

## Series framing

**Title:** Notes from the build
**Byline:** a named engineer from the team, writing in the first person
**Cadence:** fortnightly, 10 issues
**Promise to the reader:** what engineering decisions actually cost, written by
people who were there when the invoice arrived.

The name deliberately matches the main blog's existing tagline, which is honest
about the connection rather than hiding it.
