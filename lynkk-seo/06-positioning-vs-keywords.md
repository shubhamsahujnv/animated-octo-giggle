# Conversation Memory Platform vs. Notetaker — the positioning/keyword split

**Date:** 2026-10-21
**Trigger:** team direction — Lynkk is a conversation memory platform, not a notetaker
**Data source:** Ubersuggest MCP connector, live US (locId 2840)
**Revises:** `05-website-copy.md` §1 and the hero; supersedes nothing in `01`–`04`

---

## 0. The short version

Your teammate is right about what Lynkk **is**, and the CPC data backs it commercially. But
"conversation memory" has **almost no search demand**, so it cannot also be what Lynkk is
**found as**.

Those are two different jobs. Collapsing them is the mistake — in either direction. Positioning
goes in the H1, the narrative and the differentiation. Keyword targeting goes in the title tag,
the body and the dedicated pages. Lynkk can be a conversation memory platform that happens to
rank for how people describe the problem today.

`conversation memory platform` returns **no record at all** — not zero volume, but no data,

`institutional knowledge` looks like an exception at 1,900/mo until you read its long tail:
`meaning`, `synonym`, `what is`, `another word for`, `in a sentence`. That's students and

One genuine signal worth watching: **`meeting intelligence` is trending up sharply** — the last
13 months run 10, 10, 10, 20, 20, 10, 50, 30, 30, 20, **70, 90**. A 9× rise off a tiny base, at
SD 25. Too small to build on today, but worth a tracked keyword and a re-pull in 90 days. If it
keeps climbing, it becomes the bridge term between your positioning and real demand.

## 2. Why your teammate's instinct is commercially sound anyway

Compare what each framing attracts per click:

| Framing | Representative term | Vol | CPC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notetaker | ai note taker | 27,100 | $13.60 |
| Notetaker | ai meeting note taker | 3,600 | $16.26 |
| **Memory** | conversation memory | 40 | **$56.18** |
| **Intelligence** | conversation intelligence tools | 90 | **$126.46** |
| **Intelligence** | sales call recording software | 110 | **$108.65** |
| **Intelligence** | sales onboarding software | 50 | **$100.97** |
| **Intelligence** | conversation intelligence platform | 880 | **$43.16** |

**The memory/intelligence framing attracts traffic worth 4–9× more per visitor.** Notetakers are
a commodity category — dozens of free Chrome extensions, $10–20/mo price points, $13.60 CPC.
Conversation intelligence is a procurement category — $43–126 CPC, because Gong, Clari and
Chorus are bidding against each other for buyers with budget.

So "we are not a notetaker" is not brand vanity. Calling Lynkk a notetaker anchors it to the
cheapest segment of its own market. That's a real cost, and it's the argument for the direction.

## 3. But dropping the notetaker terms would cost you nearly all discoverability

| Option | Addressable demand/mo |
|---|---|
| Keep category terms in SEO | ~31,700 (`ai note taker` 27,100 + `ai meeting note taker` 3,600 + `ai meeting notes` 1,000) |
| Memory terms only | ~120 |

At **DA 1 with 3 referring domains**, targeting a ~120/mo category means functionally zero
organic traffic for the foreseeable future. You would be optimising for a search market that
does not exist yet, on a domain with no authority to win it when it does.

**This is the same pattern that has now appeared three times in this engagement:** sales-qualified
notetaker terms were 0/mo (doc `01` §0), sales *outcome* terms were 8,100/mo (doc `03` §3), and
now memory *category* terms are ~120/mo. Each time the answer was the same shape — hold the
positioning, harvest the demand where it actually sits.

Category creators do exactly this. Gong's own category term `revenue intelligence` is 320/mo;
they get found on call-recording and sales-call terms and *convert* visitors with the revenue
intelligence story. The category name is bought with brand marketing, not earned with SEO.

## 4. The recommendation: separate the layers

| Layer | What it carries | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Positioning** | conversation memory platform | H1, hero, narrative, differentiation, deck, pricing page |
| **Discovery** | the job people search for today | Title tag, meta, section headings, body, dedicated pages |
| **Enterprise** | conversation intelligence, high-CPC terms | `/for-revenue-teams`, solution and pricing pages |

### One important nuance on wording

Don't put **"AI note taker"** in the title tag — that *is* the category claim you're rejecting,
and a visitor arriving expecting a commodity notetaker will bounce off a memory platform.

Use **"AI meeting notes"** instead (1,000/mo, SD 41, $15.18 CPC). It names a *capability Lynkk
genuinely has* rather than a *category Lynkk is*. Semantically close enough that Google treats
it as the same cluster, honest about what you are, and it reads as an outcome rather than a
commodity.

That single word change — notes rather than note taker — is how you hold both layers without
lying to either audience.

### The one real risk of the split

A visitor who searches `ai meeting notes`, lands on a memory platform and finds the free tier
gated behind a team deployment will bounce — and the Chrome Web Store listing *does* promise a
simple free notetaker. Keep the two consistent on **what a single rep can do for free on day
one**, or the extension's installs won't convert into platform users and the store's uninstall
rate (a ranking factor) will suffer.

## 7. Open questions for your team

1. **Is the ICP the individual rep or the revenue team?** "Memory platform" and "$100 CPC
   procurement terms" imply a team sale; the Chrome extension and the follow-up-email demand
   (8,100/mo) imply a rep sale. Both are viable, but the pricing page and the free tier can only
   really serve one as primary.
2. **Does "not a notetaker" mean the Chrome extension is a wedge or a side product?** That
   determines whether `02`'s listing strategy still matters.
3. **Should I re-pull `meeting intelligence` in 90 days?** It's the only memory-adjacent term
   with real momentum, and if it keeps climbing it's your bridge term.
